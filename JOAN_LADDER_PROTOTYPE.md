# Joan of Arc — topic-ladder prototype (15 questions, 3 per tier)

The template artifact for the topic-ladder rebuild (see memory `feedback_topic_ladders.md`).
Slotting = conceptual difficulty + voice, NOT obscurity. One fact per question; ages/dates are
scenery, quotes and named things are payoffs. Answers marked ✓.

## T1
1. **The deed** — France was losing the Hundred Years' War — the English and their allies held half the country. Then, in 1429, a seventeen-year-old farm girl who could not read or write talked her way into the French royal court — and was given command of an army. What did Joan of Arc do with it?
   ✓ Led it to victory at Orléans, breaking the English siege of the city | Marched it straight to Paris and recaptured the capital from the English | Sailed it across the Channel to attack London itself | Held the walls of her home village against an English raid
   *Ctx: France was losing the Hundred Years' War. Nine days after Joan reached Orléans the siege broke — she then walked Charles VII to Reims to be crowned.*
2. **The sewing flex** — When Joan of Arc was captured and put on trial, the hostile judges asked what skills she had learned as a girl back home in Domrémy. Her answer was pure confidence. What did she brag about?
   ✓ Sewing and spinning — she said she "feared no woman in Rouen" at it | Riding — she said no knight in France sat a horse better | Cooking — she said her soup had fed half her village | Reading Latin — she said she had taught herself from a psalter
   *Ctx: In the trial record. They hunted witchcraft in her childhood; they got a teenager proud of her needlework.*
3. **The Fairies' Tree** — At Joan of Arc's trial, the judges kept grilling her about something back in her tiny home village of Domrémy — a spot where the village girls made flower garlands, sang, and danced every spring. What were they so suspicious of?
   ✓ A great old tree the locals called the Fairies' Tree | A standing stone the locals called the Giant's Table | A cave in the hills the locals called the Dragon's Mouth | An old well the locals called the Wishing Well
   *Ctx: Sick people drank from a spring nearby. The judges hoped her voices were fairy-magic; she admitted the customs, denied the source.*

## T2
4. **The fate** — Joan was nineteen when she was captured by her enemies. After a long church trial in the city of Rouen, what was done to her?
   ✓ She was burned at the stake in the marketplace | Locked in a tower for the rest of her life | Ransomed back to the French for a fortune | Exiled to a convent in far-off Rome
   *Ctx: May 30, 1431. Verdict annulled 25 years later; sainted 1920.*
5. **The sword** — Joan of Arc — who claimed heavenly voices guided her — carried a famous sword she never bought, and no one gave her. She said the voices told her exactly where it lay hidden. Where was it?
   ✓ Buried behind the altar of a chapel — rusty, marked with five crosses | Hanging in the king's armory, waiting for whoever could draw it | At the bottom of a river, lost by a drowned English knight | Hidden under the floorboards of her father's barn
   *Ctx: Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois; the rust fell away at once; scabbards of crimson velvet and cloth-of-gold. She never claimed it was magic.*
6. **The mandrake** — At Joan of Arc's church trial, one accusation was folk magic: the judges claimed she carried a charm-root that country people believed brought money and luck. What root were they hunting for?
   ✓ A mandrake | A wolfsbane root | A hemlock root | A ginseng root
   *Ctx: Folklore: shaped like a little man, shrieks when pulled. She'd heard of such charms, never owned one, "put no faith" in it.*

## T3
7. **The voices** — Joan of Arc swore that her mission to drive the English out of France came from heavenly voices only she could hear — and at her trial she named exactly who spoke to her. Who did she say they were?
   ✓ St. Michael the Archangel, and the saints Catherine and Margaret | The ghost of her grandmother and two village priests | King Charles himself, speaking to her through dreams | The Virgin Mary alone, every night at midnight
   *Ctx: Began ~age 13 in her father's garden, often with church bells.*
8. **The comeback** — At Joan of Arc's trial, a judge trying to trip her up asked whether St. Margaret — one of the saints whose voices Joan claimed to hear — spoke to her in English. Her reply is still quoted six hundred years later. What did she say?
   ✓ "Why should she speak English, when she is not on the English side?" | "Saints speak every tongue at once, as the apostles did at Pentecost" | "She speaks no language at all — I hear her meaning, not her words" | "Ask her yourself, if you dare put a saint on trial"
   *Ctx: Judges were university theologians on the English payroll; the notaries recorded the zinger.*
9. **The banner** — Joan of Arc rode at the very front of every assault — yet she claimed at her trial that she had never killed a single person. How?
   ✓ She carried her banner into battle instead of swinging her sword — she said she loved it "forty times" better | She fought only with the flat of her blade, to wound and never kill | Her bodyguards cut down anyone who came within a lance of her | She took enemies prisoner herself so that no one would harm them
   *Ctx: The standard was the rally point — carrying it at the front, unable to fight back, was MORE dangerous than a sword.*

## T4
10. **The sale** — In 1430, while fighting to drive the English out of France, Joan of Arc was pulled from her horse outside the walls of Compiègne — not by English soldiers, but by Frenchmen. What happened to her next?
    ✓ The Burgundians — Frenchmen allied with England — sold her to the English | King Charles VII stormed the city to rescue her, and failed | Her own soldiers traded her for prisoners the English held | An army court tried her on the spot and imprisoned her in Paris
    *Ctx: Burgundian vs Armagnac civil war inside the war; ~10,000 livres; Charles VII never offered a ransom.*
11. **"Present in the perils"** — At the coronation of Charles VII — the king Joan of Arc's victories had put on the throne — one banner stood by the altar of Reims cathedral while every other captain's stayed outside: Joan's own. Asked at her trial why hers deserved that honor, she answered in one line. What was it?
    ✓ "It had been present in the perils; that was reason enough for it to be honored" | "It bore the king's own lilies, so it stood wherever the king stood" | "My voices commanded it, and the archbishop did not dare refuse" | "A banner that has never fallen may stand anywhere it pleases"
    *Ctx: The banner: white field, fleurs-de-lis, Christ holding the world, two angels, "Jhesus Maria" — theology on cloth.*
12. **The careful answer** — At her trial, the judges tried to make Joan of Arc say that God *hated* the English — which would make her mission sound like hate instead of heaven. Her actual answer was far more careful. What did she say?
    ✓ That she knew nothing of God's love or hatred for the English — only that they would be driven out of France | That God loved all men equally, and she fought only for her king | That God had cursed England for the burning of churches, and she was the proof | That the saints had shown her England's defeat written in the stars
    *Ctx: Cartoon-Joan rants; record-Joan refuses, under oath and threat of fire, to claim she knows the mind of God — except on one point.*

## T5
13. **The grace trap** — At Joan of Arc's church trial, the judges set a perfect theological trap: "Are you in God's grace?" If she said *yes*, she was presumptuous — doctrine held no one could know for certain. If she said *no*, she had confessed her voices were lies. The notaries recorded that her answer stunned the court. What was it?
    ✓ "If I am not, may God put me there; and if I am, may God so keep me" | "Only my confessor may ask me that, and you are not he" | "I am as much in God's grace as any judge in this room" | "God's grace is not a thing a maid can lose by answering questions"
    *Ctx: A notary testified the examiners were stupefied. An illiterate 19-year-old slipped a trap built by theologians.*
14. **The engineered relapse** — Joan of Arc was not burned for witchcraft. After she signed a confession, church law could execute her only if she *relapsed* — and her "relapse" was wearing men's clothes again in her cell. Witnesses at the retrial swore it was no accident. What did they say happened?
    ✓ Her guards took away her women's clothes and threw the men's clothes back at her — forcing the violation | A forged letter planted in her cell was counted as her second offense | She was tricked into repeating her abjuration backwards, voiding it | The court redated her first offense so it fell after her confession
    *Ctx: England needed the CHURCH to condemn her so Charles VII's crown would look heaven-less. The relapse rule was the lever.*
15. **The executioner's testimony** — Twenty-five years after Joan of Arc was burned at the stake, the Church reopened her case and heard sworn witnesses. The executioner's own account, retold there, is the kind of evidence historians handle carefully — vivid, second-hand, impossible to verify. What did he reportedly say?
    ✓ That he feared he was damned for burning a holy woman — and that her heart would not burn, no matter what he used | That she had cursed him from the pyre, and he never slept again | That he refused the job, and an English soldier lit the fire instead | That she whispered the king's secret to him before the end
    *Ctx: Oil, sulfur, charcoal — testimony, not forensic fact; ashes into the Seine. This rung teaches how legends form and how to read them.*
