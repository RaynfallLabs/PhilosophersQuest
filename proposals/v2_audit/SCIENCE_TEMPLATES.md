# Science Templates (v2)

Per-tier approved stem patterns + anti-patterns. Companion to `SCIENCE_FRAMEWORK.md` (the WHY); this is the WHAT.

Authoring rule: every new science question should match an approved template AND fit somewhere on the Discovery Pattern hierarchy (`SCIENCE_FRAMEWORK.md` §1). When a question doesn't fit a template, refactor it to one — or propose the template here.

## §1 Approved stem patterns by tier

### T1 patterns (grade 5 — symbol-led / single-fact wonder)

Length cap: ≤ 280 chars total. Concrete + kid-visual + single hook.

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Symbol-by-vivid-example | "Gold doesn't tarnish — old gold coins from sunken ships come up shiny. What's gold's chemical symbol?" → "Au" | Symbol-recall with a wonder hook |
| Mechanism-by-image | "Hold a feather and a small rock together and drop them at the same height. Which usually hits the floor first?" → "The rock" | Mechanism + kid-doable |
| Named-discovery primer | "Long ago, Edward Jenner noticed that milkmaids who had touched cows did NOT get the deadly disease called smallpox. What did he invent?" → "The first vaccine" | Named figure introduces a wonder |
| Body-or-nature-fact | "Frogs and salamanders start their lives in water with little tails, breathing like fish. What's that in-between baby form called?" → "A tadpole" | Real biology + concrete name |
| Counter-intuitive primer | "If you mix red and blue paint you get purple. If you mix red and blue LIGHT, what color do you get?" → "Magenta (pinkish)" | Mechanism-recognition kid-grade |

### T2 patterns (grade 6 — one-line scene + question)

Length cap: ≤ 480.

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery moment | "Walking past a Padua cathedral in 1583, the 19-year-old Galileo timed his own pulse against the swing of a hanging lamp. What did he discover?" → "A pendulum's period depends on its length, not its weight" | Named figure + specific moment |
| Vivid mechanism | "Mendel grew 28,000 pea plants in his monastery garden and quietly counted them for 8 years. What general rule did he find about how traits pass to offspring?" → "Traits come in pairs; one can mask the other (dominant/recessive)" | Mechanism via the discovery scene |
| Real-application | "Smallpox killed roughly 300 million people in the 20th century alone. In 1980 the WHO announced something no other infectious disease has ever achieved. What?" → "Smallpox had been eradicated" | Real triumph + memorable scale |
| Reversal primer | "In 1847 a Hungarian doctor named Ignaz Semmelweis showed that doctors washing hands with chlorinated lime cut maternity-ward deaths from ~18% to ~1%. How did the Vienna medical establishment respond?" → "They mocked him; he was institutionalized and died in an asylum" | Reversal recognition T2-level |
| Named-figure backstory | "Marie Curie won Nobels in TWO different sciences — physics and chemistry. What did she discover that made both possible?" → "The radioactive elements polonium and radium" | Wonder + the answer is the cool fact |

### T3 patterns (grade 7 — scene + science/history with consequence)

Length cap: ≤ 680.

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery + mechanism | "In 1953 James Watson, Francis Crick, and Rosalind Franklin's X-ray image (Photo 51) revealed DNA's structure: two strands wound into a double helix, with A always pairing with T and G with C. Why does that complementary pairing matter for what DNA does?" → "It lets each strand serve as a template, so DNA can be copied exactly when cells divide" | Discovery + mechanism in one |
| Reversal full-arc | "In 1984 Australian gastroenterologist Barry Marshall, frustrated that no one believed his theory, drank a beaker of *H. pylori* bacteria. He developed gastritis within days. The previous medical consensus had been that ulcers came from stress and spicy food. What's the lesson about consensus?" → "Consensus can be wrong; an outsider's experiment can overturn decades of teaching" | Drama in stem, recognition in answer |
| Mystery framing | "Galaxy rotation curves show outer stars orbiting at speeds that should fling them off — unless something invisible is holding them. We can detect this 'something' only through its gravity. What's it called, and what do we know about it?" → "Dark matter — about 27% of the universe's energy content; we have no direct detection of what it actually IS" | Open mystery honored |
| Application + consequence | "Norman Borlaug spent 30 years developing dwarf wheat varieties resistant to rust. Mexico, India, Pakistan adopted them; harvests doubled or tripled. Population biologists estimate his work saved how many lives?" → "Around one billion" | Named figure + magnitude |
| Establishment-failure | "Alfred Wegener proposed in 1912 that the continents had once been joined and drifted apart. The geological community rejected his idea for how long, and what eventually vindicated it?" → "About 50 years — until seafloor spreading and magnetic stripe data in the 1960s confirmed plate tectonics" | Reversal + named figure + duration |

### T4 patterns (grade 8 — multi-sentence setup + contested-topic substance)

Length cap: ≤ 900.

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Contested-topic substance | "Steven Koonin, former Undersecretary for Science at the Obama-era DOE, published *Unsettled* in 2021. He argued that the IPCC reports themselves — the actual technical chapters, not the Summary for Policymakers — do not support the catastrophist framing of media coverage. What's Koonin's central claim?" → "Climate is warming, humans contribute, but the catastrophe framing is built on top of the actual reports, not from them" | T4 power + substance |
| Mechanism + history | "Linus Pauling won two unshared Nobels (Chemistry 1954, Peace 1962). Late in life he advocated megadose vitamin C as cancer therapy. The trials didn't replicate his claims. What does this case illustrate about scientific authority?" → "Even brilliant scientists are not authorities on every topic — and prior achievement doesn't immunize new claims from needing replication" | Named figure + epistemic lesson |
| Establishment-failure deep | "In October 2020, three epidemiologists — Bhattacharya (Stanford), Kulldorff (Harvard), and Gupta (Oxford) — published the Great Barrington Declaration calling for focused protection rather than population lockdowns. FOIA emails later showed NIH director Francis Collins privately called for 'a quick and devastating takedown.' What does this case illustrate?" → "Institutional pressure can suppress scientific dissent even from credentialed mainstream researchers" | Substance + named documents |
| Failed prediction | "In 2007 Al Gore predicted Arctic ice would be 'completely gone' in 5–7 years. In 2008 the BBC reported NSIDC scientists predicting an ice-free summer Arctic by 2013. Actual September Arctic ice extent in 2013 was about 5 million km². What's the recognition?" → "Specific named predictions on contested topics can be checked against the record — and many high-confidence climate-catastrophe predictions have not been confirmed" | Specific dated falsified prediction |
| Open question + bounds | "We can observe that life on Earth started at least 3.5 billion years ago — there are stromatolite fossils. We do NOT know how. Four serious hypotheses compete: RNA-world, metabolism-first, hydrothermal-vent, panspermia. What does the bank's honest answer look like?" → "Abiogenesis is unsolved — multiple plausible mechanisms, no decisive evidence for any one yet" | Honest open question |

### T5 patterns (grade 9-10 HARD CEILING — deep history + paradox + establishment-failure)

Length cap: ≤ 1100. Stays grade-10-appropriate. No upper-undergraduate jargon.

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Contested debate framing | "Ioannidis published 'Why Most Published Research Findings Are False' in *PLOS Medicine* in 2005, drawing on Bayesian reasoning about prior probabilities and statistical power. The Open Science Collaboration's 2015 reproducibility project found only ~36% of psychology studies replicated. What's the recognition skill for the kid reading a 'new study shows X' headline?" → "A single published finding — even peer-reviewed, even in a prestigious journal — is weak evidence on its own; pre-registration, replication, and effect-size matter more than the headline" | Replication-crisis substance |
| Eugenics history | "In 1927 the US Supreme Court ruled 8-1 in *Buck v. Bell* that compulsory sterilization of the 'feeble-minded' was constitutional. Justice Holmes wrote 'three generations of imbeciles are enough.' Carrie Buck's IQ testing was later shown to have been rigged. The decision has never been formally overturned. What's the warning here for the relationship between science and state power?" → "Scientific framing can lend constitutional cover to coercion against the powerless — and discredited science can remain legally binding long after it's discredited" | Eugenics history at T5 depth |
| WEF / transhumanism | "Yuval Noah Harari, historian and frequent WEF speaker, has stated that humans are now 'hackable animals' and that 'free will is over.' Klaus Schwab's *COVID-19: The Great Reset* (2020) and *Fourth Industrial Revolution* (2016) frame technological transformation as inevitable and globally coordinated. What's the recognition skill here?" → "When a tech-political agenda is framed as inevitable progress, it is being naturalized as 'just how things are' rather than examined as a political program that can be opposed" | Substance on the WEF agenda |
| Reversal + censorship | "Twitter Files releases (2022-2023) and the Murthy v. Missouri case (Supreme Court 2024) documented federal agency coordination with social media platforms to flag scientific speech for suppression — including content from credentialed scientists like Bhattacharya and Kulldorff. What does this case illustrate?" → "Government coordination with private platforms to suppress speech functions as censorship even without formal law — and scientific dissent is the specific kind of speech the First Amendment exists to protect" | T5 censorship-of-dissent |
| Mystery + bounds | "Consciousness — the felt experience of being someone — has no agreed mechanism in physics, biology, or neuroscience. David Chalmers calls this the 'hard problem': we can explain *correlates* (neurons firing when you see red) but not the *experience* itself. What's the honest scientific answer about consciousness as of the 2020s?" → "We don't know; there are competing serious frameworks (integrated information theory, global workspace, panpsychism, illusionism) but no decisive evidence for any of them" | Hard problem honored |

## §2 Choice-shape conventions

### Length parity

Science is in `ANSWER_OUTLIER_SUBJECTS`. The 1.6× answer-outlier flex applies — a longer mechanism-explanation answer can legitimately exceed distractor lengths by up to 1.6×.

Distractors should still be in approximate parity with each other (1.30 max/min ratio among distractors).

### Dash-shape parity

All four choices share dash usage (all use em-dash, or none). Same rule as wonder subjects.

### Distractor plausibility

Distractors must be:
- For **established facts** (chemistry, physics laws): adjacent-but-wrong real facts (a nearby element, a sibling law, a related-but-wrong mechanism). Never joke options.
- For **contested topics**: the establishment framing is often ONE of the distractors. The correct answer is the more measured / substantive position. Example: for a Koonin question, "humans are the primary driver, catastrophe imminent" can be a distractor — not because it's absurd, but because it's the framing Koonin specifically critiques.
- For **named figures**: other figures in adjacent positions (Pasteur / Koch / Lister for germ theory; Newton / Hooke / Halley for celestial mechanics).
- For **reversal questions**: plausible-wrong responses the establishment actually made (mockery, ignored, sought to suppress, eventually accepted with credit).

Banned distractor types:
- Made-up scientists, journals, dates, papers
- "Joke distractors" that don't test anything ("the molecule got tired"; "researchers were too lazy")
- Cartoon-villain framings ("scientists hate truth")
- Boilerplate-padding ("under standard laboratory conditions" — banned by May 2026 audit)

## §3 Anti-patterns (do not generate)

- **"X is best known for Y" recall walls** — the entire pattern (35 questions) was dropped in the May 2026 audit. Use scene-led discovery-moment questions instead.
- **Establishment-default framing on contested topics** — IPCC SPM as default-neutral, CDC mandates as default-correct, "97% consensus" as default-fact
- **Boilerplate filler** — "under standard laboratory conditions" (455 occurrences stripped), "in well-controlled experimental studies" (350 stripped), "in research published over recent decades" (86 stripped). Do not regenerate.
- **Slurs** — "denier", "anti-vaxxer", "conspiracy theorist", "fringe" without naming who used the label
- **Anthropomorphizing nature** — "evolution wants", "atoms prefer", "the universe decides" without scare quotes
- **Above-grade-10 jargon** — partial differential equations, Hilbert spaces, gauge theory, Lagrangians, Hamiltonian formalism, KL divergence at T5
- **Fabricated facts** — invented model names, dates, papers, or events
- **"Trust the science" framing** — the phrase is a propaganda slogan, not a scientific category
- **False balance** — Koonin presented as the mature view, not "one side of a debate"; lab leak as respectable, not equal-and-opposite to Proximal Origin
