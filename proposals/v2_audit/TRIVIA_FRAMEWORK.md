# Trivia Framework (v2)

The controlling voice + structure + stance for the trivia bank. Companion to `TRIVIA_TEMPLATES.md` (per-tier stem patterns) + `docs/quiz/subjects/trivia.md` (the canonical stance, geek-dad-canon framing) + `docs/quiz/trivia_strategies.md` (the comprehensive content spec with hundreds of strategy IDs).

This bank is **the geek-dad canon** — the deep-cut stuff the user loves and wants to share with his kids. Movies, anime, arcade games, comics, pulp fiction, cryptids, classic D&D, WWE Attitude Era, Seahawks Legion of Boom, Mariners 2001. *Ready Player One* vibe: questions that make a kid want to GO seek out the source.

## §1 The Easter Egg Pattern — voice rule

**The most memorable trivia question reveals a cool detail, scene, lore-nugget, or production fact that makes the kid want to GO experience the source — without ruining the story.**

### Hierarchy of trivia-memorability

1. **NAMED THINGS** (highest) — characters, weapons, moves, callbacks, working titles, voice-actor names, real-world inspirations, signature phrases, kill-screen levels, high-score numbers
2. **VIVID PRODUCTION DETAILS** — director choices, casting near-misses, score composers, practical-effect breakthroughs, behind-the-scenes drama (Twin Galaxies controversies, Don Bluth defecting from Disney, *Eva* episode 25/26 budget collapse, Rutger Hauer's improvised lines)
3. **PRE-CLIMAX SCENE REFERENCES** — opening shots, training montages, first encounters, world-building details, iconic visual moments (the Akira motorcycle slide; the *Princess Bride* opening shot of Fred Savage playing Hardball II on an Amiga; the Star Wars opening crawl)
4. **EASTER EGGS / CROSSOVERS / CULTURAL CALLBACKS** — guest appearances, hidden references, inside jokes, Konami code, Galaga's Avengers cameo
5. **GENERIC RECALL** (BANNED as primary answers) — bare years, generic publisher names, "what year did X come out" without dramatic specifics

### The Cultural-Osmosis Carve-out (CRITICAL)

The bank traffics confidently in cultural touchstones. Plot-derived facts that have **crossed into broader cultural knowledge independent of the source** are testable.

**Testable as common knowledge** (not spoilers):
- Secret identities: Bruce Wayne = Batman, Clark Kent = Superman, Peter Parker = Spider-Man, Diana = Wonder Woman, Tony Stark = Iron Man, Wade Wilson = Deadpool
- Origin stories: radioactive spider bit Peter Parker, Wayne parents murdered in the alley, Krypton exploded, gamma bomb created the Hulk, super-soldier serum on Cap, Bruce Banner gets mad and turns green
- Foundational setup: Frodo carries the Ring, Yoda is a Jedi Master, Aragorn is heir of Isildur, Cloud was in SOLDIER, Master Chief works with Cortana
- Decades-old culturally-memed lines: "Luke, I am your father" (1980 — 45 years old), "I'll be back", "May the Force be with you", "These aren't the droids you're looking for", "Inconceivable!", "I'm your huckleberry", "Hasta la vista, baby", "I see dead people" (borderline — see below)
- Powers + weapons + costumes: Wolverine's claws are adamantium, Thor's hammer is Mjolnir, Superman's weakness is kryptonite, the Hulk wears purple pants

**The litmus test**: *"Could a kid who has never seen/read/played this still know this fact from broader culture?"*
- Vader is Luke's father → YES (memed since 1980, on T-shirts, in commercials, in *Toy Story 2*)
- Bruce Wayne is Batman → YES (on every product since 1939)
- Sephiroth kills Aerith → NO (gated behind playing FF7)
- "Snape kills Dumbledore" → Y, but the kids have read the books, so OK (see spoiler-allowed list below)

### Spoiler-allowed franchises (the kids have seen them)

For these **ten franchises**, internal plot events — including character deaths, twist reveals, and final-act climaxes — are FAIR GAME:

1. **My Hero Academia** (anime + manga)
2. **Hajime no Ippo** (anime + manga)
3. **Harry Potter** (books + films)
4. **Star Wars original trilogy** (Episodes IV/V/VI, 1977-1983)
5. **MCU through Endgame** (2008-2019, 23 films)
6. **Toilet-Bound Hanako Kun** (anime + manga)
7. **Dragon Ball** (original DB + DBZ — GT only as continuity reference; Super NOT in scope)
8. **Scott Pilgrim** (graphic novels + 2010 film)
9. **Princess Bride** (1987 film)
10. **Super Mario Bros Movie 2023** (Illumination — NOT the 2026 sequel)

For these: testing All Might passing One For All to Deku, Sirius's death, Vader's last act, Tony's snap, Goku turning Super Saiyan against Frieza, Ramona's seventh evil ex, Westley's "As you wish" reveal — all fine. The kids already know.

### When in doubt — include content, avoid spoilers

The user's governing principle: **"If in doubt, include content but avoid spoiler."** Lean toward INCLUDING the topic; if uncertain whether a specific reveal would spoil, pick a non-spoilery angle on it. The bank should be RICH and FUN, not paranoid.

### Three-question test

Every question must pass:
1. **The Spoiler Test** — for non-spoiler-allowed franchises, would a kid who hasn't experienced the source lose anticipation? If yes, banned.
2. **The Seek-It-Out Test** — would the kid want to find the source after hearing this? If no, weak.
3. **The Dinner Test** — would the kid retell this fact at dinner?

## §2 What this bank IS NOT (anti-patterns)

- **NOT modern multiverse capeshit** — Disney+ shows, post-2019 MCU, post-Endgame anything → OUT
- **NOT Disney-era Star Wars** — sequels (VII/VIII/IX), Mandalorian, Andor, Boba Fett, Ahsoka show → OUT
- **NOT post-Attitude-Era wrestling** — boundary is Hogan-Rock WrestleMania 18, March 17, 2002 → anything after that → OUT
- **NOT post-Legends MtG** — Magic the Gathering coverage stops at the Legends expansion (June 1994)
- **NOT modern D&D errata or sensitivity reads** — classic Gygax-era / AD&D / TSR is what's celebrated; modern 5e errata, alignment removal, race/ancestry rewrites → OUT
- **NOT generic recall** — "What year did Pac-Man come out?" alone is OK at T2 only; "In what year was Akira directed?" alone is weak — pair with a dramatic specific (Otomo's directorial debut, the motorcycle slide)
- **NOT spoiler-revealing for non-allowed franchises** — Sephiroth/Aerith, Sixth Sense ending, Keyser Söze identity, Snape/Dumbledore (Harry Potter is ALLOWED, but the equivalent in other series is banned), Andy Dufresne's escape method, the Red Wedding specific death-list, mystery-film solutions
- **NOT credulous cryptid framing** — Bigfoot/Mothman/Nessie covered AS STORIES, not as "this is definitely real." Include the 1994 Surgeon's Photograph hoax confession; cover Patterson-Gimlin film as the historical artifact; treat cryptid lore with respect but without endorsing supernatural claims.

## §3 Tier ladder — fandom depth

Per the user's refined framing 2026-05-27: **tier = depth of fandom, not subject difficulty**. School-grade level doesn't fit trivia. Use this scale instead:

| Tier | Fandom level | Char cap | Voice |
|---|---|---:|---|
| T1 | **Casual cultural knowledge** | ≤ 280 | What you'd know just from existing in the culture — Pikachu, Mario, Sonic, Mickey Mouse, "Bruce Wayne is Batman", "Luke I am your father" |
| T2 | **Direct exposure** | ≤ 480 | What you'd know from actually seeing/playing/reading the thing — Akira's director (Otomo) + 1988, Pac-Man came out 1980 by Iwatani, Inigo Montoya's full vengeance line |
| T3 | **Fan** | ≤ 680 | What a fan who follows the franchise would know — Donkey Kong kill screen is Level 22, Howard wrote Conan from a Texas farmhouse 1932, Patty in the Patterson-Gimlin film Oct 20 1967, Dempsey Roll origin in real-1920s-boxer Jack Dempsey |
| T4 | **Huge fan** | ≤ 900 | The deep cuts a serious fan knows — Billy Mitchell vs Steve Wiebe King of Kong, Eva episode 25/26 production collapse, the 1983 crash + E.T. landfill in Alamogordo, the smiley-face button in every Watchmen issue, Mitchell's 2018 MAME disqualification |
| T5 | **Mega fan** | ≤ 1100 | *Ready Player One* deep-cut Easter eggs only the truly obsessed know — Minus World glitch in SMB 1-2 (the 1-2 ceiling-pipe frame-perfect jump), the Princess Bride opening shot game = Hardball II on a Commodore Amiga 1000, the Geinoh Yamashirogumi chorus on *Akira*, the ABACABB Genesis MK blood code, the Galaga Avengers cameo, Edgar's Double Oct 8 1995 (Griffey scores from first) |

T5 stays accessible — no technical-jargon dump, but the kind of stuff where a kid finishes and goes "wait, REALLY?" and immediately wants to go find the source. Hopefully the kids learn tons of information about amazing FUN topics here.

## §5 Stance — what makes this bank different

- **Geek-dad canon** — the user's personal deep-cut canon for his kids; *Ready Player One* vibe; make them want to GO seek out the source
- **No spoilers OUTSIDE the 10 allowed franchises** — for everything else, cultural-osmosis carve-out applies; specific plot reveals banned
- **Cultural-osmosis is generous** — Vader is Luke's father is FINE; Inconceivable! is fine; the Spider-bite origin is fine; Bruce Wayne is Batman is fine
- **Production lore and behind-the-scenes is GOLD** — directors, casting near-misses, scores, practical-effects breakthroughs, the King of Kong drama, the 1983 crash, *Eva*'s production collapse
- **Classic celebrated, modern slop OUT** — pre-2003 Marvel + DC, Gygax-era D&D, MtG pre-Legends, anime classics through MHA/Demon Slayer era, Attitude-era wrestling, classic arcade + console
- **Cryptids as STORIES** — historical artifact + the lore, never "this is definitely real"
- **Make them seek it out** — the success metric

## §8 Story-in-stem (§14 applied from day 1)

Per `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §14`: substance lives in the stem. For trivia specifically:
- The NAMED character + NAMED production detail + DRAMATIC specific all live in the stem
- Context is supporting detail (additional production notes, related lore, real-world connections)
- The answer carries the most memorable specific cool fact

**Canonical good shape (T3 example):**

> ✅ "On July 9, 1982, a 21-year-old construction worker named Steve Sanders played *Donkey Kong* at the Twin Galaxies arcade in Ottumwa, Iowa, achieving the first publicly verified million. A pizza-parlor owner from Florida named Billy Mitchell took the record back six months later. What was Mitchell's famous July 1982 score?"
> → "1,047,200"

The substance is in the stem (Sanders, July 9 1982, Twin Galaxies, Ottumwa Iowa, Mitchell, Florida). The answer is the iconic number. No weasel closer. No spoiler. STORY → WONDER → cool fact.

## §9 No weasel closers (§15 applied from day 1)

Per `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §15`: pointed concrete closers. Banned closers (same universal list).

For trivia specifically, additional allowed closers:
- "What was the score?" / "What was the high score?"
- "What was the character's name?" / "What was the actor's name?"
- "Who directed it?" / "Who composed the score?"
- "What was the working title?"
- "What was the kill-screen level?"
- "What year did it come out?" (only when paired with substantive scene/figure context)
- "What weapon / costume / object?"

## §10 Teach-before-test (§16 applied from day 1)

Per `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §16`: foundational terms need anchors. For trivia specifically:
- **Anime characters** — name + show before deep-cut episode references
- **Pulp authors** — Howard/Lovecraft/Burroughs/Doc Savage all introduced with their hometown + magazine before being assumed
- **D&D figures** — Gygax, Arneson, Hickman, Salvatore, Greenwood all introduced with origin context
- **Arcade-era designers** — Iwatani (Pac-Man), Miyamoto (Mario/Zelda/DK), Bushnell (Pong/Atari), Bailey (Centipede), Jarvis (Defender/Robotron), Jeff Minter (Tempest)
- **Wrestlers + athletes** — full name + signature move + era introduced before deep WrestleMania references
- **Cryptid sightings** — date + location + photographer/witness introduced before lore deep-dives

When a T3+ question references a figure, that figure should already have a T1 or T2 foundational anchor in the bank.
