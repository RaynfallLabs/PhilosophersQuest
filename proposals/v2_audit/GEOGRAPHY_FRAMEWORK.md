# Geography Bank Framework (2026-05-23)

> This doc is subordinate to `docs/quiz/moral_vision.md` (the bank's soul, supreme authority) and `proposals/v2_audit/SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md` (cross-subject universal rules). Both override anything here. Read them FIRST.

This is the rule set for what a good Philosopher's Quest *geography* question looks like. The bank teaches **wonder + scientific accuracy + human adaptation + how the land shaped the people who lived there** — anchored in Western tradition with honest substantive coverage of communist regime geography, American achievement geography, and the geographic determinism debate (Diamond's argument + Sowell's critique).

## Core principle

A 5th-10th grader should leave this bank with:
- **A desire to see the world** — concrete knowledge of bizarre wondrous places that make a kid say "I want to go there." Door to Hell, Salar de Uyuni, Socotra, Cappadocia, Mt Roraima, the Galápagos, Antarctic dry valleys.
- **Working understanding of physical geography** — plate tectonics, ocean currents, atmospheric circulation, biomes, climate zones, river systems
- **Appreciation for human adaptation** — how Vikings survived in Greenland, why Inuit built igloos and Icelanders built sod houses, how Persian qanats brought water through desert, how Polynesians navigated by stars, how Andean farmers terraced mountainsides and developed chuño
- **Insight into geography → religion + culture** — how the Nile's predictable floods shaped Egyptian religion; how Mt Olympus visible from city-states produced the Olympian pantheon; how British island isolation produced parliamentary tradition while Russian plains produced centralized autocracy
- **Christian geographic heritage substantial** — Holy Land geography, Crusader-era Mediterranean, pilgrimage routes (Santiago, Canterbury, Rome), monastic geography (Mt Athos, Meteora, the desert fathers)
- **Western voyaging tradition celebrated** — Eratosthenes ~240 BC, Magellan's circumnavigation, Cook's Pacific voyages, Lewis & Clark, Amundsen, Shackleton as substantive human achievement
- **American achievement geography substantial** — Louisiana Purchase, Lewis & Clark route, Erie Canal, transcontinental railroad, Panama Canal, Hoover Dam, Apollo landing sites
- **Honest communist regime geography** — Kolyma's permafrost-and-Pacific-isolation that made Gulag escape impossible; Henan-Anhui-Sichuan rice corridor + Great Leap collectivization that produced 30-45M famine deaths; Aral Sea desiccation as Soviet central-planning failure (the Hayek knowledge problem in geography); Iron Curtain as fortified geographic line
- **Sowell vs Diamond** on geographic determinism — recognize Diamond's argument; understand Sowell's substantive critique
- **Non-Western civilizations on their own terms** — Mali, Songhai, Great Zimbabwe, Mughal India, Qing China, Ottoman Empire, Persia, Inca, Maya, Aztec — substantive civilizations with their own achievements, NOT as exotic foils

## What this framework forbids

1. **Capital-name recall** — "What is the capital of [country]?" is BANNED, full stop. Per `feedback_no_rote_wonder.md`: capitals/dates/definitions banned for geography. Capital names go in *context* if relevant; they're NEVER the test.
2. **Toddler-trivial superlatives** — "What's the tallest mountain?" (Everest), "the longest river?" (Nile/Amazon), "the biggest country?" (Russia), "the most populous?" (India/China). These are toddler trivia.
3. **Pure date recall** — "When did Columbus arrive in America?" / "When did Magellan complete his voyage?" Pure history-as-trivia.
4. **Definition lookup** — "What does 'latitude' mean?" / "What's a biome?" — vocabulary, not geography.
5. **Toddler basic identification** — "Which continent is Egypt on?" / "How many oceans?" — too basic.
6. **Anti-anti-white colonial moralism** — Western colonial history must be HONEST (real achievements + real costs), never theatricalized as "whiteness evil."
7. **Climate-change-as-policy verdicts** — climate science as scientific content (Hadley/Ferrel cells, Koppen, Milankovitch, paleoclimate, glacial cycles); climate-change-as-policy ONLY as T5 recognize-the-move (no policy verdicts).
8. **Religious endorsement or critique** — geographic religious content is DESCRIPTIVE cultural fact. Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Hindu, Buddhist, ancient pagan all presented as cultural geographic content, not adjudicated.

## What this framework REQUIRES

1. **Wonder hook on every question** — bizarre fact, named feature, adaptive logic chain, civilizational link, or geographic mystery. **The wonder must live in the STEM, not be deferred to context.** Stem leads with the amazing; the question asks something specific (use, mechanism, breakthrough) that adds depth. Anti-pattern: stem describes a setup with named wondrous features, then asks for a rote proportion/number/date as the test while the amazing facts sit unused in context. See SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §8 and [[feedback_no_rote_wonder]].
2. **Place-name-in-geographic-context** — when a place name carries historical weight, the question is about the geographic feature/function that made it relevant (Kolyma's permafrost + isolation; the Khyber's mountain pass; Constantinople's Bosphorus position)
3. **Forcing constraint** — stem includes the detail that picks out exactly one answer
4. **Steelman distractors** — wrong, but plausibly-wrong. Real places / real facts / real biology, not absurd.
5. **Scientifically accurate** — real plate tectonics, real climate science, real biomes, real demography
6. **Place-anchoring rule** — when a stem names a place as *scaffolding* (not as the answer being tested), the stem must include enough geographic anchor (mountain range, continent, country, region) for a first-time reader to ground it. "K2" alone is free-floating; "K2 (8,611m, Karakoram on the Pakistan-China border)" is grounded. Recognition-type questions where the place IS the answer are exempt — but the *description* must still let the player ground without the name.
7. **No theory-stacking** — when a question references named theories or theory-laden concepts, the stem must anchor at least one in a concrete scenario (named person making a specific argument, specific historical event, specific named place). Pure theory-vs-theory abstraction at T5 is banned. Pattern J examples must show concrete movement — a recognizable person doing a recognizable analytical move — not an abstract framing-vs-framing battle.

## Where philosopher / explorer / dates / capital names belong

**In the `context` field.** Always. Context says "Magellan (1480-1521; died in the Philippines, 18 of 270 crew completed the circumnavigation)" or "[Country]'s capital is [City]." Stems never rely on knowing capitals or dates.

## The five tiers

### T1 — 5th grade (10-11)
- **Scope**: vivid wonder facts a kid can picture. Why is the Sahara so dry? Why does Iceland have so many volcanoes? Why do Inuit live in igloos?
- **Voice**: "Did you know?" energy. Concrete features.
- **Content**: continents and oceans by FEATURE (not by recall), basic biomes, bizarre places (Door to Hell, Salar de Uyuni), simple adaptation stories (igloos, sod houses)
- **Form**: ≤200 char stem, ≤90 char choice, context uncapped (teaching depth allowed)

### T2 — 6th grade (11-12)
- **Scope**: specific physical features + adaptation traditions. Why monsoons happen, why deserts exist, why mountains form, why Vikings settled Greenland and then abandoned it
- **Voice**: introduces "plate," "climate," "biome," "current" with brief inline meaning
- **Content**: Ring of Fire, Mid-Atlantic Ridge, named ocean currents, mountain ranges, biome boundaries, Polynesian wayfinding
- **Form**: ≤240 char stem, ≤110 char choice, context uncapped

### T3 — 7th grade (12-13)
- **Scope**: geographic systems + how geography shapes culture
- **Voice**: scientific vocabulary ("tectonic," "subduction," "rainshadow," "trophic," "watershed") with inline def first use
- **Content**: trophic cascades in ecosystems, niche climates, the Nile's predictable flood enabling Egyptian agriculture, Persian qanats, Roman aqueducts, mountain-monastery siting
- **Form**: ≤280 char stem, ≤130 char choice, context uncapped

### T4 — 8th grade (13-14)
- **Scope**: deeper geographic mechanisms + civilizational geography. Holy Land geography, Crusader-era Mediterranean, Florence's trade geography producing Renaissance, the British isolation thesis, the Russian plains thesis
- **Voice**: scientist + historian register
- **Content**: Hadley cell mechanics, Milankovitch cycles, glacial cycle climate, Sowell on geographic-vs-cultural explanations
- **Form**: ≤320 char stem, ≤160 char choice, context uncapped

### T5 — 9-10th grade (14-16)
- **Scope**: substantive moral vision content + specialist geographic facts. Diamond's argument + Sowell's critique. Communist regime geography (Gulag, Great Leap, Aral Sea) as honest historical fact. American achievement geography (Louisiana Purchase, transcontinental, Panama, Apollo). Christian heritage geography deep.
- **Voice**: analyst-grade with specialist terms parenthetical
- **Content**: thermohaline circulation; Koppen zone shifts; Sowell *Conquests and Cultures* / *Migrations and Cultures*; American Manifest Destiny as substantive historical claim; communist regime geography honestly
- **Form**: ≤360 char stem, ≤180 char choice, context uncapped

## Substantive moral vision

### 1. Western achievement is real human achievement (§3)
- Greek: Eratosthenes calculating Earth's circumference (~240 BC, accurate to within 15%) by comparing noon shadows at Syene and Alexandria
- Roman: Pliny's *Natural History*, Strabo's *Geographica*, the Roman road network (~80,000 km)
- Iberian: Henry the Navigator, Vasco da Gama (Indian Ocean), Magellan (1519-22 circumnavigation — 18 of 270 survivors), Columbus
- British: Cook's three Pacific voyages, Royal Navy hydrographic charts, the Greenwich Meridian
- Russian: Cossack expansion to the Pacific (1639), Bering's Pacific exploration
- American: Lewis & Clark (1804-06, 8,000 miles), Antarctic exploration, polar firsts, GPS (1973-1995 American military-civilian achievement), Apollo
- Modern Western cartography: Mercator (1569), Robinson, Goode interrupted projections

### 2. American exceptionalism is a substantive historical claim (§6)
American geographic achievement is REAL: Louisiana Purchase (1803, doubled US territory), Lewis & Clark survey, Northwest Ordinance, Erie Canal (1825, opened interior trade), Transcontinental Railroad (1869, joined coasts), Hoover Dam, TVA, Mississippi flood control engineering (Army Corps), Panama Canal (1914), Apollo program achievements, GPS system. These are SUBSTANTIVE American engineering and exploratory achievements, not framed as "colonialism extended."

### 3. Communist regime geography honestly recorded (§1)
The Black Book of Communism (Courtois), Conquest, Solzhenitsyn, Chang & Halliday document the geographic record. Specific geographies the bank teaches honestly:
- **Kolyma's subarctic permafrost + Pacific isolation** → Gulag camps where escape was nearly impossible. The geographic features themselves were the prison.
- **Solovki, Vorkuta, Magadan** → Gulag archipelago locations (Solzhenitsyn's title); the climates and isolation made them work
- **Henan, Anhui, Sichuan provinces** rice corridors → Great Leap Forward famine (~1959-61, 30-45M dead) when collectivization disrupted agricultural geography
- **Cambodian alluvial plains** → Killing Fields (Choeung Ek and others), where Pol Pot's regime exploited rural rice geography
- **Iron Curtain** as fortified geographic line from Lübeck to Trieste — concrete + barbed wire + mine fields running along a geographic boundary
- **Berlin Wall** — concrete geographic division of a city
- **Aral Sea desiccation** — Soviet cotton irrigation diverted Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers; the sea dropped from world's 4th-largest lake to a fraction by 2000. **The Hayek knowledge problem in geography**: central planners couldn't know the dispersed hydrology

### 4. Sowell vs Diamond — both substantive, neither default
- **Recognition** (T4-T5): Diamond's *Guns, Germs, and Steel* (Pulitzer 1998) argues geography determined which civilizations developed agriculture, technology, and immunity earliest — east-west axis of Eurasia vs north-south axis of Americas/Africa
- **Critique** (heavier weight at T5): Sowell's *Conquests and Cultures* (1998), *Migrations and Cultures* (1996), *Race and Culture* (1994) argue cultural factors (work habits, family structure, attitudes toward education, willingness to migrate, capital accumulation) explain disparities better than geography alone. Diamond's geography is one input; culture is independently load-bearing.
- **Pedagogical pattern**: same as care ethics in philosophy. 1-2 questions per tier define Diamond's argument so kids recognize it; rest of T4-T5 questions CHALLENGE geographic determinism with Sowell's substantive cultural critique

### 5. Anti-anti-white framing banned (§9)
Colonial history HONEST: real achievements (cartography, infrastructure, medicine, law, agricultural improvement) AND real costs (slavery, dispossession, disease introduction) — both stated factually, not theatricalized. The Iberian conquest is real history with real costs and real outcomes. Not a "whiteness evil" frame.

### 6. Non-Western civilizational geography on its own terms
Substantive coverage of:
- **African kingdoms**: Mali (Timbuktu's Saharan-edge trade), Songhai (16th-c gold-trade empire), Great Zimbabwe (stone city ruins), Ethiopia (Aksum), Mansa Musa's Hajj
- **South Asian**: Mughal India (Red Fort, Taj Mahal as Mughal architectural geography), Indus Valley (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro), Khmer (Angkor)
- **East Asian**: Qing China (Forbidden City, Great Wall geography), Edo Japan (isolation policy + Mt Fuji symbolism), Korean peninsula
- **Middle Eastern**: Ottoman (Topkapi, Mediterranean reach), Safavid Persia (Isfahan), Ancient Mesopotamia
- **Pre-Columbian Americas**: Inca (terracing + road network of ~40,000 km), Aztec (chinampas floating gardens), Maya (Mayan calendar geography, Tikal, Chichen Itza), Mississippian mound-builders (Cahokia)
These are substantive civilizations with their own cartographic, architectural, agricultural achievements — taught on their own terms, not as exotic foils.

### 7. Religious geography — Christian centerline + other religions substantial
The game-setting (Christian-Crusader) naturally weights Christian geographic heritage:
- **Holy Land geography**: Jerusalem (Old City quarters: Christian, Muslim, Jewish, Armenian), Bethlehem, Nazareth, Galilee, the Sea of Galilee, the Jordan River
- **Roman provinces of the Bible** (Judea, Galilee, Decapolis), the route of the Exodus
- **Monastic geography**: Mt Athos (the Athonite peninsula), Meteora (Greek pillars), the Egyptian desert fathers (Anthony, Pachomius), Tibetan monasteries, the Carthusian Grande Chartreuse
- **Pilgrimage routes**: Santiago de Compostela (Camino), Canterbury, Rome, the Via Francigena
- **Crusader geography**: the Crusader states (Outremer, Antioch, Edessa, Jerusalem kingdom), the Templar castles (Krak des Chevaliers), the routes by which spices/citrus/sugar returned to Europe
- **Reformation geography**: Wittenberg (1517), Geneva (Calvin), Augsburg, Trent
- **American religious geography**: Pilgrim New England, Mormon Trail to Salt Lake City, the Bible Belt, Catholic Southwest, Jewish New York

Other religious geography as cultural fact:
- **Islamic**: Mecca + Hejaz desert geography, Medina, the Hajj routes, Cordoba (Al-Andalus), Istanbul (post-1453)
- **Hindu**: Varanasi on the Ganges, the four dhams (Char Dham), Kumbh Mela rotation
- **Buddhist**: Bodh Gaya (tree where Siddhartha attained enlightenment), Lumbini (birthplace), Sarnath (first sermon), Mt Kailash
- **Jewish**: Western Wall, Jerusalem, the diaspora geography (Sephardi/Ashkenazi/Mizrahi)
- **Ancient Greek**: Mt Olympus, Delphi (Pythian oracle), Athens (Athena), Eleusis (mysteries), Delos
- **Ancient Egyptian**: Karnak, Luxor, Valley of the Kings, Abu Simbel
- **Norse**: Iceland's volcanic landscape inspiring the sagas, Uppsala (the pagan center)

### 8. Wonder of the world — drives desire to travel
Every question should make a kid say "I want to go there." Concrete wonder anchors:
- **Door to Hell (Darvaza)** — natural gas crater burning since 1971 in Turkmenistan
- **Salar de Uyuni** — world's largest salt flat, perfect mirror after rain
- **Socotra island** — 1/3 of plant species exist nowhere else on Earth (dragon blood trees)
- **Cappadocia** — fairy chimneys, troglodyte underground cities
- **Wadi Rum** — Mars-like Jordanian desert (Lawrence of Arabia and modern Mars filming)
- **Pamukkale** — white travertine terraces over thermal springs
- **Giant's Causeway** — basalt columns
- **Mt Roraima tepui** — Doyle's *Lost World* inspiration, isolated 1.7-billion-year-old plateau
- **Madagascar** — 90% endemic species
- **Easter Island moai** + ecological collapse story
- **Galápagos** — Darwin's laboratory
- **Antarctic Dry Valleys** — driest place on Earth, Mars analog
- **Death Valley Badwater Basin** -86m below sea level
- **Dead Sea** — lowest land point on Earth (-430m), saltiest sea
- **Lake Baikal** — world's deepest (1,642m) and oldest (~25M years) lake, ~20% of world's freshwater
- **Mariana Trench** — 11km deep
- **Yellowstone caldera** supervolcano
- **Iceland on Mid-Atlantic Ridge** — North America and Eurasia spreading apart
- **Hawaiian hotspot chain** — Pacific plate moving over a fixed mantle plume
