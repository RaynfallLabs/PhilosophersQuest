# Theology Framework (v2)

The controlling voice + structure + stance for the theology bank. Companion to `THEOLOGY_TEMPLATES.md` (per-tier stem patterns).

This bank is a **kid's gateway to Western mythological + legendary heritage** — the stories that built our cultural imagination. **Not doctrine quizzes. Not author attribution. TELL THE STORY.**

The bank reverses the May 2026 audit that purged the Greek + Norse pantheons. Those stories are restored because they're THE STORIES.

---

## §1 The Wonder Pattern — voice rule

**The most memorable theology question reveals the most specific cool fact about a story kids should know.**

Same controlling voice rule as history / animal / cooking / geography. The bank's theology content is **story-led** — every stem leads with a scene, and the answer is the most memorable specific cool fact: a named object, a vivid action, a dramatic phrase.

### Hierarchy of theology-memorability

1. **NAMED THINGS** (highest) — specific quotes ("This is my body", "Friar Tuck's quarterstaff", "By my troth"), named objects (Excalibur, Mjölnir, the Holy Grail, the Lance of Longinus, Pandora's jar, Roland's horn Olifant), specific epithets (Thor "Hammer-Wielder", Odin "All-Father", Achilles "Swift-Footed", Lancelot "of the Lake")
2. **VIVID ACTIONS** — Hercules strangling the Nemean Lion bare-handed; Thor's hammer crushing Jörmungandr; Robin Hood splitting his own arrow at the Sheriff's tournament; Daniel sealed in the lion's den overnight; David spinning his sling at Goliath; Polycarp at 86 walking to the stake refusing to recant
3. **OBJECTS / MATERIALS** — Mjölnir's making by the dwarf brothers Sindri + Brokkr; the Round Table's empty Siege Perilous reserved for Galahad; Apollo's lyre crafted from Hermes's tortoise shell; the Shroud of Turin; Sherwood's longbow; the Grail at the Last Supper
4. **NUMBERS** (singular + unforgettable) — 12 labors of Hercules; 12 apostles; 9 worlds of Yggdrasil; 7 sleepers of Ephesus; 24 knights of the Round Table; 24 hours Daniel in the den; 40 days in the wilderness; 39 articles of the Anglican Church
5. **GENERIC LABELS** (BANNED as primary answers) — bare doctrinal categories ("Trinitarianism", "transubstantiation"), dates without drama, "the doctrine of X", "the principle of Y"

### Three-question test

1. **The Dinner Test** — does the ANSWER ALONE make a parent ask "wait, what?" If "the Trinity" stops conversation cold but "Polycarp at 86 saying 'eighty and six years I have served Him'" makes them ask "WHO?", you wrote the wrong question.
2. **Most-memorable-detail test** — across all the cool facts in this story, am I asking for THE most memorable? Or burying it?
3. **Story-not-attribution test** — am I asking about the STORY (what happened) or about authorship/citation (who wrote/said it)? If the latter, rewrite to story.

## §2 What this bank IS NOT (anti-patterns)

- **NOT a doctrine quiz.** No "What is the doctrine of X?" No "Define hypostatic union." No "Which heresy denied Y?" Doctrine becomes interesting only when tied to a dramatic moment (Athanasius vs. Arius at Nicaea 325 — story; "doctrine of homoousios" — boring).
- **NOT an author-attribution quiz.** No "Who wrote the Iliad?" No "Who composed the *Prose Edda*?" No "Who said 'Here I stand'?" — the kid should learn the STORY behind those moments. The Worms diet question becomes "Standing before the Imperial Diet at Worms in 1521, Luther refused to recant. What weapon did the emperor have, and what did Luther do next?"
- **NOT comparative religion.** No "What are the Five Pillars of Islam?" Not "the Eightfold Path." These are different stories the bank doesn't carry. The Christian + Greek + Norse + Arthurian-legend stories are what THIS bank tells.
- **NOT a metaphysics quiz.** The bank takes no position on whether Christianity is "true" or Greek myths are "false." All stories are told STRAIGHT — with full dramatic seriousness, with the named details intact, with no "this one is real, these are myth" framing. Per moral_vision.md: no smug atheist voice + no smug believer voice.
- **NOT modern-cult-abuse content.** Jonestown, Heaven's Gate, Aum Shinrikyo etc. belong in a history or current-events bank, not in mythological-heritage storytelling.

## §3 Tier ladder

Standard caps. Theology IS in `ANSWER_OUTLIER_SUBJECTS` — the 1.6× answer-outlier allowance applies (a longer story-answer can exceed distractor lengths by up to 1.6×, since the kid is reading the dramatic detail). Distractors must still be in 1.30 max/min parity with each other.

| Tier | Grade | Char cap | Voice |
|---|---|---:|---|
| T1 | 5 | ≤ 280 | Crisp scene + named character/object + the wonder |
| T2 | 6 | ≤ 480 | One-paragraph story + named figure + the action |
| T3 | 7 | ≤ 680 | Scene + stakes + the dramatic moment + answer is the cool detail |
| T4 | 8 | ≤ 900 | Multi-sentence narrative + named specifics + payoff |
| T5 | 9-10 | ≤ 1100 | Deep story + characters + the most memorable specific detail |

T5 stays grade 9-10. No theological-academic jargon (no "supralapsarianism", no "perichoresis", no "filioque" — these are research-paper terms a kid bank shouldn't need).

## §5 Stance (story-led, all-stories-told-straight)

Per user direction + `moral_vision.md`:

- **All stories told straight** — Christian, Greek, Norse, Arthurian, Robin Hood — with full dramatic seriousness, named details intact, no "this one is real, these are myth" framing.
- **No smug atheist voice + no smug believer voice** — the bank presents the stories with respect. Kids should come away knowing Daniel + the lion's den as vividly as they know Theseus + the Minotaur.
- **Cultural heritage as the frame** — these are OUR stories, OUR mythological inheritance. The bank's stance is "kids should know these because they shaped Western imagination", not "let's evaluate which religions are true."
- **Christian content** — Bible narratives + saint legends + Christian-era symbolism. Treated as the LARGEST single body of stories (50% combined with Arthurian/Western-medieval legend).
- **Greek mythology** — 25%, restored from the May 2026 audit purge. Olympians, heroes, Trojan War, Odyssey, Hercules's labors, Persephone, Orpheus, Prometheus, Pandora, all the classics.
- **Norse mythology** — 25%, restored. Aesir, Vanir, jötnar, Yggdrasil, Mjölnir, Ragnarok, Beowulf adjacent.
- **Arthurian + Robin Hood + Western-medieval legend** — substantial coverage per user's "BIG spot" direction. Excalibur, Grail, Roland, Beowulf, Faust, William Tell.
- **Symbolism + rites + literature** — folded into the story content. Christian Eucharist + baptism + Christmas/Easter; Greek Olympic libations + Delphi oracle + Eleusinian mysteries; Norse blot ceremonies + runes + sagas + Edda; Arthurian knightly oaths + the chivalric code.

## §6 What's NOT in this bank (deliberate omissions)

- **Other-world religions** (Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism, Sikhism, Confucianism, Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Aztec) — the bank covers Western mythological-legendary heritage specifically. Per user's 25/25/50 framing, Greek/Norse/Christian are the three traditions; non-Western traditions belong elsewhere.
- **Modern cult-abuse content** (Jonestown, Heaven's Gate, Aum Shinrikyo) — these are history / current-events material, not mythological storytelling.
- **Theological-academic doctrine** (supralapsarianism, perichoresis, filioque, theodicy as abstract problem) — research-paper theology, not story-content for kids.
- **Comparative-religion equivalence framing** — "How is Christianity like Hinduism?" type questions. The bank tells specific stories from a specific heritage.

## §8 Story-in-stem (§14 applied from day 1)

Per `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §14`: substance lives in the stem. For story questions specifically:
- The NAMED figure + NAMED action + NAMED object + NAMED setting all live in the stem
- The kid reads the SCENE before the closer
- Context is supporting detail (extra biographical facts, related-story callouts)
- The answer carries the most memorable specific cool fact

**Canonical good shape (T3 example):**

> ✅ "Walking the long road to martyrdom in 155 AD, the elderly bishop Polycarp was eighty-six years old when the Roman proconsul demanded he renounce Christ to save himself. Polycarp's reply, recorded by the Christians who watched him burn, became one of the most-quoted lines in early Christian witness. What did he say?"
> → "Eighty and six years I have served Him, and He never did me wrong; how can I now blaspheme my King who saved me?"

The substance is in the stem (Polycarp, 155 AD, age 86, proconsul, witnesses recording). The answer is the most memorable cool fact (the exact quote). No weasel closer. No doctrine quiz. STORY.

## §9 No weasel closers (§15 applied from day 1)

Per `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §15`: pointed concrete questions only. Banned closers (same list as other banks) — "What's the recognition?", "What does this illustrate?", "What's the moral?", "What does the story teach?" etc.

For theology specifically, additional banned framings:
- "What's the doctrinal significance?" — abstract meta-prompt
- "What's the lesson?" — too vague
- "Why does this matter?" — generic

**Allowed concrete closers:**
- "What did he say?" / "What did she do?"
- "What was the object's name?"
- "Where did he go next?"
- "How many died?"
- "What did he refuse to do?"

## §10 Teach-before-test (§16 applied from day 1)

Per `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §16`: foundational terms need anchors. For theology specifically:

- **Greek + Norse pantheons** — kids may not know who Zeus, Odin, Thor, Hera, Athena are. Each major figure needs a T1 or T2 foundational question that introduces them via a vivid story (Athena springing from Zeus's forehead fully-armed; Thor's red beard and goat-drawn chariot; Persephone abducted by Hades while picking flowers)
- **Bible figures kids may not know** — Esther, Daniel, Job, Jeremiah, Hezekiah all need foundational anchors before deeper-tier questions reference them
- **Saint stories** — Polycarp at 86, Sebastian shot full of arrows, Lawrence on the grill — each name needs at least one foundational question
- **Arthurian** — Excalibur (the sword), the Round Table, the Grail, Mordred, Lancelot, Guinevere, Merlin — all need foundational anchors at T1-T2
- **Robin Hood + medieval-legend** — Sherwood Forest, Little John, Friar Tuck, Maid Marian, the Sheriff of Nottingham, Tyburn Tree — same
- **Beowulf, Roland, El Cid, Faust** — each named legendary figure needs at least one foundational story-introduction question

When a T3+ question references a figure, that figure should already have a T1 or T2 foundational story-question elsewhere in the bank.
