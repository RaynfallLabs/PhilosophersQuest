# Cooking Bank Question Templates (Source of Truth)

This document is the recipe every cooking question is built from. `COOKING_FRAMEWORK.md` is the philosophy (the "why"); this document is the mechanics (the "how"). If a generated question doesn't match a pattern here, it isn't shipped.

The chain of authority:
1. The user's stance (conversation + memory) — highest authority
2. `COOKING_FRAMEWORK.md` — long-form principles + substantive moral vision
3. **`COOKING_TEMPLATES.md` (this doc)** — concrete patterns
4. Generator scripts

Generators do not invent patterns. They instantiate patterns from this doc.

## 1. Core principles

- The bank teaches **technique + chemistry + cultural inheritance + history as lens**, not recipe-recall or definition lookup
- Chef names, restaurant names, technique terms in French/Italian/etc. belong in **context**, never as the test
- Choices must be parallel in shape — all noun phrases OR all "X — Y" structures
- Stem must contain a **forcing constraint** that picks out one answer
- **Name the subject of analysis.** If the question asks "which technique unifies X" or "which culture invented Y" or "what chemistry produces Z," name X / Y / Z in the stem. **Withholding the subject is acceptable ONLY in Pattern D (Identify-by-trait), where the player is supposed to identify the subject from the description.** All other patterns must name what they're asking about. The wonder lives in the chemistry/cultural depth, NOT in making the player guess "kimchi" from regional cues before they can answer the actual question.
- The bank does NOT moralize against meat-eating, fast food, industrial agriculture, sugar, gluten, or any other contested food-politics topic. T4-T5 critique content uses the recognize-the-move pedagogy.
- Religious dietary law is descriptive cultural fact, never endorsement or critique
- Subject identifier stays `cooking` for code stability

| Tier | Reading age | Voice | Scene material |

### Vocabulary policy

Specialist terms (Maillard, gluten, emulsion, denaturation, lacto-fermentation) appear with inline definitions on first use at T3+. French/Italian/Japanese technique terms appear with inline equivalents (sauté = pan-fry over high heat; mise en place = "everything in its place"; dashi = Japanese broth from kombu and bonito flakes).

## 4. Stem patterns by question shape

### A. Wonder-fact recognition
Stem describes a bizarre true cooking fact; question asks which specific fact/number/cause is real.

> *"Sushi was originally a method of fish preservation in the rice paddies of Southeast Asia. The fermented rice was a byproduct of the process. What role did the rice originally serve?"*
> Choices: "It generated acid that preserved the fish for months / It absorbed water and was discarded at every meal / It served as a flavoring for the preserved fish / It marked the social class of the diner"

T-range: T1-T5.

### B. Common-knowledge flip
Stem assumes a common-knowledge cooking fact, then asks the question that subverts it.

> *"'Searing meat locks in the juices,' people say. Modern food science shows that searing does NOT lock in juices. What does it actually do?"*

T-range: T1-T3.

### C. Mechanism-why
Stem gives a kitchen phenomenon; question asks the chemistry/physics behind it.

> *"Why does dough rise when yeast is added? What gas, produced by what process, expands the dough?"*

T-range: T2-T5. The science-spine of the bank.

### D. Identify-by-trait (cuisine or ingredient bundle)
Stem gives a bundle of ingredients/techniques; question asks which cuisine or what ingredient.

> *"A cuisine uses fish sauce, kaffir lime leaves, galangal, lemongrass, Thai chili, and palm sugar in a balance of salty, sour, hot, and sweet. Which cuisine is this?"*

T-range: T2-T5.

### E. Tradition role
Stem describes a cuisine; question asks what role a specific ingredient or technique plays.

> *"What role does dashi play in Japanese cooking, and why is substituting Western stock such a poor approximation?"*

T-range: T3-T5.

### F. Comparative wonder
Two cuisines, techniques, or sauces overlap on one axis but differ critically on another.

> *"French béchamel and Italian besciamella use the same three ingredients (butter, flour, milk). Only one is typically used as a base for further sauces. Which, and why?"*

T-range: T3-T5.

### G. Technique-why
Why braise vs. roast? Why brine? Why rest meat after cooking?

> *"Tough cuts of meat like beef chuck benefit from braising rather than roasting. What structural change in the meat is the long, low, moist heat designed to drive?"*

T-range: T2-T5.

### H. Cultural / historical / lens-into-civilization (T3-T5)
Food as historical lens. What does X dish at Y time tell us about Z aspect of that civilization?

> *"Royal Navy sailors after 1795 received daily lemon or lime juice rations. The 'limey' epithet for British sailors followed. What broader development did this prevent and enable?"*

T-range: T3-T5. Etiquette-origin questions also live here.

### I. Non-obvious ingredient/term/etiquette
A genuinely interesting cooking term or ingredient most people don't know.

> *"What is 'koji,' and what three Japanese fermented products depend on it?"*

T-range: T2-T5. Fine because the term is not common knowledge.

### J. Food politics recognize-the-move (T4-T5 ONLY)
A critic articulates a specific analytical move about food policy or food culture. Question asks which move.

> *"A food writer argues: 'Fast food sells cheap calories you can see, but the dietary diversity, fresh-skill development, and family table tradition you lose are invisible.' Which analytical move is the writer making?"*
> Choices: "Bastiat's seen vs unseen / Sowell's compared-to-what / Hayek's knowledge problem / Public choice"

T-range: T4-T5. Same shape as the philosophy bank's surface-good critique pattern. Never close with a verdict.

## 5. Choice structures

The defining rule: all four choices share the same shape.

### 5.1 Wonder-fact / mechanism-why / identify-by-trait
Short noun-phrase or clause-form choices. No "X — Y" structure required.
- Correct: the actual cause / cuisine / ingredient
- Distractors: plausible-wrong cuisines, plausible-wrong mechanisms

### 5.2 Cultural / historical / etiquette / food-politics
"[Name/move/tradition] — [explanation]" parallel structure.
- Correct: the actual move/tradition with the right gloss
- Distractors: other real moves/traditions, accurately stated, that don't fit this scenario

### 5.3 Comparative wonder
"[Tradition A] does X because Y / [Tradition B] does X because Z" with parallel grammar.

## 7. Anti-patterns (banned)

### 7.1 Stem anti-patterns

- *"What does [French/Italian technique term] mean?"* — definition lookup
- *"In what year did [chef/cookbook] [event]?"* — pure date recall
- *"How many cups of flour in a standard X?"* — recipe memorization
- *"What restaurant did Escoffier work at?"* — proper-noun trivia
- *"Is gluten/sugar/meat/fat bad for you?"* — fad diet moralism
- *"Are processed foods poison?"* — industrial-food blanket condemnation
- *"Should everyone be [vegan/keto/Paleo/carnivore]?"* — diet endorsement / critique
- *"Is [kosher/halal/Hindu vegetarian] practice valid?"* — religious adjudication
- *"What's the word for cooking food on a grill?"* — toddler kitchen lookup
- *"What's the white salty crystal in food?"* — toddler-trivial answer

### 7.2 Toddler-banned answers

Banned as correct answers — questions where the answer is one of these have failed the bank:

| Banned answer | Why |
|---|---|
| pizza | toddler-obvious |
| hamburger / burger | toddler-obvious |
| french fries | toddler-obvious |
| spaghetti | toddler-obvious |
| pasta (generic) | toddler-obvious; "tagliatelle" or "orecchiette" fine |
| bread (generic) | toddler-obvious; "ciabatta," "naan," "injera" fine |
| ice cream | toddler-obvious |
| cake (generic) | toddler-obvious; specific cakes fine |
| cookie | toddler-obvious |
| sandwich | toddler-obvious |
| salt | toddler-obvious answer to "what makes things salty?" |
| sugar | toddler-obvious answer to "what makes things sweet?" |
| oven, stove, pan, knife, frying pan, fork, spoon, plate | kitchen-equipment recall |
| boiling, frying, baking, grilling | generic cooking-method recall (specific techniques fine) |
| carrot, potato, tomato, onion (as answers to toddler questions) | toddler-obvious "what is X" |

These ARE fine as distractors, in context, or in stems setting up a wonder question about them. The ban is specifically: don't make the answer a toddler-trivial fact.

### 7.3 Choice anti-patterns

- Only correct answer carries citation / year / chef name (citation skim-tell)
- Distractors are absurd ("a magic spice")
- One choice conspicuously longer
- Distractors with content not introduced in stem

### 7.4 Context anti-patterns

- Authoring meta-references (template section, pattern name, tier label)
- Context shorter than 60 chars at T3+
- Context that just repeats the answer

## 8. Structural gates

### 8.1 Deterministic + heuristic (carried from animal where applicable)

| Gate | Check |
|---|---|
| **schema** | Required fields present |
| **length_budget** | Per-tier caps respected |
| **length_parity** | max/min ≤ 1.30, max dev ≤ 15% |
| **duplicate** | No stem duplicates |
| **choice_shape_parity** | All 4 choices share shape |
| **anti_pattern_clear** | No banned phrasings from §7.1 |
| **no_toddler_recall** | Correct answer not in §7.2 toddler-banned list; stem not a toddler kitchen lookup |
| **forcing_constraint** | Stem has scenario detail to pick one answer |
| **wonder_bias_check** | Stem has chemistry, technique, ingredient, cultural, or historical signal |
| **context_no_meta_references** | No template-section refs, pattern names, tier labels in context |

### 8.2 Judgment gates (Opus subagent)

| Gate | Check |
|---|---|
| **factual_accuracy** | The named chemistry/technique/cuisine/history claim is actually true |
| **distractor_coherence** | Each distractor's text matches its labeled concept |
| **distractor_plausibility** | Each distractor is a real cooking fact / real cuisine / real chemistry — no absurd distractors |
| **single_defensible_answer** | Only one choice fits the scenario |
| **wonder_present** | Question reveals something surprising/non-obvious |
| **no_food_moralism** | T4-T5 food-politics questions identify analytical moves, never declare verdicts on fast food / industrial agriculture / fad diets / religious practice |

## 9. Example bank

40 user-approved exemplars (1 per topic × tier cell) live in `tools/quizgen/scratch/_cooking_exemplars.py`. Generators consult these for shape/voice. New questions must be visually consistent with the exemplars at their tier.

## 10. Workflow rule (sample-before-scale)

Every bulk-generation batch ≤ 50 questions stops here:
1. Generate N candidates per cell
2. Run all deterministic gates + judgment criteria baked into prompt
3. Show user 5 random samples per topic
4. Yes / no / fix
5. If yes → commit, next batch
6. If no → fix and re-sample

**No batch merged without user yes-on-samples.**

## 11. What this document does NOT do

- Doesn't promise subjective elegance — still human judgment
- Doesn't replace `COOKING_FRAMEWORK.md` — mechanics on top of philosophy
- Doesn't enforce wonder perfectly — wonder check is heuristic; final judgment is human

What it DOES promise: structural failures (toddler-level kitchen lookups, recipe memorization, fad-diet moralism, industrial-food verdicts, religious adjudication, mundane "mom's spaghetti" framings) become catchable BEFORE the bank touches user-visible play.
