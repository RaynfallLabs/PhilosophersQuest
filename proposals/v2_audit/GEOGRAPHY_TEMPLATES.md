# Geography Bank Question Templates (Source of Truth)

This document is the recipe every geography question is built from. `GEOGRAPHY_FRAMEWORK.md` is the philosophy (the "why"); this document is the mechanics (the "how"). If a generated question doesn't match a pattern here, it isn't shipped.

The chain of authority:
1. The user's stance (conversation + memory) — highest authority
2. `docs/quiz/moral_vision.md` — SUPREME rubric; the soul of the bank
3. `SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md` — cross-subject universal rules
4. `GEOGRAPHY_FRAMEWORK.md` — long-form principles + substantive moral vision
5. **`GEOGRAPHY_TEMPLATES.md` (this doc)** — concrete patterns
6. Generator scripts

Generators do not invent patterns. They instantiate patterns from this doc.

## 1. Core principles

- The bank teaches **wonder + scientific accuracy + human adaptation + how geography shapes culture/religion**
- Capital names, explorer dates, country populations, philosopher names, etc. live in **context** — never as the test
- Choices must be parallel in shape
- Stem must contain a **forcing constraint** picking out one answer
- **Place-name-in-geographic-context** — when a place carries historical weight, the question is about the geographic feature that made it relevant, not the history-as-trivia
- **Place-anchoring rule** — when a place is named in the stem as scaffolding (not the answer), anchor it: range, region, country, continent. A first-time reader who doesn't already know the place must still be able to ground the question. Recognition-type questions are exempt (where the place IS the answer), but the stem's *description* must let the player ground without the name.
- **No theory-stacking** — when a question references named theories or theory-laden concepts, the stem must ground at least one in a concrete scenario: a named person making a specific argument, a specific historical event, a specific place. Pattern J questions must demonstrate the move concretely, never as abstract framing-vs-framing.
- T4-T5 questions on the geographic determinism debate use the **Sowell vs Diamond recognize-the-move pattern** (parallel to care ethics)
- No environmental moralism; climate-change-as-policy only as T5 recognize-the-move
- Religious geography is DESCRIPTIVE cultural fact (Christian centerline + other religions substantial)
- Subject identifier stays `geography`

## 4. Stem patterns by question shape

### A. Wonder-fact recognition
> *"Iceland sits directly on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge where the North American and Eurasian plates are spreading apart. The country gains about 2 cm of width per year. What does this mean for Iceland's volcanism?"*

T-range: T1-T5. Most common pattern.

### B. Common-knowledge flip
> *"Most people think the Sahara has always been desert. What does the Green Sahara period (around 6000 BC) tell us?"*

T-range: T1-T3.

### C. Mechanism-why
> *"Why does the Sahara exist at the latitude it does? What atmospheric mechanism produces deserts at ~30° N and S?"*

T-range: T2-T5. Real climate / geology / hydrology mechanisms.

### D. Identify-by-trait
> *"A landlocked country sits between Russia and China. It has the lowest population density of any country, a steppe + Gobi desert geography, a horse-and-yurt nomadic tradition. Which country is this?"*

T-range: T2-T5. (NOT capital recall; this asks for the country via its geographic features.)

### E. Place-in-context (geographic feature → historical function)
> *"Kolyma in Russia's Far East — subarctic permafrost, mean January temperature -38°C, isolation by the Sea of Okhotsk and 6,000+ km from Moscow. What geographic features made this region the setting for Stalin's most notorious Gulag camps?"*

T-range: T3-T5. **Place name anchored to GEOGRAPHIC features that made the history possible** — not history-as-trivia.

### F. Comparative wonder
> *"K2 (8,611m, Karakoram on the Pakistan-China border) is shorter than Mt Everest (8,849m, Himalaya on the Nepal-Tibet border). Yet K2's summit fatality rate is roughly 25%, vs Everest's roughly 4%. Which geographic feature of K2 explains the lethality difference?"*

T-range: T3-T5.

### G. Adaptation-why
> *"Norse settlers established colonies in Greenland around 985 AD during the Medieval Warm Period. By 1450 the colonies were gone. What geographic + climate change explains the abandonment?"*

T-range: T2-T5. Human adaptation to extreme environments.

### H. Geography-shapes-culture
> *"The Nile floods predictably each summer, deposits silt, then recedes — a cycle Egyptians could time by stars. How did this geographic predictability shape ancient Egyptian religion?"*

T-range: T3-T5. **The bank's distinctive pedagogy** — geography → religion/culture.

### I. Non-obvious term or named scenario
> *"What is a 'qanat,' and how did this Persian innovation make agriculture possible across the Iranian plateau for ~3,000 years?"*

T-range: T2-T5.

### J. Geographic-determinism recognize-the-move (T4-T5 only)
> *"Jared Diamond argues that Eurasia's east-west axis enabled faster spread of crops and livestock than the Americas' north-south axis, explaining differential development. Thomas Sowell argues this overstates geography and understates cultural factors — work habits, family structure, capital accumulation — that geography cannot explain. Which is the Sowell critique's central move?"*

T-range: T4-T5. **Same shape as philosophy's care-ethics critique pattern**: define Diamond once or twice; then T4-T5 weight goes to Sowell's substantive critique. Identify the analytical move, NEVER declare a verdict on which view is "correct."

## 5. Choice structures

The defining rule: all four choices share the same shape.

### 5.1 Wonder-fact / mechanism-why / identify-by-trait
Short noun-phrase or clause-form choices. Distractors are plausible-wrong facts/places/explanations.

### 5.2 Place-in-context
"[Geographic feature] — [function]" parallel structure. Distractors are real other places that don't quite fit.

### 5.3 Geography-shapes-culture
"[Geographic feature] → [cultural/religious consequence]" parallel. Distractors are other plausible-wrong causal chains.

### 5.4 Recognize-the-move (T4-T5 J pattern)
"[Name of analytical move] — [explanation of move]" parallel. Distractors are other real analytical moves applied to other scenarios.

## 7. Anti-patterns (banned)

### 7.1 Stem anti-patterns
- *"What is the capital of [country]?"* — banned full stop per `feedback_no_rote_wonder.md`
- *"When did [explorer] reach [place]?"* — pure date recall
- *"What does [biome / latitude / GPS / etc.] mean?"* — definition lookup
- *"What's the tallest mountain / longest river / biggest country?"* — toddler trivia
- *"Which continent is [country] on?"* — basic
- *"How many oceans?"* — basic
- *"Is climate change real?"* / *"Should we adopt [policy]?"* — policy moralizing
- *"Is colonialism evil?"* — anti-anti-white framing
- *"Just spot it."* / *"Which one is it?"* — kindergarten
- *"K2 is shorter than Everest..."* — both peaks named with no range/country anchor for a first-time reader. Free-floating place names need anchors (range, region, country) in the stem unless they ARE the answer (recognition-type).
- *"Pleistocene climate cycles produced ice ages... modern policy debates inherit a different framing... Which move is the geographer making?"* — theory-stacking. Stem references multiple theory-laden frameworks without grounding any in a concrete scenario. Pattern J requires a named figure making a specific argument the player can picture.
- *"An island off Yemen has dragon blood trees... about what fraction of its plant species are found nowhere else?"* — **wonder-deferred-to-context.** Stem describes wondrous named features (dragon blood trees, bottle trees), then asks for a rote proportion. The amazing facts (6 million years of isolation, Italian violin varnish trade) sit unused in context. The wonder MUST live in the stem; the question must test something specific about the wonder (use, mechanism, breakthrough), not a rote number. Per SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §8 and [[feedback_no_rote_wonder]].

### 7.2 Toddler-banned answers

Banned as correct answers (questions where the answer is one of these have failed):

| Banned answer | Why |
|---|---|
| Capital name of any country (Paris, London, Tokyo, etc.) | per moral vision |
| Mount Everest (as answer to "tallest mountain") | toddler-trivial |
| Nile / Amazon (as answer to "longest river") | toddler-trivial |
| Russia (as answer to "biggest country") | toddler-trivial |
| China / India (as answer to "most populous") | toddler-trivial |
| Pacific Ocean (as answer to "largest ocean") | toddler-trivial |
| Asia (as answer to "biggest continent") | toddler-trivial |
| Sahara (as answer to "biggest desert") | toddler-trivial |
| Antarctica (as answer to "coldest continent") | toddler-trivial |

These are fine as DISTRACTORS, in CONTEXT, or in stems setting up a wonder question. Banned only when the answer is the toddler-trivial fact.

### 7.3 Choice anti-patterns
- Citation skim-tells (only correct answer has date/citation)
- Decoration mismatch (only correct answer has parens with examples, quoted phrases, citations, or in-line lists; others use straight prose). The em-dash gate catches the dash, but within-shape decoration must also match across all four choices.
- Absurd distractors (no "the moon" as a country)
- One choice conspicuously longer

### 7.4 Context anti-patterns
- Template-section refs / pattern names / tier labels (caught by `context_no_meta_references` gate)
- Context shorter than 60 chars at T3+
- Context that just repeats the answer

## 8. Structural gates

### 8.1 Deterministic (carried from cooking + new)

| Gate | Check |
|---|---|
| **schema** | Required fields present |
| **length_budget** | Per-tier caps respected |
| **length_parity** | max/min ≤ 1.30, max dev ≤ 15% |
| **duplicate** | No stem duplicates |
| **choice_shape_parity** | All 4 choices share shape |
| **anti_pattern_clear** | No banned phrasings from §7.1 |
| **no_toddler_recall** | Correct answer not in §7.2 toddler-banned list |
| **forcing_constraint** | Stem has scenario detail picking one answer |
| **wonder_bias_check** | Stem has geographic / scientific / cultural / historical signal |
| **context_no_meta_references** | No template-section refs / pattern names / tier labels |
| **subject_named** | Non-D patterns name the subject of analysis |
| **stem_answer_overlap** | Answer doesn't telegraph by reusing stem tokens (>65%) or opening with verbatim stem phrase. Pattern-I/D/comma exemptions apply. |
| **list_comma_lint** | Flag 3-item participial lists missing serial commas |
| **no_capital_recall** (NEW for geography) | Correct answer is not a capital city name being tested as recall (e.g., "What is the capital of X" → "Paris") |
| **no_climate_policy_verdict** (NEW for geography) | Stems can't ask "Is climate change real?" / "Should we adopt X policy?" — climate-change-as-policy only as recognize-the-move J pattern |
| **place_anchor_check** (NEW for geography) | When stem names a specific place (city, mountain, river, etc.), check stem also contains a geographic anchor (continent, country, region, mountain range). Soft-warn — recognition-type Qs legitimately defer to choices. Human-review flag, not hard reject. |
| **no_theory_stacking_check** (NEW for geography) | When stem contains 2+ named theories or theory-laden concept tokens (e.g., "epistemic," "framing," "thesis," "doctrine," named -isms) without a concrete-scenario anchor (named person, specific event, quoted argument), flag for human review. Pattern J questions must demonstrate the move concretely. Soft-warn. **Deterministic counterpart to philosophy's `inline_teaching` judgment gate** (PHILOSOPHY_TEMPLATES.md §8.2); both implement SHARED_PRINCIPLES.md §1. |

### 8.2 Judgment gates (Opus subagent)

| Gate | Check |
|---|---|
| **factual_accuracy** | Named place/feature/mechanism/date is true geography |
| **distractor_coherence** | Each distractor matches its labeled concept |
| **distractor_plausibility** | Distractors are real places / real geography, not absurd |
| **single_defensible_answer** | Only one choice fits the scenario |
| **wonder_present** | Question reveals something surprising/non-obvious/place-evoking |
| **no_anti_western_framing** | Western achievement (cartographic, exploratory, American) presented as substantive, not as colonialism-extended |

## 9. Example bank

40 user-approved exemplars (1 per topic × tier cell) live in `tools/quizgen/scratch/_geography_exemplars.py`. Generators consult these for shape/voice.

## 10. Workflow rule (sample-before-scale)

Every bulk-generation batch ≤ 50 questions stops here:
1. Generate N candidates per cell
2. Run all deterministic gates + judgment criteria in prompt
3. Show user 5 random samples per topic
4. Yes / no / fix
5. If yes → commit, next batch
6. If no → fix and re-sample

**No batch merged without user yes-on-samples.**

## 11. What this document does NOT do
- Doesn't promise subjective elegance — still human judgment
- Doesn't replace `GEOGRAPHY_FRAMEWORK.md`
- Doesn't enforce wonder perfectly — wonder check is heuristic

What it DOES promise: structural failures (capital-name recall, climate-policy verdicts, anti-anti-western framing, generic toddler-trivia, no-wonder definitional stems) become catchable BEFORE the bank touches user-visible play.
