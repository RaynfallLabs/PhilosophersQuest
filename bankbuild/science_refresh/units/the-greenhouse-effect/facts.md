## How the greenhouse effect works

F1. Much of the incoming sunlight passes through the atmosphere and heats the ground. (Grokipedia: "much incoming visible sunlight passes through the atmosphere"; Wikipedia: sunlight "passes through greenhouse gases to heat the Earth's surface".)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect

F2. The warmed surface gives off longwave infrared radiation, and the atmosphere's greenhouse gases absorb most of it: of about 396 W/m2 the surface emits, the atmosphere absorbs "around 350 W/m2".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth%27s_energy_budget

F3. Greenhouse gases "re-emit it in all directions, including downward toward the surface" (Grokipedia, Greenhouse effect); about half of the re-emitted energy heads back toward the surface (Grokipedia, Greenhouse gas). The downward "back radiation" averages about 333 W/m2 (Grokipedia, Earth's energy budget). Wikipedia: the air "emits longwave radiation in all directions, both upwards and downwards".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_gas ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth%27s_energy_budget ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect

F4. The heat is not all kept: Earth's outgoing longwave radiation to space, about 240 W/m2, roughly balances the sunlight it absorbs ("OLR approximates the absorbed shortwave radiation (ASR) at ~240 W/m2"). The atmosphere also absorbs about 23 percent of incoming sunlight itself, mainly by water vapour, ozone and aerosols (so "greenhouse gases block no sunlight" would be false, and no question rests on it).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth%27s_energy_budget

F5. A glass greenhouse works differently: it warms "primarily" by "suppression of convection rather than selective infrared absorption" (Grokipedia); "Greenhouses retain heat mainly by blocking convection (the movement of air)" (Wikipedia). The atmospheric greenhouse effect "is purely radiative in its basic formulation" (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect

## Which gases, and why not nitrogen and oxygen

F6. The main greenhouse gases are water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and ozone (Grokipedia, Greenhouse gas; Wikipedia: "the five most abundant greenhouse gases"). Water vapour gives about 50 percent of the natural greenhouse effect, clouds about 25 percent and carbon dioxide about 20 percent (Grokipedia); "Water vapor is the most important greenhouse gas overall" (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_gas ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas

F7. Nitrogen (78 percent) and oxygen (21 percent) make up 99 percent of Earth's dry air, and both are "transparent to infrared" (Grokipedia); Wikipedia: "99% of the Earth's dry atmosphere (excluding water vapor) is made up of nitrogen (N2) (78%) and oxygen (O2) (21%)".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas

F8. Why: nitrogen and oxygen are "homonuclear diatomic molecules lacking a permanent electric dipole moment" (Grokipedia), that is, each molecule is two atoms of the same element; when they vibrate, "they have no asymmetry in the distribution of their electrical charges", so they are "almost totally unaffected by infrared thermal radiation" (Wikipedia). Greenhouse gases have "asymmetric structures or polar bonds" (Grokipedia), and "all gases with three or more atoms (including H2O and CO2), are infrared active and act as greenhouse gases" (Wikipedia, Greenhouse effect).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_gas ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect

F9. Molecular masses: methane 16.04 g/mol (Grokipedia, Methane); water about 18 (two hydrogens of 1.008 and an oxygen of 15.999); nitrogen gas about 28 (two atoms of 14.007, Grokipedia, Nitrogen); oxygen gas about 32 (two atoms of about 15.999, Grokipedia, Oxygen); carbon dioxide 44.01 g/mol (Grokipedia, Carbon dioxide). So water and methane molecules are lighter than nitrogen's and oxygen's, and carbon dioxide's is heavier: weight does not sort greenhouse gases from the rest.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Methane ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Nitrogen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_dioxide

F10. Oxygen is a "highly reactive oxidizing agent" that "readily forms compounds such as oxides with most elements" (Grokipedia, Oxygen); nitrogen, with its strong triple bond, "exhibits minimal reactivity at room temperature" (Grokipedia, Nitrogen). So reactivity does not sort them either.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Nitrogen

F11. Nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide and methane are all colourless gases (Grokipedia: N2 a "colorless, odorless, and tasteless gas"; O2 a "colorless, odorless, and tasteless diatomic gas"; CO2 a "colorless, odorless, nonflammable gas"; methane "a colorless, odorless, tasteless ... gas").
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Nitrogen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_dioxide ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Methane

## Eunice Foote, 1856

F12. Eunice Newton Foote used two identical glass cylinders, about 4 inches across and 30 inches long, each fitted with thermometers, and an air pump; she set them in sunlight and read them every two to three minutes. She compared common air (dry and moist), carbon dioxide ("carbonic acid gas"), hydrogen and oxygen. Highest readings: hydrogen 104 F, common air 106 F, oxygen 108 F, carbon dioxide 125 F. The carbon dioxide receiver "became itself much heated—very sensibly more so than the other" and took "many times as long in cooling" once out of the sun (Wikipedia); "carbon dioxide maintained its high temperature much longer than other gases did" (Physics Today). Damp air "became significantly hotter than dry air" (Physics Today; Grokipedia: "Moist air heated more than dry air").
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Eunice_Newton_Foote ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunice_Newton_Foote ; https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/eunice-newton-footes-nearly-forgotten-discovery

F13. Foote concluded: "An atmosphere of that gas would give to our earth a high temperature." Her paper was read for her by Joseph Henry of the Smithsonian at the meeting of the American Association for the Advancement of Science in Albany, New York, on 23 August 1856, and printed in the American Journal of Science and Arts.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunice_Newton_Foote ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Eunice_Newton_Foote ; https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/first-paper-to-link-co2-and-global-warming-by-eunice-foote-1856/

F14. Her test's limit: her cylinders took in the whole of sunlight; "she did not differentiate between heat from the whole solar spectrum and what we now call long-wave infrared" (Royal Institution, quoting Roland Jackson's account); her apparatus "could not separate the effects of sunlight from infrared heat radiated by the Earth. Tyndall's more sophisticated equipment could make that distinction" (Wikipedia); "her work did not address the absorption of infrared radiation which is the core mechanism" (Grokipedia). Whether Tyndall knew of her paper: "there is no positive evidence that he did" (Royal Institution).
    https://www.rigb.org/node/3085 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunice_Newton_Foote ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect

## John Tyndall, from 1859

F15. Tyndall's apparatus (described in his 1861 paper on the work begun in 1859): an "experimental tube composed of brass, polished within", 4 feet long, closed at each end by "plates of rock-salt"; the heat source a copper cube of boiling water whose radiating face "is coated with lampblack"; a "thermo-electric pile" at the far end of the tube, wired to a galvanometer whose needle showed the heat arriving.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/John_Tyndall ; https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/articles/jstor-108724/ (OCR of Tyndall, Phil. Trans. 151, 1861)

F16. Tyndall began the experiments at the Royal Institution in London on 9 May 1859, showed the work at a Royal Institution discourse on 10 June 1859, and published the full results in 1861.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyndall ; https://www.rigb.org/node/3085

F17. Results: dry air, oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen each moved the needle by only about 1 degree ("Even by the aid of a magnifying lens I could not detect the slightest change of position" for dry air). "Air direct from the laboratory, containing therefore its carbonic acid" gave an absorption of 15, and the water vapour in it "produced an absorption at least equal to thirteen times that of the atmosphere itself" (1861 paper). Grokipedia: Tyndall "identified water vapor and carbon dioxide as primary absorbers of 'dark rays' (infrared), while oxygen and nitrogen transmitted them freely"; water vapour was the strongest absorber among the air's gases; carbon dioxide and ozone absorbed far more than nitrogen, oxygen or hydrogen. Wikipedia: water vapour "is the strongest absorber of radiant heat in the atmosphere and is the principal gas controlling air temperature". He also tested methane and other trace gases (Wikipedia).
    https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/articles/jstor-108724/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Greenhouse_effect ; https://grokipedia.com/page/John_Tyndall ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyndall

F18. Tyndall's conclusions on climate: of water vapour, "every variation of this constituent must produce a change of climate"; such changes "may in fact have produced all the mutations of climate which the researches of geologists reveal" (Royal Institution). His 1859 summary: "the atmosphere admits of the entrance of solar heat; but checks its exit" (Wikipedia). The Royal Institution: he showed "the absorption and radiation by certain gases of what we now call long-wave infrared radiation".
    https://www.rigb.org/node/3085 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyndall

## Svante Arrhenius, 1896

F19. Arrhenius, a Swedish chemist, published "On the Influence of Carbonic Acid in the Air upon the Temperature of the Ground" (Philosophical Magazine 41, 1896). His motive was the ice ages: the idea "originated from Arrhenius's efforts to explain glacial-interglacial cycles" (Grokipedia); he wrote that there was "no satisfactory hypothesis that could explain how the climatic conditions for an ice age could be realized" (the 1896 paper, via Le Moyne); "In developing a theory to explain the ice ages" (Wikipedia); "he saw CO2 as the key" (AIP).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Svante_Arrhenius ; https://web.lemoyne.edu/Giunta/ARRHENIUS.HTML ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svante_Arrhenius ; https://history.aip.org/climate/co2.htm

F20. How: "thousands of manual calculations to tabulate temperature changes" (Grokipedia); "a laborious numerical computation" (AIP); Arrhenius himself called them "these tedious calculations" (1896 paper, via Le Moyne). He worked the change by zones of latitude (AIP: "averaged over all zones of latitude") for carbon dioxide at 0.67, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 times the amount then in the air (Le Moyne). For how strongly the air absorbs, he used Samuel Langley's "infrared observations of the moon" (Wikipedia); historians credit "Arrhenius's use of Samuel P. Langley's measurements of heat emission in the lunar spectrum" (Crawford 1997, via search).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Svante_Arrhenius ; https://history.aip.org/climate/co2.htm ; https://web.lemoyne.edu/Giunta/ARRHENIUS.HTML ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svante_Arrhenius

F21. His results: cutting carbon dioxide to 0.62-0.55 of its amount would lower temperatures by 4-5 degrees C between the 40th and 50th parallels (enough for an ice age); halving it would cool the global mean by 4-5 C (Grokipedia); doubling it would warm Earth by "some 5-6 C" (AIP; Grokipedia 5-6 C). At the coal burning of 1896 the doubling "would take perhaps three thousand years" (AIP; Grokipedia "over 3,000 years"). His colleague Arvid Hogbom supplied the coal figures.
    https://web.lemoyne.edu/Giunta/ARRHENIUS.HTML ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Svante_Arrhenius ; https://history.aip.org/climate/co2.htm

## Charles David Keeling, from 1958

F22. Keeling's Mauna Loa measurements began on 29 March 1958, with a first reading of 313 parts per million; the observatory stands at about 3,400 m on the northern flank of the Mauna Loa volcano in Hawaii, amid "bare lava fields devoid of vegetation, soil, or significant human activity" (Grokipedia, Keeling Curve). Wikipedia: chosen "due to its remote location far from continents and its lack of vegetation". Measurements at the South Pole began in 1957 (Grokipedia, Charles David Keeling).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Keeling_Curve ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Charles_David_Keeling ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeling_Curve

F23. The expectation Keeling's record tested: in the 1950s scientists assumed "the oceans would promptly absorb nearly all the CO2 humanity might emit"; Revelle and Suess showed the ocean's surface layer could take up barely "one-tenth the amount a naive calculation would have predicted" (AIP, Spencer Weart).
    https://history.aip.org/climate/co2.htm

F24. Keeling's 1960 paper reported both a repeating seasonal cycle and a rise in carbon dioxide (Scripps, The History of the Keeling Curve); "by 1960 he reported a rising baseline" (AIP).
    https://keelingcurve.ucsd.edu/?p=276 ; https://history.aip.org/climate/co2.htm

F25. The yearly growth at Mauna Loa has been positive in every year of the record, 1959 to 2025 (smallest 0.31 ppm in 1964) (NOAA GML growth-rate table). The rate began "at about 0.7 ppm per year in the 1960s" and reached "over 2.5 ppm per year in the 2020s"; the level was about 430 ppm in March 2026 (Grokipedia).
    https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/gr.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Keeling_Curve

F26. The yearly zigzag: "a cyclic variation of about 6 ppm each year" at Mauna Loa (Wikipedia; Grokipedia "approximately 6 ppm"), from a May maximum to a September-October minimum. Cause: land plants. In spring and summer they take carbon dioxide from the air as they grow; in autumn and winter they "die off and decay, releasing CO2 back into the atmosphere" (Wikipedia); "Most of this vegetation is in the Northern Hemisphere, where most of the land is located" (Wikipedia). Grokipedia: "seasonal uptake and release by the Northern Hemisphere's terrestrial biosphere"; the South Pole shows "minimal seasonal variability"; Keeling's own 1958 first-year data rose from April, peaked in May and reached its low in October (Scripps).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Keeling_Curve ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Charles_David_Keeling ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeling_Curve ; https://keelingcurve.ucsd.edu/?p=276

## The carbon cycle's stores

F27. Earth's carbon stores (Grokipedia, Carbon cycle): the atmosphere about 900 GtC (elsewhere 800); the oceans about 38,000 GtC, "the largest active carbon reservoir"; vegetation 450-650 GtC; soils 1,500-2,400 GtC; fossil fuels about 5,000-10,000 GtC; sedimentary carbonate rocks 60 to 65 million GtC. WHOI: the ocean holds about 50 times more carbon than the atmosphere. So, leaving the carbonate rocks aside, the oceans hold the most.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_cycle ; https://www.whoi.edu/know-your-ocean/ocean-topics/climate-weather/ocean-based-climate-solutions/blue-carbon

F28. How carbon moves (Grokipedia, Carbon cycle): photosynthesis "fixes atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic compounds"; respiration and microbial decay return it to the air; the oceans exchange carbon dioxide with the air by dissolving it; shell-builders lay down carbonate sediments; over geological time buried life has "built extensive fossil fuel deposits"; burning "rapidly transfers ancient carbon to the atmosphere"; volcanoes release carbon dioxide from Earth's interior.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_cycle

## Volcanoes: Tambora and the year without a summer

F29. Mount Tambora, on Sumbawa in present-day Indonesia, "climaxed on April 10" 1815; it was "the largest and most powerful volcanic explosion in recorded history" (VEI 7); its plumes rose "to heights of up to 43 km", into the stratosphere, and it injected "around 60 megatons of sulfur dioxide" (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/1815_eruption_of_Mount_Tambora

F30. The Year Without a Summer, 1816: snow fell in New England in June and frosts struck in several months; cold and wet weather brought crop failures and famine in Europe. Global temperatures fell by about 0.4-0.7 C; the sulfate aerosols stayed aloft about 1-3 years, and effects lingered into 1818. The cooling is laid to the sulfate aerosols, not the ash, which fell mainly near the volcano; Tambora's carbon dioxide was negligible (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Year_Without_a_Summer ; https://grokipedia.com/page/1815_eruption_of_Mount_Tambora

F31. USGS: volcanic ash leaves the stratosphere quickly ("most of it is removed within several days to weeks") and "has little impact on climate change"; sulfur dioxide becomes sulfuric acid, which "condenses rapidly in the stratosphere to form fine sulfate aerosols", which "increase the reflection of radiation from the Sun back into space", cooling the lower atmosphere "for periods of one to three years". Present-day volcanoes "release less than a percent of the carbon dioxide released currently by human activities".
    https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/volcanoes-can-affect-climate

## Common misconceptions (for honest wrong choices)

M1. Greenhouse gases "bounce" or reflect the heat back and forth like a mirror (13 percent of seventh graders; Shepardson et al. 2011, Model 5). They absorb and re-emit (F3); nothing reflects.
M2. Greenhouse gases "trap" all of the Sun's energy (Shepardson et al. 2011). Earth sends about as much energy to space as it absorbs (F4).
M3. The greenhouse effect is a greenhouse for growing plants (29 percent, Model 1), or works like one; a glass greenhouse works by stopping air moving (F5).
M4. Greenhouse gases cause or form the ozone hole (6 percent, Model 2).
M5. Carbon dioxide is the only greenhouse gas; students are often unaware of water vapour's role (Shepardson et al. 2011, and the research reviews found in search). Tyndall's result (F17) and F6 correct it.
M6. A volcano cools the world by its ash cloud blocking the Sun; the year-long cooling is from sulfate droplets, since ash falls out in days to weeks (F31).
    https://eepro.naaee.org/research/eeresearch/students-mental-models-reveal-mixed-ideas-about-greenhouse-effect (Shepardson, Choi, Niyogi and Charusombat, "Seventh grade students' mental models of the greenhouse effect", Environmental Education Research 17, 2011) ; https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/volcanoes-can-affect-climate
