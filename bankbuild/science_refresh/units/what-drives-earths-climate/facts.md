## Energy in, energy out

F1. When the energy Earth absorbs from sunlight equals the energy it sends back to space, the global mean temperature holds steady; "any imbalance would drive temperature adjustments ... until balance restores" (Grokipedia). Wikipedia: warming happens when Earth "receives more energy than it gives back to space", cooling when outgoing energy is greater.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth%27s_energy_budget ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_energy_budget

F2. Earth now takes in more than it sends out. A joint NASA and NOAA study (Norman Loeb and colleagues, Geophysical Research Letters, June 2021) compared satellite measurements of the energy entering and leaving Earth (the CERES instruments) with the Argo ocean floats' record of ocean warming and found that Earth's energy imbalance "approximately doubled" from 2005 to 2019 (Wikipedia: from +0.42 to +1.12 W/m2). Loeb called it a mix of human-caused forcing and natural variability (a warm phase of the Pacific Decadal Oscillation).
    https://nasa.gov/feature/langley/joint-nasa-noaa-study-finds-earths-energy-imbalance-has-doubled/ ; https://news.agu.org/press-release/earths-energy-imbalance-has-doubled-since-2005/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_energy_budget

F3. Where the extra heat has gone, 1971-2020 (Karina von Schuckmann and colleagues, Earth System Science Data 15, 2023): "about 89 %" stored in the ocean, "about 6 % on land", "1 % in the atmosphere", and "about 4 %" melting ice (the cryosphere). Grokipedia gives ocean 89-93 percent, land about 6, atmosphere 1, ice 4; Wikipedia: "Over 90 percent of the extra energy" since 1970 is in the ocean. MISCONCEPTION: that the warming is mostly in the air.
    https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1675/2023/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth%27s_energy_budget ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_energy_budget

## Milankovic's orbital cycles

F4. The three cycles: the tilt (obliquity) "varies between 22.1 and 24.5 over a cycle of about 41,000 years"; the stretch of the orbit (eccentricity) loosely makes a 100,000-year cycle (major component 405,000 years); the wobble (precession) runs about 19,000 to 23,000 years for the seasons' timing (axial precession about 25,700 years). Earth's large Moon "plays a significant role in stabilizing the obliquity"; without it the tilt could swing "from near 0 to about 85".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Milankovitch_cycles ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milankovitch_cycles ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Axial_tilt ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt

F5. Milutin Milankovic (civil engineer, mathematician, astronomer, geophysicist) began his climate work in 1912; his 1941 Canon of Insolation sums up "nearly three decades of research" (Wikipedia). He began his insolation calculations while interned near Budapest in 1914-1918, and his tables reaching back 600,000 years were computed "without electronic assistance" (Grokipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Milankovi%C4%87 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Milutin_Milankovi%C4%87

F6. His key condition was cool summers: he chose "summer insolation at 65 degrees North" at the suggestion of Wladimir Koppen, reasoning that great ice sheets grew near that latitude and that cooler summers "might reduce summer snowmelt, leading to a positive annual snow budget and ice sheet growth" (NASA). Wikipedia: he "accepted Koppen's suggestion that cool summers were a crucial factor for glaciation." Grokipedia: reduced summer insolation at high northern latitudes favoured snow accumulation and glacier growth. MISCONCEPTION: that ice ages need colder winters with more snow.
    https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/milutin-milankovitch ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Milankovi%C4%87 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Milutin_Milankovi%C4%87

F7. CONFLICT (not used in any question): Wikipedia (James Croll) says Croll's 1860s theory centred on winters ("winters will tend to be colder when the Earth is further from the sun in that season"); Grokipedia (Milankovitch cycles) says Croll stressed "cooler northern hemisphere summers that allow snow accumulation." Croll is left out of the questions and contexts.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Croll ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Milankovitch_cycles

F8. Reception: "for about 50 years, Milankovitch's theory was largely ignored" (NASA); opponents interrupted his talk at the 1953 INQUA congress in Rome (Wikipedia); he died in 1958.
    https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/milutin-milankovitch ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Milankovi%C4%87

F9. The test: J. D. Hays, John Imbrie and N. J. Shackleton, "Variations in the Earth's Orbit: Pacemaker of the Ice Ages", Science 194, 1121-1132 (10 December 1976). They used deep-sea sediment cores from a Southern Hemisphere site "centrally located between Africa, Australia, and Antarctica", covering about 450,000 years (Grokipedia; a 1976 news report), measuring oxygen isotopes in plankton shells (which mainly track the waxing and waning of Northern Hemisphere ice sheets) and estimating sea-surface temperatures from plankton remains. Abstract (from the search snippets of the paper's PDF): climatic variance "is concentrated in three discrete spectral peaks at periods of 23,000, 42,000, and approximately 100,000 years", corresponding to "the dominant periods of the earth's solar orbit", containing "respectively about 10, 25, and 50 percent of the climatic variance"; they concluded that "changes in the earth's orbital geometry are the fundamental cause of the succession of Quaternary ice ages." NASA: the study "examined deep-sea sediment cores" and found major climate variations "closely associated with changes in the geometry" of the orbit.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Milankovitch_cycles ; https://www2.atmos.umd.edu/~nigam/AOSC617/Hays.Imbrie.Shackelton.Orbital.theory.ice.age.Science.1976.pdf (abstract read through search snippets only; the PDF did not parse) ; https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/milutin-milankovitch ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Milutin_Milankovi%C4%87

F10. Milankovic himself had expected the tilt to matter most ("he believed obliquity mattered most ... and so calculated a 41,000-year ice-age period", Wikipedia); the record's strongest beat was the 100,000-year one, though the stretch of the orbit changes the yearly sunlight least ("only a minor variation in global mean insolation of about 0.2%", Grokipedia). Why is still argued (the 100,000-year problem). The paper's own range of study, about 10,000 to 100,000 years a cycle, is far too coarse for the 11-year sunspot cycle.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milankovitch_cycles ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Milankovitch_cycles

## Volcanoes: Pinatubo, a forecast tested

F11. Mount Pinatubo, Philippines: climactic eruption on 15 June 1991, about 15-20 million tonnes of sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere (Grokipedia gives both "approximately 20 teragrams" and "about 17 megatons"; Wikipedia's "1.7 million t" looks like a slip and is not used). Cooling: "approximately 0.5 C observed over the subsequent 12-15 months, peaking in early 1992"; effect "persisted for 18-36 months" (Grokipedia). CONFLICT: Wikipedia's infobox gives 0.5 C, its text "about 0.4 C" globally and a "more conservative" 0.2 C; Penn State gives an observed drop of about 0.6 C. The questions say only "about half a degree" in a context.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo ; https://courses.ems.psu.edu/meteo469/node/141

F12. The forecast: James Hansen, Andrew Lacis, Reto Ruedy and Makiko Sato (NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies), "Potential climate impact of Mount Pinatubo eruption", Geophysical Research Letters, January 1992, used the GISS climate model: "intense aerosol cooling is predicted to begin late in 1991 and to maximize late in 1992"; "The model predicts a return to record warm levels in the later 1990s" (abstract). Richard Kerr, "Pinatubo global cooling on target", Science 259, January 1993: Hansen predicted a cooling of about half a degree Celsius, and "Year end temperature reports for 1992 are now showing that the prediction was on target." Penn State's course page: the model "predicted a bit too much cooling", partly because an unforeseen El Nino offset some of it. So the size was close but not exact, and the peak came earlier than forecast (F11).
    https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19920041613 ; https://www.osti.gov/biblio/7065976 ; https://courses.ems.psu.edu/meteo469/node/141

F13. USGS (already in the T4 unit): volcanic ash falls out within days to weeks; the sulfate haze cools for one to three years; volcanoes release less than a percent of the carbon dioxide people now release. MISCONCEPTION: a volcano warms the world with its carbon dioxide.

## Feedbacks

F14. Albedo: fresh snow and snow-covered sea ice reflect about 80-90 percent of sunlight (0.8-0.9); bare sea ice 0.50-0.70; open ocean only 0.07-0.10, absorbing over 90 percent (Grokipedia, Ice-albedo feedback; Grokipedia, Sea ice: open ocean about 0.06-0.10).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ice%E2%80%93albedo_feedback ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Sea_ice

F15. The ice-albedo feedback: ice loss "lowers surface albedo, enhancing the absorption of incoming shortwave solar radiation"; the extra energy warms the surface and melts more ice, exposing more dark surface. It is a positive feedback that "amplifies perturbations to the surface energy balance" rather than being the original trigger: "Initial perturbations, such as from atmospheric warming, initiate ice retreat" (Grokipedia). It works in the cooling direction too: "expanding ice cover reflects more sunlight, further cooling the planet" (Grokipedia, Milankovitch cycles). Other effects push back: after the record low of 2012, the 2013 minimum was 5.10 million square kilometres, far larger (NSIDC, F24).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ice%E2%80%93albedo_feedback ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Milankovitch_cycles ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Climate_change_feedbacks

F16. Physics for the wrong choices: melting absorbs heat, "the latent heat of fusion is approximately 334 J/g at 0 C", and freezing releases it (Grokipedia, Latent heat), so melting ice does not give off heat. Liquid water's specific heat is 4.181 J/(g K) at 25 C and ice's 2.050 at -10 C (Wikipedia table), so water needs about twice the heat per gram to warm. Sea ice is much less salty than sea water: new ice 30-35 parts per thousand falling to 4-10 within weeks, as brine drains out (Grokipedia, Sea ice), so its meltwater freshens the sea rather than salting it.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Latent_heat ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_specific_heat_capacities ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Sea_ice

F17. Water vapour is a feedback, not a starter: "a molecule of water vapor stays in the atmosphere just nine days, on average"; its "concentration depends on the temperature of the atmosphere"; "For every degree Celsius that Earth's atmospheric temperature rises, the amount of water vapor in the atmosphere can increase by about 7%"; "this effect more than doubles the warming that would happen due to increasing carbon dioxide alone"; increased water vapour "doesn't cause global warming. Instead, it's a consequence of it" (NASA, 2022). Grokipedia: "roughly a 7% increase in saturation specific humidity per 1 C of warming". Cloud feedbacks "constitute the dominant source of uncertainty in projections of equilibrium climate sensitivity" (Grokipedia, Climate change feedbacks).
    https://science.nasa.gov/earth/climate-change/steamy-relationships-how-atmospheric-water-vapor-amplifies-earths-greenhouse-effect ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Climate_change_feedbacks

## The past record

F18. The warm Eocene (about 56 to 34 million years ago): early Eocene surface temperatures "roughly 12 C higher than modern preindustrial levels"; carbon dioxide "exceeding 1,000 ppm, peaking at around 1,600 ppm" (Grokipedia). Wikipedia: average temperature at the start of the Eocene about 27 C; carbon dioxide 700-900 ppm by proxies, about 1,680 ppm in models; "little to no ice was present on Earth"; "forests covered most of the Earth including the poles"; palms grew "as far north as Alaska and northern Europe"; the Antarctic ice sheet "began to rapidly expand" only at the Eocene-Oligocene transition (Grokipedia: about 33.9 million years ago).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Eocene ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene

F19. Ellesmere Island (Canadian Arctic): Eocene "leaves and wood of metasequoia" (dawn redwood) from rock about 52-53 million years old show "humid, temperate conditions with mean annual temperatures estimated at 8-15 C"; paleomagnetic analysis puts the island "near 75-80 N during the early Tertiary", against 76-83 N today; today's vegetation "typifies high Arctic polar desert and tundra" (Grokipedia). Wikipedia: swamp cypress and dawn redwood remains on Ellesmere; "Even at that time, Ellesmere Island was only a few degrees in latitude further south than it is today." Mary Dawson and Robert West found alligators, turtles and mammals there in 1975; Jaelyn Eberle's team (Earth and Planetary Science Letters, 2010) used oxygen isotopes in bones and teeth to estimate a warmest month of about 19-20 C and a coldest month of about 0-3.5 C, so it "probably did not get below freezing" there; the fauna included alligators, giant tortoises and tapirs (University of Colorado release). MISCONCEPTION: the land simply drifted north from the tropics.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ellesmere_Island ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene ; https://www.colorado.edu/today/2010/08/24/new-study-shows-how-tortoises-alligators-thrived-high-arctic-some-50-million-years-ago

F20. The Sun brightens slowly, "rising by 1% every 110 million years" (Wikipedia, Future of Earth); it gave "approximately 70-75% of its current luminosity about 4 billion years ago" (Grokipedia). So in the Eocene the Sun was slightly dimmer than now, not brighter.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_Earth ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Faint_young_Sun_paradox

F21. The Medieval Warm Period, about AD 950-1250 (Grokipedia). How warm and how global is argued: the Sargasso Sea core shows sea surface about 1 C warmer around AD 1000 than the late 20th century, Loehle (2007) and Moberg and colleagues (2005) found warmth comparable to or above parts of the 20th century; Raphael Neukom and the PAGES 2k group (Nature, 2019) found "no evidence for globally coherent warm and cold periods over the preindustrial Common Era", the peaks falling at different times in different regions, while critics cited by Grokipedia say its Southern Hemisphere records are sparse. Not asked; used only as attributed context.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Medieval_Warm_Period ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Little_Ice_Age

F22. The Little Ice Age, "approximately 1300 to 1850 CE" (Grokipedia); Northern Hemisphere cooling of 0.5-1.0 C in proxy reconstructions, the global mean "likely under 0.3 C of cooling"; proposed causes: volcanic eruptions, low solar activity (the Sporer and Maunder Minima), ocean and sea-ice changes; "no evidence of synchronous cooling across hemispheres or continents" (Grokipedia, citing the 2019 Nature study).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Little_Ice_Age

F23. The Thames frost fairs: the river was recorded frozen over at London in "24 winters" between 1400 and the removal of medieval London Bridge in 1831; the 1814 fair "began on 1 February, and lasted four days" and "was the last frost fair" (Wikipedia). Why it no longer freezes in London: the old bridge's piers had timber casings (starlings) that narrowed its arches, and ice lodging against them acted "like a dam for the river at ebb tide"; in 1831 it was replaced with "a new bridge with wider arches, allowing the tide to flow more freely"; the river was "embanked in stages during the 19th century"; and "The climate was growing milder" (Wikipedia). Grokipedia: the old bridge had 19 narrow arches and the new one five wider arches; the new bridge allowed "faster tidal flow from the sea", bringing saltier water with a lower freezing point; Victorian embankments of the 1860s narrowed the channel and quickened the current; frost fairs "from 1608 to 1814". The embankments NARROWED the river (so "widened" is wrong), no sea barrier held back the tides in the 1800s (the Thames Barrier "became operational in October 1982", is open "during normal conditions" and closes "only during forecasted high-water events", Grokipedia, Thames Barrier), and a weir slowing the flow could not be a reason the river stopped freezing, since in the record slow water is what let it freeze ("narrow arches and starlings slowed the flow"; the embankments "created a swifter current", Grokipedia). (Not claimed: that no weir was ever built on the London Thames; Richmond's, far upstream of the frost fairs, may be one, and was not checked.) CONFLICT: Grokipedia gives the 1814 fair's dates two ways (25 January to 1 February, and 1 to 5 February); the questions say only "1814".
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames_frost_fairs ; https://grokipedia.com/page/River_Thames_frost_fairs ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Thames_Barrier

## A dated forecast, and what happened

F29. Wieslaw Maslowski (Naval Postgraduate School, Monterey) said in 2007 that the Arctic Ocean "might be nearly ice free in the summer as early as 2013", later revised to "2016 +/- 3 years" (Wikipedia). BBC, December 2007, quoting him: "Our projection of 2013 for the removal of ice in summer is not accounting for the last two minima, in 2005 and 2007" (copies of the BBC story found by search; the BBC page could not be fetched).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wies%C5%82aw_Mas%C5%82owski ; https://www.carbonbrief.org/could-arctic-summers-be-sea-ice-free-in-three-years-time/

F30. Al Gore at a side event of the UN climate conference in Copenhagen, 14 December 2009. The Times (via the New Zealand Herald): "Some of the models suggest to Dr [Wieslav] Maslowski that there is a 75 per cent chance" "that the entire north polar ice cap, during the summer months, could be completely ice-free within five to seven years." PolitiFact gives "during some of the summer months" (CONFLICT in the middle words; the stem quotes only the fragments both versions share: "a 75 per cent chance", "the entire north polar ice cap", "completely ice-free"). Maslowski to The Times: "It's unclear to me how this figure was arrived at, based on the information I provided to Al Gore's office"; PolitiFact: "I would never try to estimate likelihood at anything as exact as this." He said he had been "very explicit" about near-ice-free, not completely ice-free, conditions; Gore's office said the 75 percent was a "ballpark figure" Maslowski had used in a conversation years before.
    https://nzherald.co.nz/world/gores-ice-cap-claims-disputed/VO4JUJ4TGFXA4HDIHDDWQV3YYA ; https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2021/mar/02/facebook-posts/fact-checking-claims-al-gore-said-all-arctic-ice-w/ ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/gore-arctic-ice-may-soon-vanish-in-summer ; https://www.factcheck.org/2009/12/palin-vs-gore-climate-showdown/

F31. What happened: "ice-free" means less than 1 million square kilometres of sea ice (Wikipedia; Grokipedia). The lowest September extent on record was 3.41 million square kilometres on 16 September 2012, "49 percent below the 1979-2000 average minimum of 6.71 million" (NSIDC 2012; a later NSIDC table lists 3.39, a revision); 2013's minimum was 5.10 million, "1.12 million square kilometers lower than the 1981-2010 average", and the seven lowest extents had all come since 2007 (NSIDC, NASA 2013); 2016 about 4.15-4.16 million (NSIDC tables via NOAA); 2025's minimum was 4.60 million on 10 September, tenth lowest in 47 years (NSIDC). The September minimum is shrinking "12.2 percent per decade" against the 1981-2010 average (NASA). So every summer from 2010 to 2016 kept well over 3 million square kilometres of ice, and every one was below the long-term average. The IPCC (AR6) projects the Arctic "practically ice-free in September before the year 2050" under all scenarios (Wikipedia) -- an attributed projection, not used as a key.
    https://nsidc.org/sea-ice-today/analyses/arctic-sea-ice-extent-settles-record-seasonal-minimum ; https://climate.nasa.gov/news/986/arctic-sea-ice-minimum-in-2013-is-6th-lowest-on-record ; https://cires.colorado.edu/news/arctic-sea-ice-has-reached-minimum-extent-2025 ; https://science.nasa.gov/earth/explore/earth-indicators/arctic-sea-ice-minimum-extent ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_sea_ice_decline ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Arctic_sea_ice_decline

## The greening

F32. Zaichun Zhu and colleagues, Nature Climate Change, 25 April 2016: "a quarter to half of Earth's vegetated lands has shown significant greening" over about 35 years (1982-2015), from NOAA's AVHRR and NASA's MODIS satellite sensors; the added leaf area is about "two times the continental United States"; models attribute 70 percent of the greening to carbon dioxide fertilization, nitrogen 9 percent, with land-cover and climate change smaller. Josep Canadell: "the attribution to various drivers is based on models"; Philippe Ciais: "the fertilization effect diminishes over time" (NASA, 2016).
    https://www.nasa.gov/technology/carbon-dioxide-fertilization-greening-earth-study-finds/

F33. Chi Chen and colleagues, Nature Sustainability, 11 February 2019, using MODIS data for 2000-2017: "China and India account for one-third of the greening" but "contain only 9% of the planet's land area covered in vegetation"; "The effect stems mainly from ambitious tree planting programs in China and intensive agriculture in both countries"; the world gained "more than two million square miles of extra green leaf area per year", "a 5% increase". Rama Nemani: researchers first credited "a warmer, wetter climate and fertilization from the added carbon dioxide", but now "we see that humans are also contributing" (NASA, 2019). The study did not rule out carbon dioxide fertilization globally; it credited the China-India share to land use, not to carbon dioxide, weather or forests regrowing unaided.
    https://www.nasa.gov/feature/ames/human-activity-in-china-and-india-dominates-the-greening-of-earth-nasa-study-shows
