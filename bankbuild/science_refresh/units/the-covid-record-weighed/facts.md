## The Great Barrington Declaration (October 2020)

F1. The Great Barrington Declaration was written and signed on 4 October 2020 at the American Institute for Economic Research (AIER) in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, and published on 5 October 2020. Its three authors: Martin Kulldorff, professor of medicine at Harvard University; Sunetra Gupta, professor at Oxford University; Jay Bhattacharya, professor at Stanford University Medical School. (Grokipedia gives 4 October in one place and 5 October in another; the declaration's own page says "authored and signed" on 4 October.)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Great_Barrington_Declaration ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrington_Declaration ; https://gbdeclaration.org/

F2. The declaration's plan, in its own words: its approach "is to allow those who are at minimal risk of death to live their lives normally to build up immunity", and "We call this Focused Protection." "Those who are not vulnerable should immediately be allowed to resume life as normal." "Schools and universities should be open for in-person teaching." Protecting the vulnerable: "nursing homes should use staff with acquired immunity and perform frequent testing of other staff and all visitors"; "Retired people living at home should have groceries and other essentials delivered to their home." Grokipedia: focused protection shields people at highest risk, mainly the elderly and those with serious illnesses, while low-risk people resume normal activity. MISCONCEPTION: that it proposed letting the virus spread through everyone, old and young alike; it called for the old and vulnerable to be shielded.
    https://gbdeclaration.org/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Great_Barrington_Declaration

F3. The mainstream reply, a letter in The Lancet, "Scientific consensus on the COVID-19 pandemic: we need to act now" (the John Snow Memorandum, published online 14 October 2020): "Uncontrolled transmission in younger people risks significant morbidity and mortality across the whole population"; "Prolonged isolation of large swathes of the population is practically impossible and highly unethical"; herd immunity by infection is "a dangerous fallacy unsupported by scientific evidence"; spread would "overwhelm the ability of health-care systems to provide acute and routine care"; and "there is no evidence for lasting protective immunity to SARS-CoV-2 following natural infection". (Grokipedia gives 14 October in one place and 15 October in another.) Other critics, as reported by Wikipedia: Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus of the WHO (12 October 2020) called herd immunity by exposure "scientifically and ethically problematic"; David Naylor warned that hospitals "will be over-run and deaths will skyrocket"; Yvonne Maldonado noted that shielding meant separating about 40% of Americans at higher risk from the other 60%.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7557300/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/John_Snow_Memorandum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrington_Declaration

F4. Francis Collins, director of the National Institutes of Health (NIH), emailed Anthony Fauci on 8 October 2020 (with Clifford Lane of NIAID; Lawrence Tabak copied): "this proposal from the three fringe epidemiologists ..." and "There needs to be a quick and devastating published take down of its premises." Fauci replied the same night, pasting in a piece from Wired that he said "debunks" the declaration. (The Grokipedia page on Collins names Eric Green as the second recipient; the other sources name Clifford Lane. The questions name only Fauci.)
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrington_Declaration ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Great_Barrington_Declaration ; https://reason.com/2024/01/03/former-nih-director-ignoring-collateral-damage-inflicted-by-covid-19-policies-was-really-unfortunate/ ; https://thedailyeconomy.org/article/fauci-emails-and-some-alleged-science/

F5. The email became public in December 2021, released under a Freedom of Information Act (public-records) request filed by AIER (Harrigan and Magness, 19 December 2021; Vinay Prasad in STAT, 23 December 2021, who adds that Collins confirmed on television that the email was authentic and that he stood by it, believing focused protection would cause more deaths).
    https://thedailyeconomy.org/article/fauci-emails-and-some-alleged-science/ ; https://www.statnews.com/2021/12/23/at-a-time-when-the-u-s-needed-covid-19-dialogue-between-scientists-francis-collins-moved-to-shut-it-down/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Francis_Collins (FOIA)

## Sweden's open schools

F8. Sweden kept its preschools and compulsory schools (children under 16) open through the spring of 2020, with no nationwide closure; upper secondary schools and universities moved to distance learning from mid-March 2020. The schools had no face-mask policies. Grokipedia gives the reasons as low transmission risk from children, little severe illness among them, and the harms of closing.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Sweden ; https://news.ki.se/low-risk-of-severe-covid-19-in-children ; https://depts.washington.edu/pandemicalliance/2021/01/07/open-schools-covid-19-and-child-and-teacher-morbidity-in-sweden

F9. Jonas F. Ludvigsson, Lars Engerström, Charlotta Nordenhäll and Emma Larsson, "Open Schools, Covid-19, and Child and Teacher Morbidity in Sweden", New England Journal of Medicine, letter online 6 January 2021: all 1,951,905 Swedish children aged 1 to 16, 1 March to 30 June 2020. No child with COVID-19 died. 15 children were treated in intensive care for COVID-19 or the related inflammatory syndrome MIS-C (0.77 per 100,000, about one child in 130,000); 7 of them had MIS-C and 4 had underlying illnesses. Deaths from all causes were 69 in March-June 2020 against 65 in the four months before. Ludvigsson: severe COVID-19 "is so rare among children despite schools being open during the pandemic."
    https://news.ki.se/low-risk-of-severe-covid-19-in-children ; https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Sweden ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33406327/ ; https://depts.washington.edu/pandemicalliance/2021/01/07/open-schools-covid-19-and-child-and-teacher-morbidity-in-sweden

F10. Limits of that study: it did not measure transmission in homes or schools (Grokipedia); its authors noted wide confidence intervals. Teachers: the risk of severe COVID-19 among preschool and school teachers was "similar to other occupations (excluding healthcare workers), after adjusting for sex and age" (University of Washington summary of the letter).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Sweden ; https://depts.washington.edu/pandemicalliance/2021/01/07/open-schools-covid-19-and-child-and-teacher-morbidity-in-sweden

F11. Anna Eva Hallin, Henrik Danielsson, Thomas Nordström and Linda Fälth, "No learning loss in Sweden during the pandemic: Evidence from primary school reading assessments", International Journal of Educational Research 114 (2022), 102011: reading assessments of 97,073 pupils in grades 1-3. Word decoding and reading comprehension were not lower during the pandemic than before it; pupils from low socio-economic backgrounds were not especially affected; the share with weak decoding did not rise. The authors concluded that open schools benefited Swedish primary pupils. (Grokipedia describes a 2022 study finding no loss "in reading or mathematics"; Hallin's study is of reading only, so only reading is used.)
    https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/No-learning-loss-in-Sweden-during/991013296947802368 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Sweden

F12. The honest caveat: in the PIRLS 2021 reading study (results 16 May 2023), Sweden's 4th-graders scored 544, against 555 in 2016. Peter Fredriksson, director-general of Skolverket (the Swedish National Agency for Education), put the fall down to the pandemic and to a rising share of pupils who do not speak Swedish at home; although schools stayed open, about one school in three had teaching "severely affected for two months or more" by short closures or widespread sickness absence. Sweden's fall was only slightly worse than the roughly 10-point fall across the other EU and OECD countries.
    https://www.thelocal.se/20230516/swedish-reading-skills-fell-in-2021-despite-decision-to-keep-schools-open

F13. A country that closed: Per Engzell, Arun Frey and Mark Verhagen, PNAS 2021, the Netherlands: after "a relatively short lockdown (8 wk)", "a learning loss of about 3 percentile points or 0.08 standard deviations", "equivalent to one-fifth of a school year"; losses "up to 60% larger among students from less-educated homes".
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8092566/

## Learning loss in the United States

F14. The National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP), 2022 (tested January-March 2022): grade 4 maths fell 5 points and grade 8 maths 8 points from 2019, the "Largest score declines in NAEP mathematics at grades 4 and 8 since initial assessments in 1990". The long-term-trend test for 9-year-olds showed "the first ever score decline in mathematics" (7 points) and the largest fall in reading since 1990 (NCES, quoted by Grokipedia).
    https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/highlights/mathematics/2022/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/National_Assessment_of_Educational_Progress

F15. The Education Recovery Scorecard (Thomas Kane, Harvard Center for Education Policy Research; Sean Reardon, Stanford Educational Opportunity Project), 28 October 2022: the average pupil in grades 3-8 fell behind by about half a school year in maths and a quarter in reading; the poorest quarter of schools lost about two-thirds of a year in maths. "Poverty and time spent in remote learning affected learning loss, and learning losses were greater in districts that remained online longer" (AP). Districts that stayed remote in 2020-21 had bigger declines in elementary and middle-school maths than other districts in their state, but "many districts that went back in person had bigger losses than districts that stayed remote" (Chalkbeat); students also fell behind where schools reopened quickly (AP).
    https://www.gse.harvard.edu/news/22/10/new-research-provides-first-clear-picture-learning-loss-local-level ; https://www.pbs.org/newshour/education/covid-19-pandemic-massively-set-back-learning-especially-for-high-poverty-areas ; https://www.chalkbeat.org/2022/10/28/23429271/learning-loss-remote-learning-high-poverty-schools-harvard-stanford-research/

F16. The researchers' own caveats (Chalkbeat, 28 October 2022): Kane, "you'd think that the only thing driving achievement losses would be remote learning, but actually that does not seem to be the case"; the analysis "can't disentangle the effects of remote learning from other factors"; Reardon noted that districts that stayed remote longer may have differed in other ways, and called remote learning "probably a small piece of all the ways the pandemic affected children's outcomes"; the team planned to look at COVID death rates, internet access and parents' job losses. Neither poverty nor remote learning was "a perfect predictor" (AP).
    https://www.chalkbeat.org/2022/10/28/23429271/learning-loss-remote-learning-high-poverty-schools-harvard-stanford-research/ ; https://www.pbs.org/newshour/education/covid-19-pandemic-massively-set-back-learning-especially-for-high-poverty-areas

F17. Dan Goldhaber, Thomas Kane and colleagues, NBER Working Paper 30010 (May 2022), test data from 2.1 million students in 10,000 schools in 49 states: "remote instruction was a primary driver of widening achievement gaps"; "Math gaps did not widen in areas that remained in-person".
    https://www.nber.org/papers/w30010

## Masks: the 2023 Cochrane review

F18. Tom Jefferson and colleagues, "Physical interventions to interrupt or reduce the spread of respiratory viruses", Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 30 January 2023 (CD006207.pub6): 78 randomised and cluster-randomised trials in all (11 new, 610,872 participants). Medical or surgical masks against no masks in the community: wearing masks "probably makes little or no difference" to influenza-like or COVID-19-like illness (risk ratio 0.95, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.09; 9 trials, 276,917 participants; moderate-certainty evidence) and to laboratory-confirmed influenza or SARS-CoV-2 (RR 1.01; 6 trials, 13,919 participants). Authors' conclusions: "relatively low adherence with the interventions during the studies hampers drawing firm conclusions"; "The high risk of bias in the trials"; "There is a need for large, well-designed RCTs".
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9885521/

F19. Karla Soares-Weiser, Cochrane's editor-in-chief, statement of 10 March 2023: claims that the review shows "masks don't work" are "an inaccurate and misleading interpretation"; it would be accurate to say the review looked at "whether interventions to promote mask wearing help to slow the spread of respiratory viruses", and the results were "inconclusive"; the review "is not able to address the question of whether mask-wearing itself reduces people's risk". Adherence means how many people actually wore the masks when encouraged to: in the largest community trial, 42.3% of the mask group wore them against 13.3% of the control group. Cochrane apologised that its plain-language summary was open to misreading; in June 2024 it decided changes to the summary and abstract were not needed and stopped seeking them. MISCONCEPTION: that the review tested mask laws (mandates) or tested whether a mask protects its wearer; its trials tested offering and promoting masks.
    https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/statement-physical-interventions-interrupt-or-reduce-spread-respiratory-viruses-review

## Immunity after infection

F20. Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War 2.51 (the plague reached Athens in the summer of 430 BC; Thucydides caught it and survived): "Yet it was with those who had recovered from the disease that the sick and the dying found most compassion. These knew what it was from experience, and had now no fear for themselves; for the same man was never attacked twice—never at least fatally." (Crawley's translation.)
    https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/thuc-plague.asp ; https://livius.org/sources/content/thucydides-historian/the-plague ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Plague_of_Athens

F21. COVID-19 first emerged in Wuhan, China, in December 2019; the WHO declared a pandemic on 11 March 2020. So the Lancet letter of October 2020 (F3), which found "no evidence for lasting protective immunity", was written when the disease was less than a year old.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_pandemic

F22. COVID-19 Forecasting Team, "Past SARS-CoV-2 infection protection against re-infection: a systematic review and meta-analysis", The Lancet 401 (2023), 833-842 (online 16 February, issue of 11 March 2023): 65 studies from 19 countries. Protection against reinfection fell over time for every variant, and faster for omicron BA.1: at 40 weeks, 78.6% (49.8-93.6) for the ancestral, alpha and delta variants but 36.1% (24.4-51.3) for BA.1 (pooled BA.1 protection against reinfection 45.3%). Protection against severe disease (hospitalisation and death) was above 78% on average for all variants including BA.1, and at 40 weeks still 90.2% (69.7-97.5) for ancestral, alpha and delta and 88.9% (84.7-90.9) for BA.1. The summary: "The immunity conferred by past infection should be weighed alongside protection from vaccination." (Its Research in context adds that this protection was "at least as high, if not higher than that provided by two-dose vaccination using high-quality mRNA vaccines"; the brief keeps vaccines out of this unit, so the comparison is not used.) MISCONCEPTION: that immunity after infection is either useless or a permanent shield against catching the disease again; the measured pattern is that protection against catching it fades while protection against severe illness holds.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9998097/

## Lockdowns: the Herby-Jonung-Hanke review

F23. Jonas Herby, Lars Jonung and Steve Hanke (economists; Hanke at Johns Hopkins, Jonung at Lund University), "A Literature Review and Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Lockdowns on COVID-19 Mortality", Johns Hopkins Institute for Applied Economics, Studies in Applied Economics No. 200, January 2022 (21 January, per the IEA): stringency-index studies find that lockdowns in Europe and the United States "only reduced COVID-19 mortality by 0.2% on average"; shelter-in-place orders, 2.9%. It was not peer-reviewed; Johns Hopkins (Joshua Sharfstein) distanced itself from it.
    https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ris:jhisae:0200 ; https://sites.krieger.jhu.edu/iae/files/2022/01/A-Literature-Review-and-Meta-Analysis-of-the-Effects-of-Lockdowns-on-COVID-19-Mortality.pdf ; https://uk.news.yahoo.com/revised-report-impact-covid-lockdowns-200910160.html (The Guardian, Ian Sample and Linda Geddes, 5 June 2023)

F24. The revision: a revised version (Studies in Applied Economics No. 210, May 2022) dropped studies the authors no longer judged eligible and changed some calculations, raising the figure to 3.2%, "a 16-fold increase" (The Guardian). The book, Did Lockdowns Work? The Verdict on Covid Restrictions (Institute of Economic Affairs, 5 June 2023, a peer-reviewed IEA Perspectives volume), keeps it: lockdowns in spring 2020 reduced COVID-19 mortality by 3.2% "compared with less strict policies such as Sweden's", about 6,000 deaths avoided in Europe and 4,000 in the US; an estimate from specific interventions gives 10.7%. 19,646 potentially relevant studies were screened; the included studies were "based on actual, measured mortality data, not on results derived from modelling exercises" (IEA: 22 in the meta-analysis; the Guardian says 34 studies were included, about a third by economists; no count is used in the questions). The authors credit voluntary changes such as social distancing with "a significant role in mitigating the pandemic".
    https://iea.org.uk/?p=41107 ; https://uk.news.yahoo.com/revised-report-impact-covid-lockdowns-200910160.html ; https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ris:jhisae:0210

F25. What the figures compare: Joshua Sharfstein of Johns Hopkins noted that the study compared legally required interventions with voluntary ones, not lockdowns with doing nothing; the paper says "We do not look at the effect of voluntary behavioural change" (The Guardian). The IEA: 3.2% "compared with less strict policies such as Sweden's". The outcome pooled is COVID-19 mortality; the costs of lockdowns (the IEA release: harsher measures had high costs and negligible health benefits; a paraphrase, not a quotation) are argued in the text, not counted in the percentage. MISCONCEPTION: that the percentage compares lockdowns with no response at all.
    https://uk.news.yahoo.com/revised-report-impact-covid-lockdowns-200910160.html ; https://iea.org.uk/?p=41107

F26. The critics, steel-manned (Science Media Centre, 3 February 2022): Seth Flaxman (Oxford): "they systematically excluded from consideration any study based on the science of disease transmission"; later lockdowns are less effective than early ones "because many people are already infected". Samir Bhatt (Imperial): "a highly incomplete meta-analysis"; lockdown was defined as "the imposition of at least one compulsory, non-pharmaceutical intervention", which "would make a mask wearing policy a lockdown". Neil Ferguson (Imperial): effects on deaths "are delayed, in some cases by several weeks". David Paton (Nottingham, an economist): modelling studies "(like the well-known Flaxman Nature paper) are excluded", and "That is not controversial". In 2023 Adam Kucharski (LSHTM) said the book was largely the same as the 2022 preprint, so the earlier criticisms still applied.
    https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-a-preprint-looking-at-the-impact-of-lockdowns-as-posted-on-the-john-hopkins-krieger-school-of-arts-and-sciences-website/ ; https://uk.news.yahoo.com/revised-report-impact-covid-lockdowns-200910160.html

## Where the disease came from: who judged what, when, with what confidence

F27. In The Lancet in February 2020, 27 public-health scientists (the letter organised by Peter Daszak) condemned "conspiracy theories suggesting that COVID-19 does not have a natural origin".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory

F28. The reason a natural origin was the first expectation: "More than 60% of known infectious diseases in humans are zoonotic in origin", with "approximately 75% of emerging infectious diseases arising from animal reservoirs"; the SARS outbreak of 2002-2003 came to people through animals traded in wildlife markets, and MERS (2012) from camels.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Zoonosis

F29. The US intelligence community's assessment of August 2021: four agencies and the National Intelligence Council judged, with low confidence, that the first infection most likely came from natural exposure to an infected animal; one agency (later known to be the FBI) judged, with moderate confidence, that a laboratory-associated incident was most likely; three could not settle on either.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory ; https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory

F30. FBI director Christopher Wray confirmed the bureau's view publicly for the first time on 28 February 2023: the FBI had "for quite some time now assessed that the origins of the pandemic are most likely a potential lab incident in Wuhan". The same month the US Department of Energy, reported by the Wall Street Journal on 26 February 2023, judged a laboratory leak most likely, with low confidence.
    https://www.npr.org/2023/02/28/1160214557/fbi-covid-origins-investigation-wray ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory ; https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory

F31. The CIA, 25 January 2025: a research-related origin judged more likely than a natural one, "We have low confidence in this judgement"; it "continues to assess that both research-related and natural origin scenarios of the COVID-19 pandemic remain plausible". The judgement was a fresh analysis of existing intelligence, ordered under the Biden administration and completed before John Ratcliffe became director. Four other US agencies and the National Intelligence Council still judged a natural origin most likely.
    https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/1/27/cia-says-more-likely-covid-19-escaped-from-a-lab ; https://fortune.com/2025/01/26/cia-covid-19-pandemic-origin-theory-chinese-lab-low-confidence/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory

F32. The question is not settled. The declassified intelligence report of June 2023 found no evidence of the virus in a laboratory before the outbreak but could not rule a leak out; the WHO's SAGO group in June 2025 said a jump from animals has the "weight of available evidence" while the origin stays unresolved without more primary data and a laboratory incident stays "on the table"; a 2024 survey of 168 experts averaged 77% for a natural origin and 21% for a lab leak. Germany's foreign intelligence service (BND) put the chance of a laboratory accident at 80-90% in an unpublished 2020 report that German newspapers revealed in 2025 (Grokipedia and Wikipedia both say 80-90; the brief's quarry lead of 80-95 was not confirmed).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory ; https://grokipedia.com/page/COVID-19_lab_leak_theory

## Misconceptions a 9th-grader brings (for honest wrong choices)

- The declaration meant "let it spread through everyone" (F2: it called for shielding the old and vulnerable).
- Sweden's open schools meant no disruption at all (F12: sickness absence and short closures hit about a third of schools).
- Remote schooling was the only cause of the learning loss, or made no difference (F15, F16: losses were larger where schools stayed remote longer, and in-person districts lost ground too).
- The Cochrane review tested mask laws, or tested whether a mask protects its wearer (F19: it tested offering and promoting masks).
- Immunity after infection is either worthless or a permanent shield (F22).
- The lockdown percentage compares lockdowns with doing nothing (F25: with lighter, voluntary policies).
- The origin question is settled one way or the other (F29-F32).

## Conflicts and corrections

- Old bank (the-lockdown-meta-analysis-that-corrected-itself) dated the 3.2% figure to a 2024 Public Choice paper and claimed the revised headline moved "upward by more than an order of magnitude" in 2024. The record: the 3.2% first appeared in the May 2022 revised working paper and was kept in the June 2023 IEA book (F24). The brief says the book "revised the estimate"; the questions say the revised version "became a book in 2023", which is true either way.
- Old bank (the-country-that-kept-the-schools-open) gave teachers a relative risk of 0.43; the only summary I could read says teachers' risk was "similar to other occupations" (F10). Teachers are left out of the questions.
- Collins email's second recipient: Clifford Lane (most sources) or Eric Green (Grokipedia's Collins page). Only Fauci is named in the questions.
- Hallin's study covers reading only (Grokipedia says reading or mathematics). Only reading is used.
- BND: 80-90% (Grokipedia, Wikipedia), not 80-95%. Not used in the questions.
- Left out on purpose: the vaccine comparison in the Lancet review (the brief keeps vaccines out), any detail of the virus or of any laboratory, the furin site, Proximal Origin, Ferguson's modelling record, and the "crime" and "disaster" verdicts.

## Added at the revision (sources read 2026-11-27)

F36. The Lancet 2023 meta-analysis (F22), more of its figures: protection from past infection against reinfection with the ancestral, alpha and delta variants was 85.2% (60.8-96.0) at 4 weeks and 78.6% (49.8-93.6) at 40 weeks; "protection from re-infection from all variants wanes over time", and for omicron BA.1 it "declined more rapidly"; "protection against severe disease remained high for all variants", and appears durable beyond a year (on scarce data). The paper also studied BA.2 and BA.4/BA.5 (few studies): no 40-week severe-disease figure exists for them, and their 40-week reinfection figures were 37.0% and 27.7% (fact-check), so BA.1 was not the newest form studied. The 40-week severe-disease figures are 90.2% (ancestral, alpha, delta) and 88.9% (BA.1) only. So protection against catching it again fell for every form but did not reach zero, and protection against severe illness stayed high.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9998097/
