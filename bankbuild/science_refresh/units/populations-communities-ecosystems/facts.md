# Populations, Communities and Ecosystems: fact sheet (T2, 6th grade, life strand)

Researched 2026-11-26. Grokipedia first; OpenStax, National Geographic Education and a TEKS 6.12C teaching guide to corroborate.
Neighbours not re-asked here: ecosystems-living-and-nonliving (T1: what an ecosystem is, biotic and abiotic factors, habitat); competing-for-resources and predators-partners-and-parasites (T2: competition, limiting factors, carrying capacity, predation, symbiosis); cells-the-building-blocks-of-life (T2: cell to tissue, organ, organ system, organism). This unit owns the LEVELS of ecological organisation, what sets each apart, and sorting real cases into them.

## The levels

F1 An organism is one individual living thing, such as an animal, a plant, a bacterium or a fungus; it may be one cell or many. In a forest, each tree is an organism. https://grokipedia.com/page/Organism ("a living entity, such as an animal, plant, bacterium, or fungus"; "a contiguous living system composed of one or more cells"); https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("Organisms are individual living entities"; "each tree in a forest is an organism")

F2 A population is all the individuals of ONE species living in the same area at the same time; its members interact and can potentially interbreed. https://grokipedia.com/page/Population_ecology ("assemblages of organisms of a single species occupying the same geographic area at the same time"; "a group of individuals of the same species that interact and potentially interbreed within a defined area"); https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("all the individuals of a species living within a specific area a population"; the pine trees of a forest as its example); https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en ("a group of organisms of the same species living in the same area at the same time")

F3 A population counts its members of every age and life stage: ecologists describe its age structure, the share of its individuals at each life stage. https://grokipedia.com/page/Population_ecology ("age structure refers to the proportion of individuals within a population that belong to specific age classes"; "the proportion of individuals at different life stages")

F4 A tadpole is the young (larval) stage of a frog or toad: it lives in water, breathes with gills and has no legs at first; in metamorphosis its tail is resorbed, legs grow and lungs replace the gills. https://grokipedia.com/page/Tadpole ("the aquatic larval stage of frogs and toads"; "external or internal gills for respiration"; the tail is "resorbed, limbs emerge, gills are replaced by lungs")

F5 American bullfrogs lay their eggs in shallow water, and their tadpole stage lasts about one to three years. https://grokipedia.com/page/American_bullfrog ("clutches of 10,000 to 20,000 eggs in shallow waters"; the tadpole stage "lasts 1–3 years depending on environmental factors")

F6 A community is all the interacting populations of two or more species in one area at one time: plants, animals, fungi and microbes alike. It is living things only; the nonliving parts belong to the ecosystem. https://grokipedia.com/page/Community_(ecology) ("an assemblage of interacting populations of two or more species occupying a defined geographic area at a given time"; "such as plants, animals, fungi, and microbes"; "excluding abiotic components that characterize broader ecosystems"); https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("A community is the sum of populations inhabiting a particular area"; "all of the trees, flowers, insects, and other populations in a forest form the forest's community"); https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en ("A community is all the different populations of living things that share an area")

F7 An ecosystem is the community together with its nonliving (abiotic) surroundings, such as sunlight, water, air, soil and temperature. https://grokipedia.com/page/Ecosystem ("a biological community of interacting organisms and their surrounding physical environment"); https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("all the living things in a particular area together with the abiotic, nonliving parts of that environment such as nitrogen in the soil or rain water"; "The forest itself is an ecosystem"); https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en ("the community PLUS the nonliving factors (sunlight, water, air, soil, temperature)")

F8 The biosphere is all of Earth's ecosystems together: the part of Earth where life exists, on land, in water and in the air. It is the highest level. https://grokipedia.com/page/Biosphere ("the global sum of all ecosystems on Earth"); https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("the biosphere is the collection of all ecosystems, and it represents the zones of life on Earth"); https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/biosphere/ ("part of the Earth where life exists")

F9 The biosphere reaches from about 8 km above Earth's surface (airborne microbes have been found near 10 km up) to the floor of the deepest ocean trench, about 11 km down: the Challenger Deep, 10,935 m, where amphipods, microbial mats and pressure-loving bacteria have been found. https://grokipedia.com/page/Biosphere ("extends vertically from approximately 8 kilometers above Earth's surface" to about 11 km below sea level; airborne microbes "near 10 kilometers altitude"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Mariana_Trench ("the maximum verified depth measured at 10,935 meters"; a seafloor "populated by small amphipods"; "diverse microbial mats"; Moritella bacteria from Kaiko samples); https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/biosphere/ (from "the dark environment of ocean trenches" to "high mountaintops", about 20 km top to bottom)

F10 The levels nest, smallest to largest: organism, population, community, ecosystem, biosphere. Each higher level is built from the ones below it: communities contain populations, ecosystems combine communities with abiotic factors, and the biosphere is the sum of all ecosystems. https://grokipedia.com/page/Biological_organisation ("entities at higher levels of complexity are composed of and emerge from those at lower levels"; population, community, ecosystem, biosphere in that order); https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology

F11 A step up the levels adds more kinds of living things, not necessarily more ground: "A whole community fits under one rotting log: pill bugs, beetles, ants, fungi, and a salamander, all in about a square foot." Two squirrels in one yard are one population. https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en ("Each step up adds more variety, not necessarily more territory"; "Going from organism to population to community is about how many different kinds of living things you are counting"; "Two squirrels in one yard are one population")

F12 Rotting logs are home to many kinds of living things: fungi, beetles and other insects, mosses and bacteria. https://grokipedia.com/page/Coarse_woody_debris ("saproxylic organisms, including insects, fungi, birds, mammals"; saproxylic beetles "colonize decaying wood to feed on fungi"; "Bacterial communities also contribute by colonizing wood surfaces"; "microsites ideal for ferns, mosses")

F13 Earth's spheres: the atmosphere is the envelope of gases around Earth; the hydrosphere is all of Earth's water; the geosphere is the solid Earth; the biosphere is all living things and their interactions with their surroundings. https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth_system_science ("The atmosphere is the gaseous envelope surrounding Earth"; "The hydrosphere encompasses all water on Earth"; "The geosphere comprises the solid Earth"; "The biosphere includes all living organisms and their interactions with the environment")

## The biosphere and other worlds

F14 No life has been found beyond Earth: Earth is the only place in the known universe known to support life. Possible biospheres on Mars, Europa or Enceladus are unconfirmed. https://grokipedia.com/page/Extraterrestrial_life ("no extraterrestrial life—microbial, complex, or intelligent—has been verified through direct sampling"; NASA: "no life beyond Earth has been found"); https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/biosphere/ ("The Earth is the only place in the known universe that supports life"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Biosphere (other biospheres discussed, none confirmed)

F15 Other worlds have water: Mars's north polar cap is mostly water ice, and the Phoenix lander dug up ice-cemented soil a few centimetres below the surface. https://grokipedia.com/page/Water_on_Mars ("comprises layered deposits dominated by water ice"; "water ice-cemented soil just 5 to 18 centimeters below the surface")

F16 Other worlds have atmospheres and clouds: Mars has a thin atmosphere of mostly carbon dioxide; Venus's atmosphere is 96.5% carbon dioxide, with opaque clouds of sulfuric acid about 48 to 70 km up. https://grokipedia.com/page/Atmosphere_of_Mars ("an extremely thin gaseous envelope surrounding the planet, dominated by carbon dioxide"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Atmosphere_of_Venus ("consists predominantly of carbon dioxide at 96.5% by volume"; sulfuric acid clouds between about 48 and 70 km)

## Ecosystems at work, and people

F17 Plants take up nitrogen, a nutrient, through their roots; bacteria and fungi decomposing dead leaves and other remains return the nitrogen to the soil for plants to use again. https://grokipedia.com/page/Nitrogen_cycle ("which plants assimilate primarily through roots"; "Ammonification ... occurs during decomposition of biomass by heterotrophic bacteria and fungi"; "Microbial decomposition of plant litter ... converting organic N back to ammonium"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Ecosystem (decomposers release "nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus back into the soil or water for reuse by producers")

F18 Nutrient cycling and energy flow, where living and nonliving parts act on each other, are studied at the ecosystem level. https://grokipedia.com/page/Biological_organisation (ecosystems are "dynamic systems where biotic and abiotic components interact through energy flow and nutrient cycling"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Ecosystem

F19 People are part of ecosystems: a town's or city's ecosystem includes plants, animals, microorganisms and humans, and human populations are among its living (biotic) parts. https://grokipedia.com/page/Urban_ecosystem ("biological organisms—including plants, animals, microorganisms, and humans"; "biotic components such as human populations, wildlife species, vegetation, and microorganisms")

## The cases used

F20 The sugar maple (Acer saccharum) is one species; it grows in mixed hardwood forests of eastern North America alongside American beech and yellow birch. https://grokipedia.com/page/Acer_saccharum ("Acer saccharum"; "often found in mixed hardwood forests alongside species like American beech and yellow birch")

F21 The eastern gray squirrel (Sciurus carolinensis) is one species of tree squirrel, native to the hardwood forests of eastern North America. https://grokipedia.com/page/Eastern_gray_squirrel ("a species of tree squirrel"; "native to the deciduous hardwood forests of eastern and central North America")

F22 A pond's living things include algae, rooted water plants, bacteria that decompose dead matter (many in the bottom mud), insects, amphibians and fish; cattails grow in ponds and marshes. Sunlight lights and warms a pond's water. https://grokipedia.com/page/Pond ("benthic algae and sediment-rooted macrophytes as primary producers"; "bacterial decomposition"; "breeding grounds for amphibians, insects, and fish"; "solar heating"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Typha (cattails "thrive in fresh to slightly brackish waters such as marshes, ponds, and riverbanks"); https://keslerscience.com/teks-5.12a-biotic-abiotic-interactions?hsLang=en (its pond example counts the bacteria in the mud as biotic)

F23 Grade: TEKS 6.12C has 6th graders "describe the hierarchical organization of organism, population, and community within an ecosystem"; the older Texas standard (6.12F) had them diagram organism, population, community and ecosystem, and Kesler's guide says the old version ran on to biome and biosphere. https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en ; https://texasgateway.org/teks/s612c-0/alignments

## Misconceptions (honest wrong choices)

M1 "A population is any group of animals living together"; in fact it is one species in one area. https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en

M2 "A community and an ecosystem are the same thing"; in fact the community is the living things only. https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en

M3 "Each step up the hierarchy means a bigger patch of ground"; in fact each step adds variety, not territory (F11). https://keslerscience.com/teks-6.12c-hierarchy-of-ecosystems?hsLang=en

M4 Students leave people out of ecosystems: a teacher reports her students "rarely ... include themselves or other humans in their representations of an ecosystem" and "do not see themselves as part of the ecosystem they live within"; undergraduate studies find the same "human exceptionalism". https://teachers.yale.edu/curriculum/units/2020/5/6/2 ; https://cos.northeastern.edu/human-exceptionalism-hinders-environmental-action-northeastern-scientists-find

M5 Living things in soil or mud are lumped with the "dirt": "Plants are biotic, but the soil they grow in is just dirt" is a listed misconception, and bacteria are easy to forget as living members of a community. https://keslerscience.com/teks-5.12a-biotic-abiotic-interactions?hsLang=en

M6 In everyday speech a community is a group of people (Grokipedia: "a social aggregation of individuals linked by recurrent interactions"), so a town's people are called "the community"; in ecology a community always holds two or more species. https://grokipedia.com/page/Community ; F6

M7 Author's judgement: because a tadpole looks and breathes so unlike a frog (gills, no legs, F4), a student may leave the young out, or count them only once they look like frogs (with legs); Aristotle described early frogs as "small, fish-like creatures with tails" (Grokipedia, Tadpole). They are the same species, so the same population (F2, F3).

M8 Author's judgement: students may think water, air or clouds are found only on Earth, and so that other worlds lack a biosphere for want of them; in fact Mars has water ice and Mars and Venus have atmospheres, Venus with thick clouds (F15, F16). What no other world is known to have is life (F14).

## Added in the revision (2026-11-26; read on the web this day)

F24. Life reaches tens of kilometres up, not just 10 km: an ISRO balloon cryosampler flown from Hyderabad collected air from 20 to 41 km up, and 12 bacterial and six fungal colonies grew from it, three of them new species (ScienceDaily, 2009); Wikipedia Biosphere: life is found "at least 64 km (40 mi) high in the atmosphere". Grokipedia Biosphere gives microbes "as high as 10 kilometers", so 10 km is a height where microbes are found, not the top of life's reach.
    https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/03/090318094642.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Biosphere

F25. Amphipods are small crustaceans of "shrimp-like" form, most under 20 mm long, found "from coastal zones to the deepest trenches" (Grokipedia Amphipoda); the floor of the Challenger Deep, about 11 km down, is "populated by small amphipods" (F9).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Amphipoda ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mariana_Trench

F26. A biome is a "large ecological community of plants, animals, and microorganisms adapted to a dominant climate regime", such as deserts, tundra or tropical rainforests; biomes are "aggregations of such ecosystems unified by overarching climatic envelopes" (Grokipedia Biome), and "The scope of the biosphere includes diverse biomes" (Grokipedia Biosphere). So a biome is one great region of similar ecosystems, not all of Earth's ecosystems.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Biome ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Biosphere

F27. Where one population ends: a population is "a group of individuals of the same species that interact and potentially interbreed within a defined area" (Grokipedia Population ecology), and groups of one species in separate habitat patches are "spatially discrete subpopulations of the same species occupying separate habitat patches" (Grokipedia Metapopulation). Two ponds whose frogs never cross between them hold two populations, each counting its young (F3).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Population_ecology ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Metapopulation

F28. The organism level: ecology runs "from individual organisms and their physiological responses" up through populations, communities and ecosystems, and "The biosphere represents the broadest scale" (Grokipedia Ecology). The red fox (Vulpes vulpes) is one species, "the most widely distributed wild carnivore", active through the winter, when it hunts rodents "under snow cover up to 1 meter deep" (Grokipedia Red fox).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ecology ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Red_fox

F29. Correction to F11: only the step from population to community adds more kinds of living things. The step from organism to population adds more members of the same species, the step from community to ecosystem adds the nonliving surroundings, and the biosphere, all of Earth's ecosystems, does add ground (fact-check of 2026-11-26, against OpenStax Biology 2e 1.2).
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology

F30. A community holds every kind of living thing in its area, "plants, animals, fungi, and microbes", and leaves out the nonliving parts (F6). So a list of a pond's fish, frogs, insects and birds leaves out its plants, algae and bacteria; its water, mud and sunlight belong only to its ecosystem (F7, F22); and people living in a town some way off are not in the pond's area at all (F2).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Community_(ecology) ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology
