# Born Knowing or Learned: fact sheet (T1, 5th grade)

Researched 2026-11-26. Grokipedia first; Wikipedia, CDC, NPS, Florida FWC and others to corroborate.

## Facts

F1 An instinct is a behaviour a living thing does without having learned it or met it before: it is inborn, and it takes the same fixed form in every member of a species. https://grokipedia.com/page/Instinct ("an innate, complex pattern of behavior"; "performed without prior learning or experience"; "follows a fixed, highly consistent form across individuals of the same species"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instinct ("Any behaviour is instinctive if it is performed without being based upon prior experience")

F2 Instincts help a living thing survive: they evolved because they raise its chance of surviving and having young. Texas's 5th-grade standard asks students to explain how instinctual and learned behaviours both increase the chance of survival. https://grokipedia.com/page/Instinct ("having evolved to enhance survival and reproductive success"); https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/19-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-112-7 (TEKS 5.13B: "instinctual behavioral traits such as turtle hatchlings returning to the sea" and "learned behavioral traits such as orcas hunting in packs increase chances of survival")

F3 People have instincts too. A newborn's primitive reflexes, such as rooting (turning toward a touch on the cheek) and sucking, are present at birth and help the baby feed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instinct ("Examples of instinctive behaviours in humans include many of the primitive reflexes, such as rooting and suckling"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Primitive_reflexes (rooting "is present at birth" and "facilitates breastfeeding"; sucking is "fully functional at birth")

F4 The palmar grasp reflex: stroking or placing something in a newborn's palm, such as a finger, makes her fingers curl round it. It is fully present at birth, purely automatic, and fades between about 4 and 6 months of age. https://grokipedia.com/page/Palmar_grasp_reflex ("elicited by stroking or placing an object in the palm of the hand"; "fully present at birth in healthy term infants"; "purely reflexive and automatic at birth"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Primitive_reflexes ("typically integrates and disappears between 4 and 6 months of age")

F5 A newborn cannot yet reach out for a toy, lift her arms to be picked up or wave bye-bye. The grasp reflex gives way to voluntary reaching at 4 to 6 months; the CDC lists "Reaches to grab a toy she wants" at 6 months, "Lifts arms up to be picked up" at 9 months and "Waves bye-bye" at 1 year, and tells parents to teach a baby to wave bye-bye. https://grokipedia.com/page/Instinct ("the grasping reflex in newborns evolves into voluntary reaching by 4-6 months"); https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/6-months.html; https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/9-months.html ("Teach your baby to wave 'bye-bye'"); https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html

F6 Spinning a web is an instinct: newly hatched spiders build working webs without ever watching one being built and without practice; orb-weavers follow an inborn set of building rules, and young orb-weavers spin webs as complex as adults'. (Caveat: in one study, spiderlings raised alone at first laid silk with no clear shape before building their kind's web, so the stems avoid "on the first try" and "perfect".) https://grokipedia.com/page/Instinct ("Newly emerged spiders construct functional webs without observation or practice"); https://www.ipm.org/show/amomentofscience/2022-06-22/how-orb-weaving-spiders-build-their-webs ("guided by an innate set of rules"); https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/842806 ("Baby orb weaver spiders can weave as complex webs as adults", PLOS One, 2021)

F7 The European garden spider is an orb-weaver that builds large, wheel-shaped webs. The female lays her eggs in late autumn and dies soon after; the young hatch in spring, scatter on silk threads in the wind, and set up webs of their own. https://grokipedia.com/page/Araneus_diadematus ("commonly known as the European garden spider"; "constructing large, wheel-shaped webs"; female "dies shortly after oviposition"; "Hatching occurs in spring"; juveniles "disperse to establish individual webs")

F8 Sea-turtle hatchlings dig out of the nest mostly at night and head for the sea by instinct: they have an inborn tendency to move toward the brightest direction, which on a natural beach is the night sky over the ocean. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instinct ("Sea turtles, newly hatched on a beach, will instinctively move toward the ocean"); https://myfwc.com/research/wildlife/sea-turtles/threats/artificial-lighting/ ("Sea turtle hatchlings have an inborn tendency to move in the brightest direction"; they "emerge primarily at night"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Sea_turtle ("often at night"; "crawling toward the brightest horizon reflecting moonlight or starlight over the ocean")

F9 No adult turtle is there to teach the hatchlings: the mother comes ashore at night, digs a nest, lays her eggs, covers them with sand and returns to the sea, leaving the nest untended. https://home.nps.gov/drto/learn/nature/seaturtleslight.htm ("returns to the sea"; "leaving the nest untended")

F10 Because the instinct is fixed, artificial lights near a beach lead hatchlings astray: a hatchling crawling toward a lamp is following the same instinct that leads it seaward on a dark beach. https://myfwc.com/research/wildlife/sea-turtles/threats/artificial-lighting/ ("following the same instinctive response that leads them seaward"); https://home.nps.gov/drto/learn/nature/seaturtleslight.htm ("disorient hatchling sea turtles, leading them astray")

F11 A learned behaviour comes from experience: learning is a lasting change in behaviour that results from experience, including watching others and the rewards that follow an action. https://grokipedia.com/page/Learning ("a relatively permanent change in an organism's behavior or knowledge that results from experience"; observational learning is acquiring "new behaviors by observing others"; "Positive reinforcement adds a desirable stimulus")

F12 Learned behaviours can change to fit new or changing conditions, while instincts are rigid. https://grokipedia.com/page/Learning (learned behaviours "offer flexibility for coping with variable or novel conditions," while "innate actions are efficient and low-risk but rigid")

F13 Dogs learn tricks and commands, such as sitting and giving a paw to shake, through rewards of treats or praise. https://grokipedia.com/page/Dog_training ("Dogs of any age can learn this trick with positive reinforcement" (the "give paw" trick); "reward-based protocols using treats or praise"; "receiving food for sitting on command")

F14 A reflex is an automatic, inborn response that often protects the body; the withdrawal reflex pulls a hand away from a hot surface without any thought. https://grokipedia.com/page/Reflex ("an involuntary, rapid, and stereotypical response to a stimulus"; "the withdrawal reflex pulling a hand from a hot surface"; reflexes often serve "to protect the organism"; "innate (unlearned) reflexes"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Withdrawal_reflex ("serves a fundamental protective role")

F15 A sneeze is a protective reflex that clears dust and other irritants from the nose. https://grokipedia.com/page/Sneeze ("a protective, semi-autonomous reflex that expels irritants from the nasal passages"; irritants "include dust, pollen, smoke")

F16 A cough is a protective reflex that clears the airways of irritants and bits of foreign matter. https://grokipedia.com/page/Cough ("serving as a protective reflex mechanism to clear the airways of irritants"; "foreign particles")

F17 Looking both ways before crossing a street is taught: children are taught to look left, right and then left again, and children younger than 10 should not cross streets alone. https://www.chop.edu/centers-programs/injury-prevention-program/pedestrian-safety ("Teach children to look LEFT, RIGHT and then LEFT again before crossing a street"; "Children younger than 10 years should not cross streets by themselves")

F18 Blue tits in Britain learned to peck through the foil caps of milk bottles left on doorsteps and drink the cream. The habit was first reported in 1921 near Southampton and spread to over 200 places by 1947, passed from bird to bird by learning; it faded as doorstep milk delivery declined. https://grokipedia.com/page/Eurasian_blue_tit ("first reported in 1921 near Southampton, where birds learned to peck through foil caps to access cream"; "diffusion from initial sites to over 200 locations by 1947"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_blue_tit ("blue tits teaching one another to open traditional British milk bottles with foil tops"; "culturally transmitted learning")

F19 Eye colour is an inherited trait, set by genes (chiefly OCA2 and HERC2) passed down from parents. https://grokipedia.com/page/Eye_color ("Human eye color is a polygenic trait influenced by multiple genes"; "Eye color exhibits a polygenic inheritance pattern")

F20 Things gained during life, such as scars, injuries, a bodybuilder's muscles and skills ("the ability to do something well"), are acquired characteristics, and they are not passed on to children. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquired_characteristic ("a non-heritable change in a function or structure of a living organism caused after birth"; "The muscles acquired by a bodybuilder"; "scars"; "The ability to do something well is an acquired characteristic"; "They are not passed on to offspring through reproduction"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Acquired_characteristic ("scar tissue from wounds"; "muscle hypertrophy from repeated exercise"; changes that "do not propagate to offspring")

F21 The language a person speaks is learned, not inherited: babies can learn any language, and children adopted into a family that speaks another language learn the new one and usually lose their birth language. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_acquisition ("human infants have the ability to learn any language"); https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/internationally-adopted-children-shed-light-on-how-babies-learn-language.html ("these older children lose their birth language rapidly and become fluent speakers of their new language")

F22 An old idea held that changes gained in life could be handed down, and its classic example was the strong forearms of blacksmiths, built by years of hammering. The idea was rejected: in August Weismann's experiment, mice whose tails were cut off never had young with short tails. https://grokipedia.com/page/Lamarckism ("the robust forearms of blacksmiths, developed through habitual hammering"; "no offspring exhibited shortened tails or taillessness")

F23 A child inherits genes from both parents, one copy of each gene from the mother and one from the father. https://grokipedia.com/page/Heredity ("inherit two alleles per gene—one from each parent")

F24 Inherited traits versus learned behaviours is 5th-grade content: Texas's 2017 grade-5 standard asked students to "differentiate between inherited traits" and "learned behaviors such as an animal learning tricks or a child riding a bicycle"; NGSS says some traits "result from individuals' interactions with the environment, which can range from diet to learning." https://shmoop.com/teks/science/elementary/5th-grade/TEKS-112-16-b-10/TEKS-112-16-b-10-B (TEKS 112.16(b)(10)(B)); https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/3-ls3-2-heredity-inheritance-and-variation-traits (DCI LS3.A)

## Misconceptions (for honest wrong choices)

M1 Students often think that changes gained in a lifetime (muscles, injuries, skills) can be passed on to children ("Lamarckian" reasoning); in one study about a third of students held that traits developed over a lifetime are inherited. https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/106669/1/cogs12097.pdf (Cognitive Science paper, read through a search excerpt only); https://www.lifescied.org/doi/suppl/10.1187/cbe.12-06-0078/suppl_file/combinedsupmats2.pdf (CBE Life Sciences Education supplement, figure read through a search excerpt only)

M2 Children tend to assume a young animal learns its first skills from its mother (the spider's web, the hatchling's crawl to the sea). Author's judgement from the facts above (F6, F7, F9), not a separately sourced survey.

M3 A learned skill that has become automatic (riding a bicycle, a dog's trick) is easily mistaken for an instinct. Author's judgement; used only in contexts, not keyed.

## Lane notes
- Each species appears only as an example of "inborn or learned, and how it helps" (TEKS 5.13B's lane note). No question asks a species' own anatomy or behaviour for its own sake.
- Kept apart from adaptations-built-for-a-place: no body-part adaptations (webbed feet, blubber, beaks, camouflage, cactus stems). The inherited side here is a person's traits (eye colour) against things gained in life (a scar, a skill, muscle, a language).

## Added in the revision (2026-11-26)

F25 Sea-turtle eggs take about two months to hatch (45 to 70 days by species, about 60 on average for loggerheads and greens), and by then the mother is long gone: she covers the nest and returns to the sea (F9). Sea turtles give no parental care: the hatchlings never know their parents and fend for themselves from the moment they reach the beach. https://grokipedia.com/page/Sea_turtle ("typically ranging from 45 to 70 days depending on species"; incubation "averages around 60 days under natural conditions"; "hatchlings synchronize emergence, often at night"); https://www.pbs.org/wnet/nature/baby-tales-home-alone/1994 (PBS Nature: "Sea turtles never know their parents"; "they must fend for themselves once they break out of their leathery eggs and scramble down the beach to the sea"); https://home.nps.gov/drto/learn/nature/seaturtleslight.htm ("returns to the sea, leaving the nest untended")

F26 The road-crossing drill depends on which side traffic drives on: the US drill is left, right, then left again (F17), while Britain's old Kerb Drill was "Eyes right, eyes left, eyes right again" and today's Green Cross Code says "Look all around for traffic and listen". So the context says only that children are taught to look both ways, and then again. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Cross_Code

F27 Riding a bicycle is a skill (procedural) memory: it is acquired gradually through repeated practice, then performed "effortlessly without requiring conscious attention", and it lasts a long time "with minimal decay even without regular practice". https://grokipedia.com/page/Procedural_memory ("riding a bicycle"; "typically acquired through repetition"; "can be performed effortlessly without requiring conscious attention"; "persisting over extended periods with minimal decay even without regular practice"). With F1 (an instinct is inborn), F20 (a skill is not passed on to children) and F24 (TEKS names a child riding a bicycle as a learned behaviour).

F28 The blue tit is a small bird, and the bottles' cream sat at the top: cream "naturally rises to the top of unhomogenized milk". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_blue_tit ("a small passerine bird in the tit family"; "to open traditional British milk bottles with foil tops to access the cream underneath"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Cream ("the higher-fat layer that naturally rises to the top of unhomogenized milk")

F29 A learned behaviour is not inherited, though it can spread by teaching and copying (puppies learn by watching their mother or a person, per the fact-check's source), so the dog's context says a trick is not inherited and each puppy must learn it for itself, not that learned behaviour is never passed on. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquired_characteristic ("They are not passed on to offspring through reproduction"); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6033932 (Fugazza et al. 2018, Scientific Reports, cited by the fact-check)
