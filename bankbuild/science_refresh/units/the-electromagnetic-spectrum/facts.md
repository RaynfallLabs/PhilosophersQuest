## The family of waves

F1. The electromagnetic spectrum's bands, from longest wavelength (lowest frequency) to shortest (highest frequency): radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays (Grokipedia: "radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays"). The curriculum map line gives the same order "from low to high frequency".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum ; bankbuild/science_refresh/curriculum_map.json (G8.P7)

F2. All electromagnetic waves "propagate through space at the constant speed of light in vacuum" (Grokipedia), and they need no medium: "EM waves don't need a medium in which to propagate; they can travel through a vacuum, such as outer space" (OpenStax). Radio waves "propagate through free space at the speed of light" (Grokipedia, Radio wave).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum ; https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-introduction-to-electromagnetic-waves ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Radio_wave

F3. Higher frequency means more energy: "Photon energy increases with frequency (E = hf)" (Grokipedia). Ultraviolet photons "have more energy than visible light" (Wikipedia, Ultraviolet), and gamma rays are "the most energetic and penetrating type in the electromagnetic spectrum" (Grokipedia, Gamma ray).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Gamma_ray

F4. Ionizing radiation (X-rays, gamma rays and the shortest ultraviolet) "can ionize atoms by removing electrons"; radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light and most ultraviolet are non-ionizing (Grokipedia). NASA: the highest-energy waves "have such a high energy that they can knock electrons out of atoms".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum ; https://science.nasa.gov/ems/01_intro/

## Colour

F5. Visible light runs from violet at the short end (around 400 nm) to red at the long end (around 700 nm) (Grokipedia, Electromagnetic spectrum). Frequencies run from about 430 THz for red to 790 THz for violet, and "Each specific wavelength corresponds to a distinct hue" (Grokipedia, Visible spectrum: 450 nm blue, 550 nm green). Wikipedia's table: violet 380-450 nm, blue 450-485, green 500-565, yellow 565-590, orange 590-625, red 625-750 nm. So light of about 650 nm is red.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Visible_spectrum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_spectrum

## How the invisible bands were found

F6. William Herschel, 1800: he wanted to learn "how much heat passed through the different colored filters he used to observe the Sun" (IPAC). He spread sunlight with a glass prism and used thermometers with blackened bulbs "to better absorb the heat"; for each colour one bulb sat in the colour and "the other two were placed beyond the spectrum as control samples" (IPAC; Linda Hall: controls "registering room temperature"). Every colour read warmer than the controls, and "the temperature of the colors increased from the violet to the red part of the spectrum". Just beyond the red, "in a region apparently devoid of sunlight", he found "the highest temperature of all" (IPAC; Grokipedia: highest "beyond the visible red band, where no light was apparent").
    https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/Outreach/Edu/Herschel/herschel_bio.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/William_Herschel ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Infrared ; https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/william-herschel-4/

F7. Herschel called them "calorific rays" (Grokipedia, William Herschel; Wikipedia, Infrared) and published in 1800. They "were reflected, refracted, absorbed and transmitted just like visible light" (IPAC); Grokipedia: "subject to the same laws of reflection and refraction as light". "The term "infrared" did not appear until the late 19th century" (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/William_Herschel ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared ; https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/Outreach/Edu/Herschel/herschel_bio.html

F8. Johann Wilhelm Ritter, 1801: "motivated by William Herschel's recent discovery of infrared radiation", he passed sunlight through a prism in a darkened room and laid strips of silver chloride paper along the spectrum, including regions beyond the visible colours. The silver chloride blackened most strongly in the violet of the visible colours, weakening toward red, and most of all just past the violet, where no light showed. He called them "chemical rays"; he announced it on 22 February 1801 (Grokipedia). Wikipedia: "invisible rays just beyond the violet end of the visible spectrum darkened silver chloride-soaked paper more quickly than violet light itself"; "ultraviolet" means beyond violet.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Johann_Wilhelm_Ritter ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum

F9. Wilhelm Röntgen, 8 November 1895, University of Würzburg: he had "enclosed the tube in a thick black cardboard shield", and a screen "coated with barium platinocyanide" glowed (Grokipedia; Wikipedia: "a faint shimmering from a bench a few feet away"). His paper: the fluorescence "is still visible at two metres distance"; the screen "will light up when placed behind a book of a thousand pages"; "Boards of pine two or three centimetres thick absorb only very little"; "Lead 1.5 mm thick is practically opaque"; "The density of the bodies is the property whose variation mainly affects their permeability". He named them X-rays for the unknown (Grokipedia: "Recognizing the rays' unknown nature"; paper: "as I will call the rays, for the sake of brevity"). Paper "On a New Kind of Rays", 28 December 1895.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Wilhelm_R%C3%B6ntgen ; https://web.lemoyne.edu/~giunta/roentgen.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_R%C3%B6ntgen

F10. Röntgen's paper: with a hand before the screen "the shadow shows the bones clearly with only faint outlines of the surrounding tissues". On 22 December 1895 he X-rayed his wife Anna Bertha's hand, a plate exposed "for about 15 minutes" that showed the bones and "her wedding ring" (Grokipedia; Wikipedia: Anna Bertha Ludwig, "About six weeks after his discovery"). Bone absorbs more X-rays than soft tissue: "denser materials, like bone, absorb more X-rays than softer tissues, such as muscle" (Grokipedia, Radiography).
    https://web.lemoyne.edu/~giunta/roentgen.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Wilhelm_R%C3%B6ntgen ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_R%C3%B6ntgen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiography

F11. X-rays travel in straight lines (Röntgen's pin-hole photograph showed "the rectilinear propagation of the rays"); prisms of water and carbon disulphide "showed no deviation", and lenses cannot concentrate them. A magnet did not bend them: "I have not succeeded in observing any deviation of the X-rays even in very strong magnetic fields."
    https://web.lemoyne.edu/~giunta/roentgen.html

F12. Heinrich Hertz, Karlsruhe, 1886-1888 (first radio waves 1887: Grokipedia, Radio wave and Electromagnetic spectrum). His transmitter made sparks "across a small gap between two polished spheres or rods"; his receiver was "a resonant loop antenna with its own adjustable spark gap", where the waves caused "visible micro-sparks" (Grokipedia), at distances up to about 12 m (Grokipedia), 18 m (IEEE). He reflected the waves from a zinc plate about 12 m away to make standing waves (Wikipedia), which "made it possible to measure the wave length and therewith the velocity of wave propagation" (IEEE). He showed "the equality of their velocity of propagation with the velocity of light" and "observed the reflection, refraction and polarization of the waves" (IEEE plaque; Wikipedia: "the velocity of these waves was equal to the velocity of light"), verifying Maxwell's prediction (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Heinrich_Hertz ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Hertz ; https://ethw.org/Milestones%3AFirst_Generation_and_Experimental_Proof_of_Electromagnetic_Waves%2C_1886-1888 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Radio_wave

## The bands at work

F13. Radio waves have "the longest wavelengths and lowest frequencies in the electromagnetic spectrum" (about 100 km to 1 mm) and carry "AM and FM radio broadcasting", "cellular networks, Wi-Fi, and GPS navigation"; an antenna turns them back "into an electrical signal" (Grokipedia, Radio wave). A mobile phone connects to its network "via radio frequency signals", with areas divided "into cells served by base stations" (Grokipedia, Mobile phone).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radio_wave ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mobile_phone

F14. Radio waves are not sound. OpenStax: "Many people confuse sound waves with radio waves, one type of electromagnetic (EM) wave. However, sound and radio waves are completely different phenomena"; "radio waves are electromagnetic waves, like visible light"; a car radio decodes the waves and "uses a speaker to change it back into a sound wave". Sound needs a medium (F2).
    https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-introduction-to-electromagnetic-waves

F15. A microwave oven's magnetron makes microwaves at 2.45 GHz, "where the wavelength is about 12.2 cm". The oscillating field "causes these polar molecules [water] to rotate rapidly in an attempt to align with the field", and the motion gives "intermolecular friction that generates heat". The metal walls "reflect the waves repeatedly, creating a Faraday cage"; glass and ceramics let "microwaves pass through with minimal absorption". Microwave radiation is non-ionizing: it "lacks the energy to break chemical bonds or damage DNA directly and does not make food radioactive". Ice absorbs far less than liquid water.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Microwave_oven ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum

F16. Infrared "is emitted by all objects with temperatures above absolute zero"; a human body emits mostly around 9-10 micrometres (Grokipedia: peak about 9.3 μm; Wikipedia: "around 10 μm"). NASA: night-vision goggles or infrared cameras let people see "the infrared waves emitting from warm objects such as humans and animals"; "humans ... only emit infrared waves" (not visible light). Remote controls use infrared LEDs, "around 940 nm" (Grokipedia, Electromagnetic spectrum; Wikipedia: "Infrared is the most common way for remote controls to command appliances"; NASA: a remote "uses light waves just beyond the visible spectrum").
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Infrared ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared ; https://science.nasa.gov/ems/07_infraredwaves/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum

F17. A thermal camera's thermography captures "the natural infrared radiation emitted by objects and surfaces due to their temperature", works "in total darkness", and finds survivors "amid smoke, rubble, or dense foliage"; "total energy radiated is directly proportional to the fourth power of the absolute temperature", so warmer things give off far more. It "differs fundamentally from night-vision technologies that employ image intensification", in which "ambient visible and near-infrared light is amplified", and which fails "in complete darkness or through heat-obscuring media like smoke". A separate active method shines "an external infrared illumination source" on the target (Grokipedia, Thermography; Grokipedia, Infrared: active night vision uses near-infrared illumination).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Thermography ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Infrared

F18. Ultraviolet (100-400 nm, shorter than visible light: Wikipedia). UVB "is largely absorbed in the epidermis and is the main cause of sunburn" (Wikipedia). Germicidal UV-C (low-pressure mercury lamps at 254 nm) "inactivates microorganisms primarily through direct absorption of photons by their nucleic acids", making dimers in DNA and RNA that "block DNA replication and transcription", "leading to cell death or inability to reproduce"; it "plays a critical role in drinking water treatment"; it works only where it reaches the microbes. Visible light does not inactivate them; it can even help repair UV damage (Grokipedia, Ultraviolet germicidal irradiation). Grokipedia, Electromagnetic spectrum: "UVC disrupts microbial DNA to inactivate pathogens in air, water, and surfaces".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ultraviolet_germicidal_irradiation ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum

F19. Light slows in water and glass (Wikipedia, Speed of light; refractive index of water about 1.33), and shorter wavelengths meet higher refractive indices and bend more (Grokipedia, Visible spectrum), so in water ultraviolet travels no faster than red light.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Visible_spectrum

F20. Gamma rays: wavelengths "shorter than approximately 10 picometers"; "the most energetic and penetrating type"; they pass through materials "opaque to UV and visible light, such as metals or human tissue" and need "dense materials like lead or concrete" to stop them; they damage cells "through ionization", including "damage to critical cellular components such as DNA"; radiotherapy uses "cobalt-60 (Co-60) sources"; gamma irradiation is "a standard method for sterilizing medical equipment". Paul Villard first observed them in 1900.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gamma_ray ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum

F21. Radiation therapy uses X-rays and gamma rays "to damage the DNA within targeted cells and prevent their growth and division"; it "can cause side effects by affecting healthy cells", and planning aims at "minimizing exposure to surrounding healthy tissue" (Grokipedia, Radiation therapy).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiation_therapy

F22. The Gamma Knife: "201 cobalt-60 sources" (Wikipedia; Grokipedia: "192 to 201"), "arranged to converge on a single focal point" (Grokipedia, Radiosurgery). "Each individual beam is of relatively low intensity, so the radiation has little effect on intervening brain tissue and is concentrated only at the tumor itself" (Wikipedia). Lars Leksell built it at the Karolinska Institute in 1967-1968.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_Knife ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiosurgery ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiation_therapy

F23. Radiation from a machine outside the body does not make a patient radioactive: "External-beam therapy, the most common type of radiation treatment, does not make a person radioactive in any way" (MSK; also Stanford Medicine).
    https://www.mskcc.org/news/answers-common-questions-about-radiation-safety ; https://mededucation.stanford.edu/courses/having-radiation-therapy/lessons/radiation-therapy-basics/topic/is-radiation-therapy-safe

## Common misconceptions (for honest wrong choices)

M1. Radio waves are sound waves (OpenStax, F14; a pilot study found "A majority of students still incorrectly conclude that 'radio waves' are sound, even after instruction"; a high-school survey found the same).
    https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-introduction-to-electromagnetic-waves ; https://ijlter.org/index.php/ijlter/article/view/971 ; https://archive.aps.org/mar/2013/c38/10
M2. Microwaves make things radioactive, or break molecules (Grokipedia denies both, F15).
M3. All night-vision cameras work the same way (thermography is not image intensification, F17).
M4. Radiation treatment makes the patient radioactive (F23).
M5. The reverse picture of an X-ray: bones letting more rays through than flesh (F10 shows the opposite).
M6. Electric and radio effects cross space instantly, or at the speed of sound: speed of light in fact (F2, F12); light taking no time at all was Descartes's view (describing-waves facts F21).

## Added in the revision (2026-11-26; Grokipedia first)

F24. Herschel's rays as light: "What Sir William had discovered was a form of light (or radiation) beyond red light", and the "calorific rays" "were later renamed infrared rays or infrared radiation" (IPAC). The fact-check notes that Herschel himself later came to treat the rays of heat and light as different (his second 1800 paper), so the card says they are NOW known to be a kind of light, not that he concluded it.
    https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/Outreach/Edu/Herschel/herschel_bio.html

F25. A photon is "a massless, chargeless elementary particle", and "The energy of a photon is given by E = hν", with ν the wave's frequency (Grokipedia, Photon). So microwaves, like every electromagnetic wave, bring no electric charge into the water: the wrong choice "They fill it with electric charge" is wrong.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Photon

F26. The thermal camera: humans "are not as hot and only emit only infrared waves" (NASA), so a body at about 37 C gives off no visible (red) light and no ultraviolet. Thermography works by "measuring the infrared radiation emitted from objects", "detects and visualizes surface temperature variations", and works in "total darkness where visible light is absent" (Grokipedia, Thermography): it reads rays, not warm air. The fact-check: warm skin gives off only about 1.4 to 1.7 times the cool ground's infrared (radiated power goes as the fourth power of absolute temperature, F17), so the card says "more", not "far more".
    https://science.nasa.gov/ems/07_infraredwaves/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Thermography

F27. Germicidal ultraviolet works "by damaging their genetic material through the formation of pyrimidine dimers in nucleic acids (DNA and RNA)"; "the high-energy photons (shorter wavelengths) are efficiently absorbed by genetic material"; used on liquids it is "a non-thermal method", giving "preservation without heat pasteurization" (Grokipedia, Ultraviolet germicidal irradiation). So ultraviolet kills germs by the energy of each packet, not by heating them, and a bright red lamp with more energy in all still cannot (fact-check on 599d4b10f3).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ultraviolet_germicidal_irradiation

F28. Radio waves' wavelengths, about 100 km down to 1 mm (F13), are far longer than visible light's, about 400 to 700 nanometres (F5), which eyes can see; so radio waves are too long for eyes to see.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radio_wave ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Electromagnetic_spectrum

F29. Gamma rays pass through "human tissue" and need "dense materials like lead or concrete" to stop them (F20), but they are weakened on the way through (the judge's note), so they "pass deep through" flesh and bone rather than "straight through".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gamma_ray
