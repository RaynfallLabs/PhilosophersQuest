# Two Ways to Make Offspring (T3, 7th grade, life strand)

be taught wrong. No old-bank topic was quarried. Lower-tier units already teach

## The two ways, in general

F1. Asexual reproduction needs a single parent, and its offspring are genetically identical copies, or clones, of that parent, barring mutations (rare copying mistakes).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Asexual_reproduction ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-1-reproduction-methods

F2. Asexual reproduction is fast and needs no mate: a single individual can produce large numbers of offspring quickly, which suits a stable environment where the parent's traits already work. Its weakness is that the offspring "may not have the genetic variation to survive in new or different conditions" (OpenStax), and limited diversity leaves a population vulnerable to environmental change and disease (Grokipedia).
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-1-reproduction-methods ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Asexual_reproduction

F3. In sexual reproduction two parents contribute genetic material: their reproductive cells (gametes: sperm and eggs in animals, pollen and ovules in plants) join, and the offspring are genetically unique, differing from each other and from their parents. Sexual reproduction "produces offspring with novel combinations of genes" and gives a species a better chance in an unpredictable or changing environment. Its costs: it takes more resources and time, and a mate must be found. Why sex evolved and persists is still debated by biologists.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Sexual_reproduction ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-1-reproduction-methods

F4. Many species use both ways, depending on conditions (Grokipedia gives Daphnia, asexual under benign conditions and sexual when stressed). In fungi, "sexual reproduction introduces genetic variation into a population" and "often occurs in response to adverse environmental conditions" (OpenStax).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Asexual_reproduction ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-1-characteristics-of-fungi

MISCONCEPTIONS for F1-F4: that each new cell of a dividing bacterium gets half of the genes (each gets a full copy); that asexual offspring are weaker or sexual offspring always stronger than their parents (sexual offspring are only different, some better suited and some worse); that anything grown from a seed must be a copy of its parent.

## Asexual reproduction: the cases

F5. Bacteria reproduce by binary fission: the cell copies its circular chromosome, grows longer, and divides at its midpoint into two genetically identical daughter cells. Under nutrient-rich conditions at 37 degrees Celsius, Escherichia coli divides about every 20 minutes. (Arithmetic from that rate: 20 doublings, 400 minutes, turn one cell into 2^20 = 1,048,576, over a million in under seven hours.)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Fission_(biology) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Asexual_reproduction

F6. Baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) reproduces asexually by budding: a small bud forms on the mother cell's surface, grows, and separates as a daughter cell, leaving a bud scar on the mother.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Saccharomyces_cerevisiae ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saccharomyces_cerevisiae

F7. When yeast is starved of nutrients, its diploid cells can sporulate: they go through meiosis and form four haploid spores, a sexual phase. Haploid yeast cells of opposite mating types (a and alpha) fuse to form diploid cells, and mating "leads to genetic recombination" (a new mix of genes). Caveat: Wikipedia notes that biologists debate whether variation or the repair of damaged DNA is the main force keeping meiosis in yeast.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saccharomyces_cerevisiae ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Saccharomyces_cerevisiae

F8. Moulds reproduce mainly asexually, producing vast quantities of microscopic spores. Black bread mould (Rhizopus stolonifer) grows a cottony web of threads over bread and raises upright stalks topped with black sporangia, sacs packed with spores; the lightweight spores are carried by air currents, and a spore that lands on a suitable surface sends out threads that grow into a new mould. Its sexual route, thick-walled zygospores, forms only when threads of two mating types (+ and -) meet.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Rhizopus_stolonifer ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mold ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-1-characteristics-of-fungi

F9. Mould spores are invisible to the naked eye and "float through outdoor and indoor air"; mould begins to grow when spores land on damp surfaces (EPA). Bread is contaminated after baking: spores "can settle on bread during cooling, slicing, or packaging" (Puratos, a bakery-ingredients firm). The respectable wrong theory: spontaneous generation, the old idea that living things arise from non-living matter. Pier Antonio Micheli, an Italian botanist, placed spores on melon slices and found that the fungi that grew were the same kind the spores came from (his Nova plantarum genera, 1729), and concluded that fungi did not arise by spontaneous generation.
    https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home ; https://www.puratos.com/blog/mold-packaged-bread-understanding-the-science-behind ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Spontaneous_generation ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Antonio_Micheli

F10. Strawberry plants send out runners (stolons), horizontal stems that form new plants at their nodes; the runner's daughter plants are genetically identical to the mother plant. Strawberries can be grown from seed, but the seedlings "will not develop true to the variety" (UConn); the garden strawberry is an octoploid hybrid, and breeders make new kinds by controlled crosses. Strawberry flowers are pollinated mainly by bees. (The specks on a berry's skin are achenes, each a tiny fruit holding one seed.)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Strawberry ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Asexual_reproduction ; https://homegarden.cahnr.uconn.edu/factsheets/strawberries

F11. A potato is a tuber, a swollen underground stem whose eyes are dormant buds that can sprout new shoots; an onion bulb is a short stem wrapped in fleshy leaves. Both grow new plants asexually.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Asexual_reproduction

F12. Planarians are small freshwater flatworms with huge powers of regeneration, driven by stem cells called neoblasts: a fragment as small as 1/279th of a worm can regrow into a complete worm within a few weeks. Asexual planarians reproduce by fission: the body pinches across its middle into a head piece and a tail piece, and each regrows the parts it lacks, making clones without a mate. Thomas Hunt Morgan made some of the first systematic studies of their regeneration.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Planarian ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planarian ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Fission_(biology)

F13. Cycads are cone-bearing seed plants (gymnosperms) whose seeds are not enclosed in a fruit. They are dioecious: male plants make pollen cones, and only female plants bear seed cones with ovules. Cycads also reproduce asexually by offsets, shoots that sprout from the base of the plant.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cycad

F14. Wood's cycad (Encephalartos woodii): John Medley Wood, a botanist, found it at the edge of the Ngoye Forest in South Africa in 1895. It was a single male plant with four stems, and no female has ever been found, so the species cannot reproduce sexually and is extinct in the wild. Every plant alive today (about 500) is a clone grown from offsets of that one male; Kew Gardens received an offset in 1899, and its plant first bore a male cone in 2004 (Wikipedia); drone surveys of the forest in 2022 and 2024 found no female.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Encephalartos_woodii ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encephalartos_woodii

## Flowers, pollination and fruit

F15. A flower's parts: the sepals, outermost, protect the developing bud; the petals attract pollinators; each stamen is a slender filament carrying an anther, where pollen is made; the pistil is made of the stigma, which receives pollen, the style beneath it, and the ovary at its base, which holds the ovules that become seeds.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Flower ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Stamen

F16. Pollination is the carrying of pollen from an anther to a stigma; fertilization comes after, when a pollen tube grows down through the style to an ovule and a male gamete joins the egg cell there. MISCONCEPTION: pollination and fertilization are one and the same.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Flower ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Sexual_reproduction

F17. After fertilization the ovary develops into a fruit enclosing the seeds, which come from the ovules: in botany a fruit is the mature, ripened ovary of a flowering plant. Tomatoes and cucumbers are botanically fruits though cooks use them as vegetables, and a pea pod is a dry fruit. MISCONCEPTION: a fruit must be sweet.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Fruit ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Flower

F18. Wind-pollinated flowers are small and plain, with no showy petals, scent or nectar; they release vast quantities of light, smooth, dry pollen and have large feathery stigmas to catch it. Grasses (maize among them), oaks, birches, ragweeds and nearly all conifers are wind-pollinated. Wind pollination is wasteful: on average only about 0.32 percent of pollen reaches a target, and grass flowers can make over 22 million grains a head. Airborne pollen of this kind triggers hay fever.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Anemophily ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome

F19. Corn (maize) is wind-pollinated: pollen from the tassel at the top of the stalk is carried by the wind to the silks of the ear. Each silk is the long stigma and style of a female flower and is connected to a single ovule, a potential kernel; a pollen grain that lands on it grows a tube down the silk to the ovule. "Each kernel develops only when a pollen grain lands on the silk attached to that kernel" (UNH). Block plantings of at least three or four short rows are pollinated more successfully than one or two long rows (Virginia Tech); too few rows in a block gives poor pollination and poorly filled ears. Caveat: dry weather during silking, crowding and poor soil can also leave ears poorly filled. Corn is mainly cross-fertilizing, but a stalk's own pollen also works: breeders self-pollinated maize to make inbred lines (Grokipedia, Maize). Corn needs no insects to carry its pollen.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Maize ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Corn_silk ; https://extension.unh.edu/resource/growing-sweet-corn-fact-sheet ; https://www.pubs.ext.vt.edu/426/426-405/426-405.html

F20. Bird-pollinated flowers are bright red, orange or yellow, tubular, with plenty of dilute nectar hidden deep inside, open by day, and odorless, because birds have a poor sense of smell and find flowers by sight; hummingbirds are strongly drawn to red.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ornithophily ; https://fs.usda.gov/wildflowers/pollinators/animals/birds.shtml

F21. Bee-pollinated flowers are bright, usually blue or yellow ("bees cannot see red", USDA Forest Service), sweet or minty in scent, with landing platforms. Bees can see ultraviolet light, and many bee flowers have a region that absorbs ultraviolet near the centre of each petal, a nectar guide that helps a bee find the flower's centre quickly.
    https://fs.usda.gov/wildflowers/pollinators/animals/bees.shtml ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome

F22. Nectar guides are markings (lines, spots or blotches) that direct pollinators to a flower's reward of nectar or pollen and are thought to cut the time a visitor spends at each flower. The meadow buttercup's guides are visible only in ultraviolet light: under ultraviolet its flowers show a darker centre, where the nectaries are. The ultraviolet colour, invisible to humans, has been called "bee violet".
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nectar_guide ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome

F23. Moth-pollinated flowers are white or pale, often open at dusk or at night, have a strong sweet scent and long, narrow tubes.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome

F24. Bat-pollinated flowers open at night, are large and sturdy, dull in colour (white, green, muted tones), give off strong musty, fruity or sulphurous scents, and offer abundant dilute nectar. Beetle-pollinated flowers are large open bowls or dishes, dull in colour, with strong yeasty, fruity or putrid scents, and rarely offer nectar (pollen is their reward).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome

F25. Caveat on all such flower types: they are tendencies, not rules. Grokipedia notes that only about 30 percent of plant species match their predicted pollinators closely, and a Forest Service study found bees also pollinating a red, hummingbird-style Penstemon. Questions therefore ask which pollinator a flower "most likely" suits.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome ; https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/49644

## Added in the revision (2026-11-26)

F26. Yeast spores are tough resting cells. Starving yeast "differentiates into a dormant cell type, termed a spore, which is resistant to many types of environmental stress"; "The stress resistance of the spore is due primarily to a spore wall that is more elaborate than the vegetative cell wall", "a thick coat ... more extensive than the cell wall of vegetative cells", made of four layers; spores survive treatments such as "temperature shock at 55°C". The toughness is a second way sporulation carries yeast through hard times; it does not make the spores' genes better.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2478712/ (Coluccio et al., PLoS ONE 2008) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Saccharomyces_cerevisiae (spores form "under nutrient starvation conditions" and "germinate under favorable conditions")

F27. Corn silks catch pollen with a sticky surface: the stigma's cells release a "viscous exudate to adhere pollen grains upon contact", and the pollen tube then grows down the silk to the ovule, usually "within 24 hours of pollination". A grain that lands is held fast, not blown off again by the wind.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Corn_silk

F28. Hawk moths pollinate moth flowers: "white or pale coloration", "strong sweet scents released primarily at dusk", and "long, narrow corolla tubes that match the pollinators' proboscises" (Nicotiana flowers "emit potent night fragrances" to draw hawkmoths). Bird flowers are "bright red, orange, or yellow", and birds have "a weak sense of smell, rendering scent unnecessary and often absent". Bumblebees are bees, which cannot see red (F21).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Pollination_syndrome

F29. Besides the main circular chromosome, many bacteria carry plasmids: "small", "extrachromosomal, usually circular, double-stranded DNA" molecules that "exist independently of the bacterial chromosome". So a bacterium keeps nearly all, not all, of its genes on its one main loop.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Plasmid
