# Cells: The Building Blocks of Life (T2, 6th grade, life strand)

Sources were read on 2026-11-25. Grokipedia first; a second source is given
for anything a child could be taught wrong.

## Cell theory, size and Hooke

F1. Cell theory has three ideas: every living thing is made of one or more cells (the first); the cell is the basic unit of life (the second); every new cell comes from a cell that already exists (the third).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_theory ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-1-studying-cells

F2. With few exceptions, single cells are too small to see with the naked eye; a human red blood cell is about 8 micrometres (eight millionths of a metre) across.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-1-studying-cells

F3. Robert Hooke looked at thin slices of cork under his microscope and named the tiny box-like compartments "cells" in his book Micrographia, published in 1665.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Robert_Hooke ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_theory ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/3-2-foundations-of-modern-cell-theory

F4. Hooke likened the cork's compartments to the cells of a honeycomb. The word cell comes from the Latin cellula, "small room".
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11530768/ (Peters, The Plant Cell 36(11), 2024) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology)

F5. Cork is made of dead, thin-walled cells whose insides are largely empty and filled with air; the cork cells are dead at maturity. Hooke's cork cells "were long dead and, therefore, lacked the internal structures", so he saw only the walls (Grokipedia: "empty walls without visible contents"; "the lignified cell walls of dead plant tissue").
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cork_(material) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cork_cambium ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/3-2-foundations-of-modern-cell-theory ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Robert_Hooke

## One cell or many

F6. A unicellular (one-celled) organism is a single cell that carries out all the jobs of life by itself; examples are bacteria, the amoeba, the paramecium and yeast. Most are microscopic.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Unicellular_organism

F7. A multicellular (many-celled) organism is made of more than one cell, and its cells specialise to do different jobs; animals, plants and most fungi are multicellular. An adult human body has around 30 trillion cells (Grokipedia gives about 36 trillion for adult men).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Multicellular_organism ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology)

## Prokaryotes and eukaryotes

F8. Prokaryotes (bacteria and archaea) are single-celled and have no nucleus and no other membrane-bound parts. Their genetic material (DNA) sits in the cytoplasm, in a region called the nucleoid, with no membrane around it.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Prokaryote

F9. Eukaryotic cells keep their DNA inside a nucleus wrapped in a membrane; plants, animals, fungi and protists are eukaryotes. Eukaryotes can be single-celled or multicellular.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_(biology) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology)

F10. Every cell, prokaryote or eukaryote, has a cell membrane, cytoplasm, DNA and ribosomes. Bacteria have no mitochondria and no chloroplasts (no membrane-bound organelles). Most bacteria have a cell wall.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells

F11. An amoeba is a single-celled eukaryote: it has a nucleus, is bounded by a thin cell membrane with no cell wall, and cannot make its own food (it engulfs food).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Amoeba ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoeba

## Parts and their jobs

F12. The cell membrane is a selective barrier around the cell: it controls the passage of water, oxygen, food molecules and wastes into and out of the cell.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_(biology)

F13. The nucleus houses the cell's genetic material (DNA) and is the cell's control centre: it directs the cell's work, including the making of proteins.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_nucleus ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells

F14. The cytoplasm is the jelly-like substance that fills the cell between the membrane and the nucleus; the cell's other parts sit in it.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_(biology) ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells

F15. Mitochondria release usable energy from food such as the sugar glucose, using oxygen (aerobic respiration, which makes ATP); they are often called the "powerhouse of the cell".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Mitochondrion ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells

F16. Plant cells have mitochondria as well as chloroplasts: OpenStax calls mitochondria the powerhouses "of both plant and animal cells", and SAPS states that all plant cells contain mitochondria.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells ; https://www.saps.org.uk/growth-hub/misconceptions-in-biology/

F17. Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis: they use light energy, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar (glucose) and oxygen. Chlorophyll in them makes them green. They are found in plants and algae, not in animal cells, and not in roots.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chloroplast ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells

F18. The cell wall is a stiff layer outside the cell membrane; it supports the cell and keeps its shape. A plant's wall is mainly cellulose. Animal cells have no cell wall; fungi, algae and most bacteria have walls.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_wall ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells

F19. A mature plant cell has a large central vacuole, often 80 to 90 percent of the cell's volume, that stores water (and dissolved food, salts and wastes). Animal cells have vacuoles too, but they are smaller and fewer.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vacuole ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology)

F20. Water pushing into the vacuoles presses the cell out against its wall (turgor) and keeps the plant firm. When the plant runs short of water, the central vacuole shrinks, the wall is left unsupported and the plant wilts. Ordinary wilting reverses once the plant takes up water again.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Wilting ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Vacuole

## Levels of organisation

F21. In a many-celled body: similar cells working together form a tissue; two or more kinds of tissue form an organ; organs working together form an organ system; and the systems together make the organism.
    https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/1-2-structural-organization-of-the-human-body ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-1-studying-cells

F22. The heart is an organ made of cardiac muscle, connective and lining (endothelial) tissues that together pump blood. The circulatory system, an organ system, is the heart, the blood vessels and the blood.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Organ_(biology) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Circulatory_system

F23. Plants have organs too: roots, stems and leaves (a leaf is made of several tissues).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Organ_(biology)

## New cells from old: a scraped knee

F24. New skin covers a wound when living skin cells (keratinocytes) of the epidermis at the wound's edges multiply and move across it.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Wound_healing

F25. A scab is a dried-up blood clot (platelets, blood cells, fibrin and plasma) that shields the wound from germs while new skin tissue grows underneath. Once the skin has healed, the scab falls off.
    https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/scab ; https://kidshealth.org/en/kids/scab.html

F26. The outermost layer of the skin (the stratum corneum) is made of dead cells. New skin cells start in the living bottom layer of the epidermis (the stratum basale) and move up.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Stratum_corneum

## Misconceptions (honest wrong choices)

M1. "There are no mitochondria in plant cells" and "plants don't respire, they only carry out photosynthesis" are recorded student misconceptions. Mixing up the two energy parts (chloroplasts and mitochondria) follows from it.
    https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=889 ; https://www.saps.org.uk/growth-hub/misconceptions-in-biology/

M2. Students think plant cells are surrounded by a cell wall instead of a cell membrane, rather than by both; and some think the cell wall is the part that controls what moves in and out.
    https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=156861&section=5 ; https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=889

M3. Students mix up cells and molecules, including their sizes, and some think a prokaryotic cell contains a nucleus.
    https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=156861&section=5 ; https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=740

M4. The "nucleus is the control centre" label makes students mix up the nucleus with the cell membrane, which controls what passes in and out (author's note on the two "control" words; F12, F13).

M5. MYTH, not used in the questions: Hooke named cells after the little rooms monks slept in. Grokipedia and Wikipedia repeat it, but Peters (2024) reports that "No monks or monasteries are found anywhere in Hooke's writings on cells, and neither is the Latin cellula"; Hooke's own comparison was a honeycomb (F4).
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11530768/

M6. Folk belief that the scab itself turns into the new skin; it does not, it falls off (F25).

## Added in the revision (2026-11-25; sources read today, Grokipedia first)

F27. Bacteria (and archaea) are prokaryotes, single-celled organisms; a single bacterium is one cell. Prokaryotic cells are 0.1 to 5.0 micrometres across, much smaller than eukaryotic cells such as a person's, which are 10 to 100 micrometres across.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells ("We classify only the predominantly single-celled organisms Bacteria and Archaea as prokaryotes"; "At 0.1 to 5.0 um in diameter, prokaryotic cells are significantly smaller than eukaryotic cells, which have diameters ranging from 10 to 100 um") ; https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotes, single-celled organisms")

F28. A virus is not a cell: it is an acellular infectious agent, a genome in a protein coat, with no cytoplasm or ribosomes of its own; it is a different thing from a bacterium.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Virus ("A virus is an acellular infectious agent"; "viruses lack cellular structures such as ribosomes, cytoplasm, and independent metabolic machinery")

F29. Atoms form molecules, and molecules build up into the parts of cells; the cell is the smallest fundamental unit of structure and function in living things (the second idea of cell theory), so a molecule is far smaller than a cell.
    https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology ("Atoms form molecules"; "Some cells contain aggregates of macromolecules surrounded by membranes"; "The cell itself is the smallest fundamental unit of structure and function in living organisms")

F30. New skin over a scrape grows from living skin cells (basal keratinocytes) at the wound's edges AND from skin cells left in the wound's floor, lining hair follicles and sweat glands; they spread across the wound underneath the scab, which they free from the tissue below.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Wound_healing ("migration of keratinocytes from the viable epidermis at the wound margins"; cells "must burrow beneath" scabs) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wound_healing ("Basal keratinocytes from the wound edges and dermal appendages such as hair follicles"; reepithelization "from the wound margins and from skin appendages such as hair follicles"; keratinocytes "stay underneath the scab") ; https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/679597 (Rittie and colleagues, American Journal of Pathology, 2012: sweat glands beneath a wound are a major source of the new skin cells)

F31. Cardiac muscle (myocardium) is a muscle TISSUE: it forms the thick middle layer of the heart's walls, makes up most of the heart's mass, and contracts to pump blood. (The heart, built of several tissues, is the organ.)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cardiac_muscle ("Cardiac muscle, also known as myocardium, is the specialized striated muscle tissue"; "forms the thick middle layer of the heart walls"; "comprising the majority of the heart's overall mass"; "responsible for the organ's contractile function in pumping blood")

F32. Animal cells have only small vacuoles (about 0.1 to 1 micrometre), and some animal cells have none. This corrects F19's "fewer": Wikipedia says animal vacuoles are "usually greater in number", while Grokipedia says "fewer", so the questions say neither.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vacuole (animal vacuoles "typically small, ranging from 0.1 to 1 um in diameter") ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuole ("Animal vacuoles are smaller than their plant counterparts but also usually greater in number. There are also animal cells that do not have any vacuoles.")

F33. CAUTION, correcting F10: not every cell carries DNA. Mature mammalian red blood cells push out their nucleus and most organelles, so no question or context says that every cell has DNA.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Red_blood_cell ("the nucleus and most organelles, including mitochondria and ribosomes, are extruded")

M7. Bacteria and viruses are lumped together as "germs". Simon, Enzinger and Fink (2017), PLoS ONE 12(3): e0174402, found severe misconceptions about viruses among Austrian high school and university students, such as "defining a virus as a pro- or eukaryotic cell". The lure "A kind of virus, not a cell" is the same confusion seen from the bacterium's side (author's judgment).
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5370109/
