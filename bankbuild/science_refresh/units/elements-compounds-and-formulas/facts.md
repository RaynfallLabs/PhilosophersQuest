## Elements, molecules and compounds

F1. An element is a pure substance made of only one type of atom, and it cannot be broken down into other substances by chemical means. Texas TEKS 7.6A has seventh graders "compare and contrast elements and compounds in terms of atoms and molecules, chemical symbols, and chemical formulas"; its guide says compounds always contain more than one type of element, and "O2 is made up of two oxygen atoms and is an element, not a compound."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_element ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_substance ; https://teksguide.org/teks/s76a/alignments

F2. A molecule is "an electrically neutral entity consisting of more than one atom", the atoms "bound together by chemical bonds". "Molecules of elements contain only one kind of atom", while "molecules of compounds incorporate two or more different elements" (water, H2O, where two hydrogen atoms bond with one oxygen atom). A molecule is "the smallest unit of a chemical compound that retains its composition and chemical properties". The noble gases, such as helium and argon, exist as single atoms, not molecules.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Molecule ; https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-4-chemical-formulas

F3. Ordinary oxygen is "a colorless, odorless, and tasteless diatomic gas (O2)", about 21 percent of the air by volume, and oxygen is an element (symbol O, atomic number 8). Ozone, O3, is "a triatomic allotrope of oxygen, consisting of three oxygen atoms": a pale blue gas with a pungent smell, like the smell after lightning, that irritates the eyes, nose, throat and lungs. The ozone layer in the stratosphere "absorbs nearly all of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet" light. Christian Friedrich Schönbein named ozone (1839-1840) from the Greek ozein, "to smell". OpenStax: most samples of hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen are made of two-atom molecules, and these are still elements.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Ozone ; https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-4-chemical-formulas

F4. A compound is two or more different elements chemically bonded together in a fixed ratio; it can be broken down into its elements only by chemical reactions. A compound usually has properties quite unlike those of the elements it is made from. Oak National Academy's KS3 lesson (ages 11-14) names the misconception "Compounds will have the same properties as the elements from which they are made" and corrects it: "Compounds usually have completely different properties from the elements from which they are made." The RSC's 11-14 guide: "sodium chloride has very different properties to elemental sodium."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_compound ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/atoms-elements-and-compounds/lessons/compounds-and-their-formation ; https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/elements-and-compounds/3009350.article

## Reading a chemical formula

F5. In a chemical formula, subscripts "appear after symbols to denote atom counts, omitted when equal to one", so a symbol with no number stands for one atom: H2O is "two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom"; CO2 is "one carbon and two oxygens". TEKS 7.6B: "use the periodic table to identify the atoms and the number of each kind within a chemical formula"; its guide calls water's formula a fixed 2:1 proportion. A subscript applies only to the symbol just before it.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_formula ; https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-4-chemical-formulas ; https://teksguide.org/teks/s76b/alignments

F6. Element symbols are one or two letters, "the first letter uppercase and the second lowercase", so a capital letter starts each new symbol. Cobalt is Co (atomic number 27, a hard, lustrous, bluish-grey metal, ferromagnetic at room temperature); copper is Cu, from the Latin cuprum; calcium is Ca. CO is two symbols, carbon and oxygen: carbon monoxide, "one carbon atom covalently bonded to one oxygen atom", "a colorless, odorless, tasteless" gas that is poisonous because it binds to haemoglobin; it comes chiefly from the incomplete burning of carbon fuels.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_formula ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cobalt ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Copper ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_monoxide

F7. Ammonia has "the chemical formula NH3, consisting of one nitrogen atom bonded to three hydrogen atoms". It is a colourless gas with "a sharp, pungent odor". Bacteria and fungi that decompose "decaying biomass and animal wastes" release ammonia (ammonification). The NGSS clarification for MS-PS1-1 names ammonia as an example of a simple molecule.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Ammonia ; https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps1-1-matter-and-its-interactions

F8. Glucose has the molecular formula C6H12O6. OpenStax: "Molecules of glucose (blood sugar) contain 6 carbon atoms, 12 hydrogen atoms, and 6 oxygen atoms." Grokipedia: glucose is "known also as dextrose or blood sugar", is made by plants "primarily through photosynthesis", and is "the primary energy source for most living organisms". The curriculum line G7.P1 names glucose, C6H12O6, as a formula seventh graders read.
    https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-4-chemical-formulas ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Glucose

F9. Some compounds are not made of separate molecules but of one repeating network of atoms (an extended structure); the NGSS clarification for MS-PS1-1 gives "sodium chloride or diamonds" as examples. Quartz is "a mineral composed primarily of silicon dioxide (SiO2)", built as "a three-dimensional framework of interconnected SiO4 tetrahedra", with each oxygen "bridging two silicon atoms", which is why its overall formula is SiO2: two oxygen atoms for every silicon atom. Sand is mostly quartz. For such crystals the formula gives a ratio: NaCl "denotes the ratio in the crystal lattice rather than a single entity".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Quartz ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Sand ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_formula ; https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps1-1-matter-and-its-interactions

## Sodium, chlorine and table salt

F10. Sodium is "a soft, bright, silvery-white solid at room temperature" that "can be easily cut with a knife"; "contact with water triggers a violent reaction" that "generates flammable hydrogen and sufficient heat to ignite the gas", so it is stored under mineral oil or kerosene, and it "is never found free in nature". Humphry Davy first isolated it in 1807 by electrolysis of molten sodium hydroxide.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium

F11. Chlorine is a gas at room temperature with "a distinctive pale yellow-green color and pungent odor"; it is "highly toxic as a pulmonary irritant"; it exists as the two-atom molecule Cl2. Carl Wilhelm Scheele discovered it in 1774, and Humphry Davy showed in 1810 that it was an element, with no oxygen in it.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chlorine ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium_chloride

F12. Sodium chloride, NaCl, is table salt: sodium and chloride "in a 1:1 ratio", in an ionic lattice where each sodium is surrounded by six chlorides. It is "chemically stable and non-reactive under ordinary conditions", safe to eat in moderate amounts, an "essential electrolyte in living organisms", and forms colourless cubic crystals (a white powder when fine). Oak: "Sodium metal and chlorine gas are both dangerous and toxic to humans, however sodium chloride is added to food."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium_chloride ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/atoms-elements-and-compounds/lessons/compounds-and-their-formation

F13. Other well-known compounds of sodium or chlorine each need another element as well. Household bleach is a 3-6 percent solution of sodium hypochlorite, NaOCl (sodium, oxygen, chlorine), made industrially from chlorine and sodium hydroxide solution. Caustic soda, also called lye, is sodium hydroxide, NaOH, "highly corrosive to metals, tissues". Hydrochloric acid is "a colorless to slightly yellow aqueous solution of hydrogen chloride gas (HCl) in water", strongly corrosive; it is the main part of the stomach's gastric acid.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium_hypochlorite ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium_hydroxide ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Hydrochloric_acid

## Fixed proportions

F14. Electrolysis "decomposes liquid water into hydrogen and oxygen gases in a 2:1 molar ratio", which is a 2:1 ratio by volume: hydrogen collects at the wire joined to the negative terminal, oxygen at the positive one. The page says the ratio "reflects the stoichiometric requirement of two water molecules to yield two hydrogen molecules and one oxygen molecule", and the Hofmann voltameter's two tubes measure it, confirming "water's molecular composition". Hydrogen gas is H2 and oxygen gas O2, both two-atom molecules.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electrolysis_of_water ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Hydrogen ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen

F15. Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac announced in 1808 that "2 volumes of hydrogen gas combine with 1 volume of oxygen gas to produce 2 volumes of water vapor". Amedeo Avogadro proposed in 1811 that equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules, with diatomic molecules such as H2 and O2, which accounted for those volumes.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gay-Lussac's_law

F16. Gas volume does not come from atom size or weight, and the hydrogen side of the 2:1 is neither bigger atoms nor heavier gas. Hydrogen is "the lightest and most abundant chemical element", and Henry Cavendish's hydrogen of 1766 had a density "approximately one-tenth that of common air". A hydrogen atom is not twice the size of an oxygen atom by any measure: empirical radius 25 pm against 60 pm for oxygen; van der Waals radius 110-120 pm against 152 pm; covalent radius 32 pm against 63 pm (one calculated value, 53 pm against 48 pm, puts them about equal). In a series circuit, such as the two wires of an electrolysis cell, "the current through each component is identical".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Hydrogen ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_radii_of_the_elements_(data_page) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Series_and_parallel_circuits

F17. The law of definite proportions: a compound always contains its elements in the same proportions by mass, whatever its source. Water is always 1 part hydrogen to 8 parts oxygen by mass (11.19 percent hydrogen, 88.81 percent oxygen); Wikipedia: oxygen is "about 8/9 of the mass of any sample of pure water".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Law_of_definite_proportions ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_definite_proportions

F18. Joseph Louis Proust, a French chemist, first stated the law in a paper of 1794 (printed 1797) and backed it with work published from 1797 to 1804. He made copper carbonate in the laboratory and compared it with the natural mineral, and found the same mass ratio of copper, carbon and oxygen (about 5:1:4) regardless of source; he did the same with the two tin oxides and the two iron sulfides. Claude Louis Berthollet, another French chemist, argued (Essai de Statique Chimique, 1803) that elements "could combine in any ratio", the composition set by affinity and conditions; the decade-long debate was "largely resolved in Proust's favor during the 1810s", and Jöns Jacob Berzelius credited Proust with the law in 1812. Honest exception: some crystals, non-stoichiometric compounds named "berthollides" after Berthollet, vary a little (wüstite, Fe(1-x)O).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Law_of_definite_proportions ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Proust ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_definite_proportions

F19. Hydrogen peroxide, H2O2, consists of "two hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms bonded in a chain". Pure, it is "a pale blue, viscous liquid" and "a powerful oxidizing agent", used to bleach hair; it slowly decomposes into water and oxygen gas, while water is a "stable, non-reactive solvent". The same two elements in a different proportion make a different compound.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Hydrogen_peroxide ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_atomic_theory

## The first discovery stories

F20. John Dalton, an English chemist, published the first part of A New System of Chemical Philosophy in 1808, setting out his atomic theory: "all atoms of an element have the same weight; atoms of different elements have different weights"; atoms "combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds"; "No atoms are created or destroyed in chemical reactions." By his "rule of greatest simplicity" he took water to be one hydrogen and one oxygen, HO, not H2O. He drew atoms as circles, and Jöns Jacob Berzelius proposed letter symbols from the elements' Latin names in 1813-1814, first writing atom counts as superscripts (H²O), later changed to subscripts.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/John_Dalton ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_atomic_theory ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalton ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Jöns_Jacob_Berzelius ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_formula

F21. Gold is denser than lead (19.3 against 11.34 grams per cubic centimetre), but a gold atom (atomic mass 196.97) is a different kind of atom from a lead atom (standard atomic weight 207.2; atomic numbers 79 and 82), not the same atom packed tighter. "Gold possesses a single stable isotope, 197Au, which constitutes 100% of naturally occurring gold", so the atoms in pure gold really are all alike in weight, as Dalton held. A single atom has no colour or shine of its own: the RSC's 11-14 guide warns of the misconception "that atoms share the properties of the bulk material", and notes that "individual copper atoms have no colour, but billions of them joined together make a lustrous red metal."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gold ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lead ; https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/elements-and-compounds/3009350.article

F22. Before atomic theory, the alchemists' sulfur-mercury theory held that "metals form in the earth through the mixing of sulfur and mercury", gold coming from "the most subtle and well-balanced sulfur"; it was "a mineralogical theory that would remain dominant until the 18th century". (Behind a wrong choice and a context line; not keyed.)
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabir_ibn_Hayyan

## Misconceptions (honest wrong choices)

M1. Students confuse subscripts and coefficients in formulas (a common misconception in a Chemistry Education Research and Practice study, 2012); author's judgment, unsourced: students also stretch a subscript over the whole formula (reading NH3 as three of each atom) or hang it on the wrong symbol.
    https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2012/rp/c2rp00015f

M2. Students think a compound keeps the properties of its elements (F4, Oak National Academy), or that a compound's properties are a blend of its elements'.
    https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/atoms-elements-and-compounds/lessons/compounds-and-their-formation

M3. "A common misconception is that atoms share the properties of the bulk material" (RSC: copper atoms have no colour); author's judgment: students speak of "water atoms" and picture the smallest bit of water as a tiny droplet.
    https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/elements-and-compounds/3009350.article

M4. Students could not "discriminate between particulate representations of compounds and elements" (Briggs and Holding, 1986); author's judgment: a molecule of joined atoms, such as O2 or O3, is taken for a compound because its atoms are bonded. TEKS guide (F1) corrects it with O2.
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-differences-between-elements-compounds-and-mixtures-beyond-appearances/4017771.article ; https://teksguide.org/teks/s76a/alignments

M5. Author's judgment: students mix up the C symbols (carbon C, calcium Ca, cobalt Co, copper Cu), and read a formula for a crystal (SiO2, NaCl) as the whole grain's count of atoms.

## Added in the revision (2026-11-26)

F23. Avogadro's law: "equal volumes of gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of molecules", a rule "independent of the gas's chemical identity" (the page's examples are hydrogen and oxygen; a litre of helium and a litre of nitrogen at STP hold the same number of molecules, though helium is far lighter). So a lighter gas does not fill more space for the same number of molecules.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Avogadro's_law

F24. Quartz sand grains are of two kinds: "monocrystalline quartz where each sand grain is a single crystal" and "polycrystalline quartz where grains consist of crystal aggregates", "several interlocking crystals". So a grain is made of crystal, but not always of one crystal.
    https://www.geological-digressions.com/the-mineralogy-of-sandstones-quartz-grains/

F25. Dalton's theory as taught: "Atoms of one element cannot be converted into atoms of another element" (University of Houston chemistry slides); "Atoms cannot be subdivided, created or destroyed" and "In chemical reactions, atoms are combined, separated, or rearranged" (Wikipedia). The alchemists' transmutation of one metal into another is what this denies.
    https://www.uh.edu/~chem1p/c2/tsld015.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalton

F26. Later chemists found that the atoms of one element can differ in mass: an isotope "differs in the number of neutrons in its nucleus, leading to variations in atomic mass". "80 of the first 82 elements in the periodic table possess at least one stable isotope" (Grokipedia), and only 26 elements have a single one (Wikipedia, Monoisotopic element), gold-197 among them; so most elements' atoms come in more than one weight, while gold's are all alike (F21).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Isotope ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoisotopic_element
