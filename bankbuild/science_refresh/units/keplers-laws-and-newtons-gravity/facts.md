## Tycho Brahe's sightings

F1. Tycho Brahe was "the last major astronomer to work without the aid of a telescope" (Wikipedia); OpenStax: "the last and greatest of the pre-telescopic observers in Europe." His positions reached "an accuracy approaching one arcminute", about five times as accurate as those of Wilhelm of Hesse (Wikipedia); Grokipedia gives "accuracies of 0.5 to 2 arcminutes", an order of magnitude better than his predecessors, against medieval tables with "positional errors exceeding 30 arcminutes". His Mars record runs to more than 1,000 entries from 1582 to 1600 (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Tycho_Brahe ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion

F2. Johannes Kepler joined Tycho near Prague in 1600 as his assistant, and Tycho set him the task of working out the orbit of Mars. Tycho died on 24 October 1601 and Kepler, his successor as Imperial Mathematician, inherited the records. Smallpox in childhood had left Kepler with weakened vision.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Tycho_Brahe ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Johannes_Kepler ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe

## The eight arcminutes (Astronomia Nova, 1609)

F3. Kepler's first model of Mars, the "vicarious hypothesis", put the planet on a circle with an equant point and matched Tycho's oppositions of Mars "to within Tycho's observational accuracy of two minutes of arc"; Kepler still judged it false. The version with the eccentricity bisected erred by 8 minutes of arc, "greater than Tycho's observational error", and Kepler wrote that "these eight minutes alone will lead us along a path to the reform of the whole of Astronomy." (Wikipedia.) Grokipedia: residuals "such as an eight-minute angular discrepancy" that no combination of circles and equants could take up. Eight arcminutes is about a quarter of the full Moon's width (the Moon looks about half a degree, some 30 arcminutes, across; NASA gives 1,896 arcseconds, about 31.6 arcminutes, at opposition).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomia_nova ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Astronomia_nova ; https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html

F4. Astronomia Nova was finished in manuscript in 1607 and "in print by August 1609" (Wikipedia); Grokipedia: "appeared in August 1609." Kepler found the equal-areas law first (1602) and the ellipse later (1605), "after exhaustive trials of alternative shapes" (Grokipedia); Wikipedia: "Kepler discovered the 'second law' before the first."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Astronomia_nova ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomia_nova

F5. Before the ellipse Kepler tried an oval "slightly wider at the perihelion than at aphelion", "properly an egg shape"; it missed by 8 minutes of arc in the opposite direction to the circle model, and Kepler concluded the true path lay halfway between the two: the ellipse.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomia_nova

F6. Astronomers from Ptolemy (the Almagest, about 150 CE) built planetary paths from circles: a deferent circle carrying a smaller epicycle on which the planet moves. Copernicus's Sun-centred model "retained circular orbits and employed epicycles"; Kepler's first law "eliminated the need for epicycles." Copernicus's predictions were "comparable to, but not superior to" Ptolemy's. Copernicus died on 24 May 1543, long before Tycho's sightings of Mars.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Deferent_and_epicycle ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Nicolaus_Copernicus

F7. Galileo built his first telescope in June or July 1609 and began systematic telescopic observation of the Moon late in 1609; he turned it on Jupiter in early January 1610 and found its moons. So no telescope had been used on Mars when Kepler found the eight arcminutes.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Galileo_Galilei

## Kepler's three laws

F8. First law: "the orbit of every planet is an ellipse with the Sun at one of the two foci" (Grokipedia); OpenStax: "an ellipse, with the Sun at one focus of the ellipse." The planets' ellipses are nearly round: Mars's orbit, "drawn to scale, would be practically indistinguishable from a circle" (OpenStax), and textbook figures exaggerate them ("the eccentricities of the planets' orbits in our solar system are substantially less than shown here"). Eccentricities: Mercury 0.206, Mars 0.094, Saturn 0.052, Earth 0.017 (NASA). MISCONCEPTION: the Sun sits at the centre of the orbit; orbits are long thin ovals.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion ; https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/

F9. Second law: "a line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time", so "planets move faster when closer to the Sun (at perihelion) and slower when farther away" (Grokipedia); OpenStax: "the planet speeds up as it comes closer to the Sun and slows down as it pulls away from the Sun." The first two laws were published in Astronomia Nova (1609).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion

F10. Earth is nearest the Sun (perihelion) around 3 January, about 147 million km away, and farthest in July, about 152 million km (Grokipedia Season; NASA: perihelion 147.1, aphelion 152.1 million km). The seasons are uneven: from the March equinox 92.75 days to the June solstice, 93.65 days to the September equinox, 89.85 days to the December solstice and 88.99 days back to the March equinox (Wikipedia). Grokipedia: "a shorter Northern winter of about 89 days compared to a longer Northern summer of roughly 94 days", because Earth "moves faster in its elliptical orbit" near perihelion. NASA: "by Kepler's 2nd law, the Earth moves in its orbit a little faster in winter." So the southern winter (June solstice to September equinox) is the longest season. MISCONCEPTION: the seasons come from Earth's changing distance (Wikipedia: "The seasons are not the result of the variation in Earth's distance to the Sun"); the tilt makes the seasons.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Season ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season ; https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/Skepl2A.htm ; https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/

F11. Third law: "the square of a planet's orbital period (T) is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis", published in Harmonices Mundi (1619) (Grokipedia). In words: the farther out a planet, the longer its year. Saturn: about 9.5 times Earth's distance (1,432.0 against 149.6 million km) and a period of about 29.5 years (10,747 days); orbital speed 9.7 km/s against Earth's 29.8, about a third (NASA; Grokipedia: Saturn 9.64, Earth 29.78 km/s). Saturn's orbit is nearly round (eccentricity 0.052) and tilted only 2.5 degrees to Earth's (NASA).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion ; https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Orbital_speed

F12. Orbital speed "decreases with increasing orbital radius, as the gravitational pull weakens farther from the central body"; "The mean orbital speeds of planets in the Solar System decrease with increasing distance from the Sun" (Grokipedia). OpenStax: Neptune has "a period of 165 years and an average orbital speed of just 5 kilometers per second."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Orbital_speed ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-4-orbits-in-the-solar-system

## Newton's universal gravitation

F13. Newton's law: "every particle of matter in the universe attracts every other particle" (Grokipedia), with a force "proportional to the product of their masses" and "inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers" (Wikipedia). OpenStax: "Everything with mass attracts everything else with mass anywhere in the universe"; a planet twice as far from the Sun feels a quarter of the force. Published in the Principia (1687). Ten times as far, the pull is a hundredth as strong (10 x 10 = 100).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Newton's_law_of_universal_gravitation ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_law_of_universal_gravitation ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation

F14. The Moon test: the Moon is about 60 Earth radii from Earth's centre, so under the inverse square its acceleration toward Earth should be 1/3600 of g at Earth's surface (Grokipedia Isaac Newton: "This value equals g/3600"); OpenStax: the predicted acceleration is 1/3600 of the apple's, and "This is precisely the observed acceleration of the Moon in its orbit." In numbers: an apple falls about 4.9 metres in its first second, while the Moon falls toward Earth, away from a straight path, about 0.00136 metres (1.4 mm) each second; 4.9 / 3600 = 0.00136 (Ohio State). Britannica: Newton found the Moon's acceleration 1/3,600 of that at Earth's surface and related 3,600 to the square of 60. The apple story comes second-hand, from Newton's own retelling in old age (via John Conduitt); the questions use the apple only as a falling body.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Isaac_Newton ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/thompson.1847/161/gravity.html ; https://www.britannica.com/science/gravity-physics/Newtons-law-of-gravity (search excerpt; the page refused a direct fetch) ; https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/Sgravity.htm

F15. Newton "mathematically proved that an inverse-square central force produces elliptical orbits with the sun at one focus", matching Kepler's first and second laws, and Kepler's third law follows from universal gravitation (Grokipedia Isaac Newton); Grokipedia Kepler's laws: they "arise as consequences of his second law of motion and the law of universal gravitation." Principia Book 1, Proposition 1: a central force sweeps equal areas in equal times (Grokipedia Principia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Isaac_Newton ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Philosophi%C3%A6_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica

F16. Older ideas of what moves the planets: Kepler held that the change in speed "must be the result of a force from the sun" and suggested magnetism, which likewise weakens with distance (Wikipedia Astronomia nova); Grokipedia: Kepler drew on Gilbert to "explain planetary attractions as analogous to terrestrial magnetism." Newton "systematically critiqued René Descartes' vortex theory", arguing that vortices did not explain Kepler's laws (Grokipedia Isaac Newton).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomia_nova ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Astronomia_nova ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Isaac_Newton

F17. Weighing a planet by its moons: Newton's form of the third law includes the masses, so "we can calculate the mass of the Sun by using the distances and orbital periods of the planets, or the mass of Jupiter by noting the motions of its moons" (OpenStax). In the Principia the periods of Jupiter's four large moons "yield Jupiter's mass as about 1/1,069 of the Sun's" (Grokipedia; today about 1/1,048 from NASA's 1,898 x 10^24 kg against the Sun's 1.989 x 10^30). Jupiter has about 318 times Earth's mass (NASA: 1,898 against 5.97 x 10^24 kg). Galileo found the four moons in January 1610 (F7).
    https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Philosophi%C3%A6_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica ; https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/

F18. A planet's own year tells nothing of its mass: the third law holds "with the same constant of proportionality across the solar system", and in Newton's form the planet's mass is negligible beside the Sun's (Grokipedia); OpenStax: "the tiny mass of the planets compared to the Sun is the reason that Kepler did not realize" that both masses matter. The Jupiter trojans, asteroids from metres to about 225 km across, "share Jupiter's orbit around the Sun" and have "orbital periods of about 12 years, identical to Jupiter's" (Grokipedia). MISCONCEPTION: a heavier planet moves more slowly or takes longer to go round.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Jupiter_trojan

## Halley's comet comes back late (1758-1759)

F19. The comet's period "was over a year longer between 1531 and 1607" than between 1607 and 1682; Halley "found that Jupiter and Saturn would cause the appropriate delays" (Wikipedia). Alexis Clairaut, Joseph Lalande and Nicole-Reine Lepaute, three French mathematicians, computed before the return "the attraction of Jupiter and Saturn having caused a delay of 618 days" (Wikipedia); Grokipedia: Clairaut "accounted for perturbations from Jupiter and Saturn". They predicted perihelion within about a month (Wikipedia: 13 April 1759; Grokipedia: 15 April; the date is left out); the comet reached perihelion on 13 March 1759 (both). Johann Georg Palitzsch first saw it on 25 December 1758. Wikipedia: "one of the earliest successful tests of Newtonian physics"; Grokipedia: "confirming Isaac Newton's laws of motion and universal gravitation." (The T3 unit comets-asteroids-and-moons already asks that the three comets were one; this unit does not.)
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley%27s_Comet ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Halley's_Comet ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Alexis_Clairaut

F20. Other effects on a comet: sunlight and the solar wind push a comet's tail away from the Sun (T3 unit); jets of gas from the nucleus shift Halley's perihelion timing by only about four days on average (Wikipedia). Neither comes near a 618-day delay.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley%27s_Comet

## Neptune found on paper (1846)

F21. Uranus "appeared to move too quickly before then [about 1822] and too slowly afterward" compared with Bouvard's tables, the gap reaching "around 30 arcseconds by 1835 and 70 arcseconds by 1840" (Grokipedia). The hypotheses: the Sun's gravity might act differently at so great a distance, the old observations might be in error, or "perhaps Uranus was being pulled, or perturbed, by an undiscovered planet" (Wikipedia); Grokipedia adds unseen features near Uranus, "such as an undetected ring system or additional satellites", and that Bouvard himself "favored the possibility of an exterior perturbing body."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Discovery_of_Neptune ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_Neptune

F22. Urbain Le Verrier, in Paris, worked out where the unseen planet must be and wrote to Johann Galle at the Berlin Observatory; Galle and his assistant Heinrich d'Arrest found Neptune "just after midnight" on the night of 23-24 September 1846, "less than 1 degree from the position Le Verrier had predicted" (Wikipedia); Grokipedia: "within 1° of Le Verrier's coordinates." John Couch Adams in England had worked out the same problem separately; his final prediction was about 12 degrees off (Wikipedia). Both men "greatly overestimated the planet's distance from the sun" (Wikipedia; Grokipedia's page says otherwise, so no distance figure is used).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_Neptune ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Discovery_of_Neptune

F23. What the find showed: "Newton's law of gravitation, which Airy had almost called in question, prevailed even at the limits of the Solar System" (Wikipedia); Grokipedia: the discovery "validated Isaac Newton's law of universal gravitation on an interplanetary scale."
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_Neptune ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Discovery_of_Neptune

## Mercury: where Newton's law needed Einstein

F24. Le Verrier found in 1859 that the perihelion of Mercury (the point of its orbit nearest the Sun) advances faster than Newton's law and the known planets explain: by 38 arcseconds per century in his analysis, later refined to about 43 (Grokipedia Vulcan; thatsmaths). He blamed unseen matter inside Mercury's orbit, a planet ("Vulcan") or "an unknown asteroid belt near the Sun", and his Neptune prediction, made "using the same techniques", lent the idea credibility (Wikipedia). Searches, including at the eclipse of 1860, found nothing (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vulcan_(hypothetical_planet) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_(hypothetical_planet) ; https://thatsmaths.com/2022/11/10/mercurys-mercurial-orbit/

F25. Asaph Hall suggested in 1894 changing the inverse power of distance in Newton's law from 2 to about 2.00000016, which would turn Mercury's orbit the observed extra 43 arcseconds a century (Hall 1894, Astronomical Journal 14, 49); thatsmaths calls it "an ad hoc solution", and it was abandoned. Sources differ on its effect on the other planets (arXiv 2402.04643 says it spoiled their results; thatsmaths says the effect was insignificant), so that point is not used.
    https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1894AJ.....14...49H ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04643 ; https://thatsmaths.com/2022/11/10/mercurys-mercurial-orbit/

F26. In 1915 Albert Einstein published the general theory of relativity, "showing how mass distorts spacetime" (thatsmaths); it accounts for the 43 arcseconds per century as a result of "the curvature of spacetime caused by the Sun's mass" (Wikipedia), "eliminating the need for a hypothetical planet like Vulcan" (Grokipedia). Newton's law "still continues to be used as an excellent approximation of the effects of gravity in most applications"; relativity is needed "only when there is a need for extreme accuracy" or "when dealing with very strong gravitational fields" (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vulcan_(hypothetical_planet) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_(hypothetical_planet) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_law_of_universal_gravitation ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Newton's_law_of_universal_gravitation

## Cavendish weighs the Earth (1797-1798)

F27. The torsion balance was devised by John Michell, who died in 1793 before using it. Henry Cavendish set it up in a closed shed on his estate in 1797-1798: a wooden rod about 1.8 m (six feet) long hung from a thin wire, with a small lead ball of about 0.73 kg at each end, and two lead balls of about 158 kg set near the small ones; their pull twisted the wire, and he watched the rod through telescopes from outside the shed. He published "Experiments to Determine the Density of the Earth" in 1798. Earth's pull on a small ball is its weight, so the ratio of the two pulls gave Earth's density (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cavendish_experiment ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_experiment

F28. Cavendish's result: Earth's mean density is about 5.5 times that of water (printed 5.48; Francis Baily found in 1821 an arithmetic slip, and the correct figure from his data is 5.448). The accepted value today is 5.514 g/cm³ (Wikipedia; Grokipedia 5.51). Cavendish did not compute the gravitational constant G; he gave his result as Earth's density (Wikipedia; Grokipedia). With Earth's known size, the density gives its mass, hence "weighing the Earth."
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_experiment ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cavendish_experiment

F29. What the density showed: the result "provided additional evidence for a planetary core made of metal" and is "80% higher than the density of the Earth's outer crust" (Wikipedia); Grokipedia: "a denser interior structure with heavier materials concentrated toward the core." Crust densities: continental "about 2.7 g/cm³", oceanic "approximately 3.0 g/cm³" (Grokipedia Earth's crust).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_experiment ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cavendish_experiment ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth's_crust

F30. Edmond Halley proposed in 1692 a hollow Earth: "a solid outer shell approximately 500 miles (800 km) thick" around two inner concentric shells and a central core, to explain changes in the compass; he drew on Newton's estimate of the Moon's density. Measurements of Earth's pull (Schiehallion, 1774; Cavendish, 1798) showed a low-density hollow sphere is incompatible with gravity as observed (Grokipedia Hollow Earth).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Hollow_Earth

## Misconceptions a 9th-grader brings (for honest wrong choices)

- The Sun sits at the centre of a planet's orbit; orbits are long thin ovals (F8).
- Seasons come from Earth's distance to the Sun; the tilt shortens a season (F10).
- A stronger pull near the Sun slows a planet down (F9, F10: it speeds up).
- A heavier planet is slower or takes longer to go round (F18).
- An outer planet's year is longer only because its path is longer (F11, F12: it also moves more slowly).
- Tycho used a telescope (F1, F7).
- The pull fades in simple step with distance (F13, F14: with the square).
- A planet's mass can be read from its size or brightness (F17).
- Newton's law was simply wrong; or it was perfect (F23, F26: it held to the edge of the known planets, and needed Einstein only where gravity is strong).

## Added in the revision (2026-11-27)

F31. Earth's spin barely changes through the year: "annual and semi-annual LOD variations of approximately 1 millisecond", driven by winds and ocean currents trading angular momentum with the solid Earth; the page gives no change of spin with distance from the Sun (Grokipedia, Day length fluctuations). The astronomical seasons run between the solstices and equinoxes (F10).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Day_length_fluctuations ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season

F32. February "is the second and shortest month of the Gregorian calendar, comprising 28 days in common years", 29 in leap years (Grokipedia). The northern winter runs from the December solstice to the March equinox, moments set by Earth's place on its orbit (F10), so the months' lengths do not set the season's length.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/February

F33. The Schiehallion experiment, led by Nevil Maskelyne, was conducted in 1774; Charles Hutton's analysis (1778) gave Earth a density of 4.5 to 5.0 times that of water, 24 years before Cavendish's torsion balance (Grokipedia). So Cavendish's result was not the first such estimate.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Schiehallion_experiment

F34. Cavendish's method (Wikipedia): "the gravitational force of the Earth on the small ball could be measured directly by weighing it"; with the force between the balls found from the rod's twist, "the ratio of the two forces allowed the relative density of the Earth to be calculated", using Newton's law and Earth's known radius; "the gravitational constant does not appear explicitly in Cavendish's work." Newton's law makes the pull depend on the masses only (F13), and gravity gives "identical accelerations regardless of body composition" (Grokipedia, Equivalence principle), so what a body is made of does not change its pull.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_experiment ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Equivalence_principle
