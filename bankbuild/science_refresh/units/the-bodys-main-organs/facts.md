# The Body's Main Organs: fact sheet (T1, 5th grade)

Researched 2026-11-26. Grokipedia first; KidsHealth (Nemours), Cleveland Clinic, MedlinePlus and Guinness World Records to corroborate. Lane: clinical and simple, no reproductive system; the 6th-grade unit takes the systems as systems and the body's defences against germs.

## Facts

F1 The heart is a muscular organ in the centre of the chest, between the lungs, about the size of a closed fist in an adult. It is the pump of the circulatory system: it pushes blood through the body to deliver oxygen and nutrients. https://grokipedia.com/page/Heart ("a vital muscular organ located in the center of the chest"; "roughly the size of a closed fist"; "between the lungs"; "the central pump of the circulatory system by propelling blood to deliver oxygen and nutrients throughout the body"); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/heart.html ("The heart is an organ that's a pump")

F2 With each heartbeat the heart sends blood out through the body; at rest an adult's heart beats about 60 to 100 times a minute. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/heart.html ("With each heartbeat, the heart sends blood throughout your body, carrying oxygen to every cell"; "usually beating about 60 to 100 times per minute"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Heart ("60 to 100 times per minute at rest")

F3 The pulse is the throb of blood pushed out by each heartbeat, felt in an artery near the skin; the commonest place to feel it is the wrist (the radial artery). A resting pulse is about 70 to 110 beats a minute for children aged 6 to 12. https://grokipedia.com/page/Pulse ("the rhythmic ejection of blood by the left ventricle into the aorta during each cardiac cycle"; "can be palpated at superficial arteries"; "the radial artery at the wrist"; "70-110 bpm for ages 6-12 years")

F4 Blood cells are made in the marrow inside bones, not in the heart. https://grokipedia.com/page/Blood ("All blood cells originate from hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow"); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/bones-muscles-joints.html (bone marrow: "this soft bone is where most of the body's blood cells are made")

F5 Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back to the heart. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/heart.html ("Arteries carry blood away from the heart, and veins carry blood back to the heart"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Blood

F6 Blood carries oxygen and nutrients (digested food) to the body's cells, and carries away carbon dioxide and other wastes. https://grokipedia.com/page/Blood ("delivering oxygen and nutrients to cells while removing metabolic waste products such as carbon dioxide"); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/heart.html ("carries oxygen, nutrients, and hormones to cells, and removes waste products, like carbon dioxide")

F7 The lungs take oxygen from breathed-in air into the blood, and pass carbon dioxide from the blood into the air breathed out. The swap happens across the thin walls of tiny air sacs (alveoli). Air reaches the lungs through the nose or mouth, the windpipe (trachea) and the bronchi. https://grokipedia.com/page/Lung ("facilitate the intake of oxygen from inhaled air into the bloodstream and the expulsion of carbon dioxide from the blood into the exhaled air"; "Gas exchange occurs across the thin alveolar walls"); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/lungs.html ("takes in oxygen and sends out carbon dioxide"; trachea, bronchi, alveoli)

F8 Carbon dioxide is a waste gas that the body's cells make as they do their work: cells break down sugar from food with oxygen, giving carbon dioxide and water. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/lungs.html ("As cells do their work, they make carbon dioxide"; "carbon dioxide (a waste product)"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Cellular_respiration ("the breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen, producing carbon dioxide and water as byproducts")

F9 Breathed-in air is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Breathed-out air is still about 78% nitrogen, its oxygen is down to about 16%, and its carbon dioxide is up to 4-5%. So nitrogen goes in and comes out in about the same amount. https://grokipedia.com/page/Exhalation ("nitrogen at approximately 78%"; "oxygen reduced to about 16% from the 21% in inhaled air"; "carbon dioxide elevated to 4-5%")

F10 The trace of hydrogen in breath is made by bacteria in the gut fermenting undigested food, not by the body's own cells as they work. https://grokipedia.com/page/Hydrogen_breath_test ("produced primarily by fermentative bacteria"; undigested carbohydrates "fermented by resident anaerobic bacteria")

F11 Swallowed food goes down the esophagus (food pipe), a muscular tube, to the stomach. The stomach is a hollow, muscular, J-shaped organ whose walls stretch to hold a meal. Its muscles churn and mix the food with digestive juices containing hydrochloric acid and enzymes, turning it into a thick semi-liquid (chyme), which passes on to the small intestine. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/digestive.html ("a soft, muscular tube that moves food from the pharynx to the stomach"; "The stomach muscles churn and mix the food with digestive juices that have acids and enzymes"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Stomach ("a J-shaped, hollow, muscular organ"; gastric juice is mainly "hydrochloric acid (HCl), pepsinogen, mucus..."; chyme, "a viscous, semi-liquid mixture of partially digested food particles"; released "through the pyloric sphincter into the duodenum")

F12 The stomach absorbs very little (some alcohol, a little water); most nutrients are absorbed in the small intestine. https://grokipedia.com/page/Stomach ("The stomach plays a limited role in nutrient and substance absorption")

F13 The small intestine runs from the stomach to the large intestine and is where most digested food is absorbed: its lining has tiny finger-like villi, through which nutrients pass into the blood. It absorbs over 90% of the nutrients. It is a long coiled tube (about 6-7 m measured after death, about 3-5 m in a living adult), called small for its width, not its length. https://grokipedia.com/page/Small_intestine ("over 90% of nutrients and water"; "from the pylorus of the stomach to the ileocecal valve, where it connects to the large intestine"; villi; lengths); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/digestive.html ("The villi are the vehicles through which nutrients can be absorbed into the blood"; "called small because of its width, not its length")

F14 The large intestine takes water out of the leftover, undigested matter and forms it into solid waste. https://grokipedia.com/page/Large_intestine ("primarily functions to absorb water and electrolytes"; "receives undigested material from the small intestine"); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/digestive.html ("The large intestine's main job is to remove water from the undigested matter and form solid waste")

F15 The liver is the largest organ inside the body (about 1.4-1.8 kg; Guinness: about 1.5 kg), and it makes bile. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/21481-liver ("Your liver is the largest internal organ in your body"; "between 3 and 5 pounds"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Liver ("the largest solid organ in the human body"; "between 1.4 kg in females and 1.8 kg in males"; bile); https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/83791-largest-human-organ ("largest internal organ", about 1.5 kg)

F16 The brain is the body's control centre ("command center"): it takes in news from the senses and sends orders to the muscles. Nerves are cord-like bundles of fibres that carry these signals between the brain or spinal cord and every part of the body: sensory nerves carry signals in from the senses, motor nerves carry orders out to the muscles. https://grokipedia.com/page/Brain ("It serves as the body's command center, processing sensory input, coordinating voluntary movements"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Nerve ("a cordlike organ of the peripheral nervous system composed of multiple bundles of axons"; sensory and motor nerves); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/brain-nervous-system.html ("a central computer that controls all the body's functions"; "threadlike nerves that branch out to every organ and body part")

F17 The spinal cord, the pathway for messages between the brain and the body, is protected by the backbone (spine, spinal column). https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/bones-muscles-joints.html ("The spinal cord, a pathway for messages between the brain and the body, is protected by the backbone, or spinal column"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Spinal_cord ("protected by the bony vertebral column")

F18 Tendons are cords that join muscles to bones and pass on a muscle's pull; ligaments are straps that join bone to bone. Veins are blood vessels (F5). https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/bones-muscles-joints.html ("Skeletal muscle is attached by cord-like tendons to bone"; "Bones are fastened to other bones by long, fibrous straps called ligaments"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Tendon ("to transmit the forces generated by muscle contraction"; "Unlike ligaments, which connect bone to bone")

F19 Bones hold the body up and give it its shape, and some protect soft organs: the skull (22 bones joined together) protects the brain; the ribs (12 pairs) form a cage around the heart and lungs; the backbone guards the spinal cord (F17); the pelvis shelters the bladder and part of the gut. An adult has about 206 bones. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/bones-muscles-joints.html ("Bones provide support for our bodies and help form our shape"; "The skull protects the brain"; "The ribs form a cage that shelters the heart and lungs"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Skull ("encloses and protects the brain"; "22 bones"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Rib_cage (12 pairs; "safeguarding vital organs such as the heart, lungs"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Human_skeleton ("approximately 206 bones in adults"; pelvis protects "the bladder, and portions of the digestive tract")

F20 A muscle moves a bone by contracting: it gets shorter and pulls on the bone through its tendon. Muscles can pull bones but cannot push them, so they work in pairs: the biceps, at the front of the upper arm, bends the elbow, and the triceps, at the back, straightens it. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/bones-muscles-joints.html ("Muscles can pull bones, but they can't push them back to the original position. So they work in pairs of flexors and extensors"; "the biceps muscle, in the front of the upper arm, is a flexor"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Skeletal_muscle (contraction "shortens the muscle fiber"; force passed through tendons); https://grokipedia.com/page/Biceps ("The biceps brachii contributes to elbow flexion"; "opposed by the antagonist triceps brachii, which extends the elbow")

F21 The skin is the body's largest organ, covering the whole outside of the body. An adult's skin weighs about 3.6-3.8 kg and covers about 1.5-2 square metres; the liver, the largest organ inside the body, weighs about 1.5 kg. https://grokipedia.com/page/Skin ("The skin is the body's largest organ, covering its entire external surface"); https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/skin.html ("Skin is the body's largest organ"); https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/83791-largest-human-organ ("Skin is the largest organ in a human body"; about 3.8 kg, 1.5-2 sq m; liver about 1.5 kg); https://medlineplus.gov/ency/anatomyvideos/000029.htm (about 6 pounds of skin covering 18 square feet, "making skin the body's largest organ")

F22 The skin covers and protects the body: it keeps germs out, keeps the body's water from drying away, feels touch, heat, cold and pain, and helps control body heat by sweating. https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/skin.html ("forms a barrier that prevents harmful substances and germs from entering the body"; "prevents water that's inside our bodies from evaporating"; nerve endings "let us feel warmth, cold, pain"; sweat glands control temperature); https://grokipedia.com/page/Skin ("a dynamic barrier that protects against pathogens"; "regulates body temperature through sweat production")

F23 For size: an adult brain weighs about 1.4 kg and an adult heart about 250-350 g; an empty stomach is much smaller still than the skin or the liver. https://grokipedia.com/page/Brain ("weighing about 1.4 kg (3 lb)"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Heart ("approximately 250 to 350 grams"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Stomach (a J-shaped hollow organ holding about 1 to 4 litres when filled)

F24 Carbon monoxide is a different, poisonous gas, made chiefly when fuels such as gasoline, natural gas, wood and coal burn incompletely (faulty furnaces, stoves, fireplaces, engines); it clings to the blood's oxygen carrier far more strongly than oxygen does. The body makes only a trace of it, from breaking down old blood pigment (heme), not from using food for energy. https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_monoxide ("the incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels such as gasoline, natural gas, wood, and coal"; "malfunctioning furnaces, water heaters, stoves, fireplaces"; affinity for hemoglobin "approximately 200-250 times greater than oxygen"; "endogenously produced in mammals primarily through the enzymatic degradation of heme by heme oxygenase", about 450 micromoles a day)

## Misconceptions a 5th grader may hold (for honest wrong choices)

M1 About the heart, among students in grades 3-5: "Blood is produced in the heart", "The function of the heart is to clean blood", "The heart produces necessary energy for the body". Özgür (2013), International Journal of Environmental & Science Education, via SCIMAP: https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=722. Corrected by F1, F4, F6, F8.

M2 Muscles push as well as pull. KidsHealth corrects it directly ("Muscles can pull bones, but they can't push them", F20); teaching resources list "muscles can pull and push" as a misconception (UK revision and teaching sites found by search; author's judgment that it holds in grade 5).

M3 Food is taken into the body in the stomach. Author's judgment (no survey found); corrected by F12 and F13.

M4 The liver (the biggest organ a student can picture inside the body) is the largest organ, and the skin is not an organ at all. Author's judgment; Guinness names the skin the largest organ and the liver the largest internal organ (F15, F21).

M5 Mixing up which gas goes in and which comes out, or thinking the body uses the nitrogen in air. Author's judgment; corrected by F7, F8, F9.

M6 Other mix-ups of jobs between parts: blood carrying air or messages, tendons or veins carrying the brain's messages. Author's judgment; each part's real job is in F5, F7, F16, F18.

## Notes and cautions

- Do not say the heart "cleans" the blood or "makes" blood (M1); other organs clean it and the marrow makes it.
- Do not say the ribs guard only the heart and lungs as if nothing else: the lower ribs also shelter part of the upper belly. No question asks what the ribs guard.
- Do not put the lungs in a "largest organ" question: their air sacs have a huge inner surface, which a well-read student could defend. Keep the comparison to weight (skin about 3.8 kg vs liver about 1.5 kg).
- Do not use "water vapour" as a wrong answer for the waste gas: breath does carry water vapour, and cells make water. Hydrogen was set aside too: a trace of it, made by gut bacteria, is carried in the blood and breathed out (F10). Carbon monoxide is the wrong answer used, and the stem ties the waste gas to cells breaking down food for energy, which CO2 is and CO is not: the body's trace of CO comes from breaking down heme (F24).
- The small intestine's length differs by source (3-5 m living, 6-7 m after death): give no number.
- The small intestine also takes in most of the water, so do not offer "takes in water" as a wrong answer for it; the large intestine's distinct job is forming solid waste (F14).
