## Pure substances: elements and compounds

F1. A pure substance has a constant composition and distinct properties that stay the same whatever its origin or however it was made; pure substances are of two kinds, elements and compounds. Oak's KS3 lesson: "A pure substance contains only one type of chemical", and "A pure material has a fixed melting point and boiling point."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_substance ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/solutions/lessons/pure-substances

F2. An element is a pure substance made of only one type of atom, and it cannot be broken down or converted into other substances by chemical means.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_substance ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_element

F3. A compound is two or more different elements chemically bonded together in a fixed ratio. It can be broken down into its elements only by chemical reactions, not by physical separation.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_compound ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_substance

F4. A compound has physical and chemical properties that differ from those of the elements it is made of (Grokipedia's example: soft, explosive sodium and poisonous chlorine make table salt; that example belongs to the T3 unit and is not used here).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_compound

F5. Ancient Greek thinkers held that matter was made of four elements: earth, water, air and fire (Empedocles' four "roots", systematised by Aristotle). Antoine Lavoisier's work of 1789 demoted all four from elemental status.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Classical_element

F6. Electrolysis: a direct electric current passed through water splits it into its constituent gases, hydrogen (at one wire) and oxygen (at the other), with twice as much hydrogen as oxygen by volume. William Nicholson and Anthony Carlisle first did it with a battery in May 1800. Water is the compound H2O, of hydrogen and oxygen.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Electrolysis_of_water ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen

F7. Boiling and freezing change only water's state. The bubbles in boiling water are water vapour (water as a gas), not hydrogen and oxygen gases split from the water.
    https://www.nsta.org/science-scope/science-scope-januaryfebruary-2021/capitalizing-student-misconceptions ; https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article

F8. Charcoal is predominantly elemental carbon (80-95 percent carbon for wood charcoal), a lightweight, black, porous solid that burns; burning it gives off carbon dioxide.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Charcoal

F9. Most fires need three things: fuel, heat and oxygen. Oxygen is a colourless gas that strongly supports burning: Scheele called it "fire air", and Priestley found a candle burned brighter in it than in ordinary air.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Fire_triangle ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Oxygen

F10. Carbon dioxide is a compound of carbon and oxygen: a colourless, odourless, nonflammable gas. It is widely used in fire extinguishers, which put fires out by displacing the oxygen (smothering). Animals, people included, breathe it out.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Carbon_dioxide ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Fire_triangle

## Mixtures: homogeneous and heterogeneous

F11. A mixture is a physical combination of two or more substances with no chemical bonding between them and no fixed proportions; each component keeps its own properties, and a mixture can be separated by physical methods such as filtration, evaporation, magnetism and distillation.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Mixture ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_compound

F12. A homogeneous mixture has a uniform composition throughout (examples: air, salt water, and alloys such as brass, a solid solution of copper and zinc). A heterogeneous mixture's composition varies from one region to another, and its distinct parts are often visible (examples: sand mixed with water; granite).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Mixture ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_substance ; https://teksguide.org/teks/s66b-0/alignments

F13. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc, with zinc generally 5 to 40 percent by weight; alpha brass is "a single solid solution of zinc in face-centered cubic copper". It has a golden-yellow colour and is used for wind instruments. Grokipedia's Alloy page calls brass "a copper-zinc mixture", and an alloy "a mixture composed of two or more elements, at least one of which is a metal".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Brass ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Alloy ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mixture

F14. Copper is a chemical element. Distilled water is made by boiling water and condensing the steam in a separate container; the salts and minerals stay behind, and the water is essentially free of impurities (a pure compound).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Copper ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Distilled_water

F15. Granite is a coarse-grained igneous rock made mainly of quartz, feldspar and mica; its individual mineral crystals are visible to the naked eye, giving it a speckled look (pink, red, grey or white, with darker flecks of mica).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Granite ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mixture

F16. Soil is a mixture of mineral particles from weathered rock, organic matter, water and air (roughly 45 percent minerals, 5 percent organic matter, 25 percent water and 25 percent air by volume). Its mineral grains are sorted by size: sand 0.05-2 mm, silt 0.002-0.05 mm, clay smaller than 0.002 mm.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Soil

F17. The soil jar test: soil shaken hard with water in a jar and left undisturbed for 2 to 3 days settles in layers by grain size. Sand, the largest, settles in about a minute and lies at the bottom; silt settles in about two hours and lies above it; clay, the smallest, settles last (typically 1 to 3 days) and forms the top layer, under clear water.
    https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/estimating-soil-texture-sandy-loamy-or-clayey/ ; https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/soil-texture-analysis-the-jar-test/

F18. Sand is mostly quartz (silicon dioxide), often 90-95 percent or more of continental sands, and it has negligible solubility in water.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Sand ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Quartz

## Telling substances apart and separating mixtures

F19. Pure water boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level; a substance's normal boiling point is "a key physical characteristic for identifying and characterizing pure substances". Once boiling, a liquid takes in heat and vaporises without its temperature rising further.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Boiling_point ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/solutions/lessons/pure-substances

F20. Dissolving a nonvolatile solute such as salt raises water's boiling point, and the rise grows with the concentration: a solution of 1 mole of sodium chloride per kilogram of water boils at about 101.02 degrees Celsius. As the water boils away and the solution grows more concentrated, the boiling point rises. Oak's KS3 lesson: "Impurities change the melting and boiling point of a material", and "Mixtures melt or boil over a range of temperatures."
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Boiling-point_elevation ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Boiling_point ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/solutions/lessons/pure-substances

F21. Seawater holds about 35 grams of dissolved salts per kilogram (3.5 percent), mostly sodium chloride. At sea level it boils at about 100.6 degrees Celsius (an engineering property table gives 100.56 degrees at 35 g/kg; the boiling-point-elevation rule gives about 100.6).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Seawater ; https://jehall.com/files/ww/engineering-standards/6-sea-water-physical-properties-02-006-01-iss-06-19.pdf ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Boiling-point_elevation

F22. Distillation separates the parts of a liquid mixture by their different boiling points: the mixture is heated so the part with the lower boiling point turns to vapour, and the vapour is cooled in a condenser (a tube kept cold, usually by cold water) back into a liquid that is collected. In the simple distillation of a solution such as ink or copper sulfate solution, only the water distils off, and the dissolved substance stays behind in the flask (RSC: "it is only water that distils off when a solution is boiled").
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Distillation ; https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/recovering-water-from-copperii-sulfate-solution/1768.article ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/separation-techniques/lessons/simple-distillation ; https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/lessons/distillation-separating-a-mixture-of-inks

F23. Gold panning is gravity separation: sediment in a pan is dipped under water and swirled, and the moving water washes away the lighter sand and gravel while the heavier gold settles to the bottom. Gold is about 19 times denser than water (19.3 grams per cubic centimetre); quartz, the main mineral of sand, is 2.65.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gold_panning ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Gold ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Quartz

F24. Gold is among the softest pure metals (Mohs hardness 2.5); quartz is much harder (Mohs hardness 7).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gold ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Quartz

F25. Pure gold is not magnetic: it is weakly diamagnetic and, if anything, slightly repelled by a magnet. In normal conditions only iron, cobalt and nickel, and alloys made from them such as steel, are magnetic.
    https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/378 ; https://spark.iop.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/BEST%20Diagnostic%20questions%20for%20Rules%20for%20magnetism_0.doc

F26. Spring water is groundwater, and groundwater picks up dissolved minerals (calcium, magnesium, bicarbonate; sodium and chloride in some places) from the rock it flows through. USGS: "as water moves through soil and rock it dissolves small amounts of naturally-occurring minerals." Mineral water "contains naturally dissolved minerals and trace elements" from contact with rock.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Spring_(hydrology) ; https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mineral_water

## Misconceptions (honest wrong choices)

M1. Pupils take the everyday sense of "pure" (pure orange juice, pure spring water) for a pure substance; everyday "pure" means one source or nothing added, while the chemist's means one substance.
    https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/solutions/lessons/pure-substances ; https://edu.rsc.org/feature/a-question-of-substances/3009905.article

M2. "A pure substance is something that occurs in nature" is a documented misconception (students call natural rock salt pure, and the processed sodium chloride from it not).
    https://edu.rsc.org/lesson-plans/what-do-chemical-words-mean-exploring-key-terms-14-16-years/85.article

M3. Many students think the bubbles in boiling water are hydrogen and oxygen split from the water (about 25 percent at age 12, about 40 percent at 17, in a New Zealand study).
    https://www.nsta.org/science-scope/science-scope-januaryfebruary-2021/capitalizing-student-misconceptions ; https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article

M4. Students confuse compounds with mixtures: about 39 percent of 15-year-olds judged a compound's particle diagram to be a mixture (Briggs and Holding, 1986), and about half took any diagram with different atoms in it for a mixture.
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-differences-between-elements-compounds-and-mixtures-beyond-appearances/4017771.article

M5. Students commonly believe that all metals are attracted to magnets.
    https://science.nasa.gov/eclips/videos/magnets-and-metals ; https://spark.iop.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/BEST%20Diagnostic%20questions%20for%20Rules%20for%20magnetism_0.doc

M6. Author's judgment, not sourced: students often think only an element counts as "pure", so that a compound such as water cannot be a pure substance (the definition in F1 says otherwise); that a dissolved solid leaves water's boiling point unchanged because "only the water boils"; that the cold tube of a still catches the dye like a filter; and that heavy, sticky clay sinks first in a soil jar.

## Added in the revision (2026-11-26)

F28. A breath out is mostly nitrogen: inhaled air is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and 0.04% carbon dioxide; in exhaled air "oxygen levels drop to around 16%, while carbon dioxide rises to approximately 4%", with nitrogen "largely unchanged at 78%". So carbon dioxide is one of the gases a person breathes out, about 4 parts in 100.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Breathing ; https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/biology/cie/23/revision-notes/9-human-gas-exchange/9-1-human-gas-exchange/investigating-the-differences-in-inspired-and-expired-air

F29. Salt itself boils far above water: Grokipedia gives sodium chloride a melting point of 800.7 degrees Celsius and "The boiling point of sodium chloride is 1465 °C".
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium_chloride

F30. In the simple distillation of ink or copper sulfate solution, "it is only water that distils off when a solution is boiled"; the liquid collected is colourless, and "It may be possible to detect a darkening of the original solution" left in the flask as it grows more concentrated (RSC; "ink was the traditional one").
    https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/recovering-water-from-copperii-sulfate-solution/1768.article

F31. The gold a panner recovers is mostly "small amounts of fine gold dust or flakes"; a session yields "a few grains to 0.05g", and "large nuggets (1g+) are extremely rare" (Grokipedia Gold panning). A pebble is a grain of gravel "4 to 64 millimetres" across (Grokipedia Pebble); the smallest quartz pebble, 4 mm across, has a mass of about 0.09 grams (its volume, about 0.034 cubic centimetres, times quartz's 2.65 g/cm3 from F23). So a tiny gold flake weighs far less than a pebble of gravel: gold stays in the pan because it is denser, not because each flake is heavier.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gold_panning ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Pebble ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Quartz
