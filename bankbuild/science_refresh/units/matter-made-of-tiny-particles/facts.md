# Matter Made of Tiny Particles: fact sheet (T1, 5th grade)

Researched 2026-11-25. Grokipedia first; Wikipedia, NGSS, and science-education research to corroborate.
Map line G5.P2 (NGSS 5-PS1-1).

## Facts

F1 Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. https://grokipedia.com/page/Matter ("any substance that has mass and occupies space"); https://pengi.ai/skills/what-is-matter-macro-view-504d4dac-08b4-4b47-96e9-acad2ae16822 (Amplify Science, Grade 5: "Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space.")

F2 All ordinary matter is made of atoms: they are its basic building blocks. https://grokipedia.com/page/Matter ("At its most fundamental level, matter is composed of atoms."); https://grokipedia.com/page/Atom ("Atoms form the basis of all ordinary matter in the universe."); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom ("the fundamental building blocks of matter")

F3 Air is matter: the air in a room is one of matter's common forms (a gas). https://grokipedia.com/page/Matter (lists "the air in a room (gas)" as matter); https://pengi.ai/skills/what-is-matter-macro-view-504d4dac-08b4-4b47-96e9-acad2ae16822 (Amplify Grade 5: "Air is matter because it has mass (you can weigh it) and takes up space (you can compress it in a syringe).")

F4 Atoms are extremely small: about 0.1 to 0.5 nanometres across (around 100 picometres); a human hair is about a million carbon atoms wide. https://grokipedia.com/page/Atom ("The diameter of an atom typically ranges from 0.1 to 0.5 nanometers."); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom ("a typical human hair is about 1 million carbon atoms in width")

F5 Atoms are too small to be seen with an ordinary (optical, light) microscope: such microscopes can show nothing smaller than about 200-250 nanometres, and atoms are thousands of times smaller than the wavelengths of light. Special instruments such as the scanning tunnelling microscope can map atoms on a surface. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom ("they cannot be viewed using an optical microscope"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Optical_microscope ("fundamentally limited to about 200-250 nanometers for visible light"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Atom (scanning tunneling microscopy)

F6 The 5th-grade particle model: all matter is made of particles too small to see, even with a microscope; there is empty space between the particles. https://horizon-research.com/ASSET/?p=379 (grade 5, NGSS PS1.A: "all matter is made of particles that are too small to see, even with a microscope"; "there is empty space between those particles")

F7 NGSS 5-PS1-1 (grade 5): "Develop a model to describe that matter is made of particles too small to be seen." Its evidence examples: adding air to expand a basketball, compressing air in a syringe, dissolving sugar in water, and evaporating salt water. https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/5-ps1-1-matter-and-its-interactions

F8 NGSS PS1.A, grade 5: matter of any type can be divided into particles too small to see, but the matter still exists and can be detected by other means; a model of gases as particles too small to see, moving freely around in space, explains the inflation and shape of a balloon. https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/5-ps1-1-matter-and-its-interactions

F9 Air has mass: dry air at sea level has a density of about 1.2 kilograms per cubic metre. https://grokipedia.com/page/Density_of_air ("the density of dry air is 1.225 kg/m³" at 15 C)

F10 A basketball weighed flat and then pumped full of air has more mass when inflated, which shows that gas is matter, with mass and taking up space. https://www.howtosmile.org/node/2476 ("The inflated ball has the greater mass so learners can conclude that gas is matter because it has mass and takes up space."); https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/5-ps1-1-matter-and-its-interactions (adding air to expand a basketball)

F11 A gas can be squeezed into a smaller space (it is highly compressible) because its particles are widely separated; the tightly packed particles of liquids and solids resist squeezing. So sealed air in a syringe can be pushed into less space, and water hardly at all. https://grokipedia.com/page/Gas (particles "widely separated"; "high compressibility"; "the tightly packed molecules in liquids and solids" resist squeezing); https://pengi.ai/skills/what-is-matter-macro-view-504d4dac-08b4-4b47-96e9-acad2ae16822 (Amplify Grade 5: compress air in a syringe)

F12 A smell comes from tiny particles (volatile chemical compounds) carried in the air; they enter the nose and are sensed by receptors there. https://grokipedia.com/page/Odor ("stimulation of olfactory receptors by volatile chemical compounds"; "volatile odorant molecules enter the nasal cavity via inhalation")

F13 A flower's scent is made of compounds given off mainly by its petals; they turn to vapour easily and can be sensed some distance away. Roses' scents draw bees. https://grokipedia.com/page/Floral_scent ("emission of volatile organic compounds"; "emitted primarily from floral tissues such as petals"; "detection over distances"; "the scents emitted by roses ... effectively draw bees")

F14 Particles spread out by themselves from where they are crowded to where they are few (diffusion), driven by their own random motion: perfume vapour spreads through the still air of a closed room, and a drop of ink spreads through still water. https://grokipedia.com/page/Diffusion ("the slow permeation of perfume vapor through still air in a closed room"; "an ink droplet within a beaker of quiescent water")

F15 When salt or sugar dissolves in water, it makes a solution: its particles spread evenly through the water, down at the level of molecules and ions, and no bits of the solid can be seen. https://grokipedia.com/page/Solution_(chemistry) ("solute particles are uniformly dispersed at the molecular or ionic level"; "no distinct particles of the original substances can be observed"; sodium chloride and sugar in water as examples)

F16 Dissolving is not melting. Salt (sodium chloride) dissolves when its particles (sodium and chloride ions) separate from the crystal into the water; salt melts only at 800.7 degrees Celsius. https://grokipedia.com/page/Sodium_chloride ("the separation of Na+ and Cl- ions from the ionic lattice"; "a melting point of 800.7 °C")

F17 Seawater is clear yet holds about 35 grams of dissolved salts in every kilogram (3.5 percent), mostly sodium chloride. When it evaporates, fresh water vapour leaves and the salts stay behind. https://grokipedia.com/page/Seawater ("35 grams of dissolved salts per kilogram of seawater (3.5 percent by weight)"; "dominated by sodium and chloride ions"; evaporation "removes freshwater vapor and concentrates salts")

F18 When seawater is left in shallow ponds, the Sun and wind evaporate the water and the salt is left behind as crystals on the pond floor; when a drop of salty liquid such as sweat dries, the dissolved solids stay as a residue. https://grokipedia.com/page/Salt_evaporation_pond ("sunlight and wind gradually remove water"; "sodium chloride crystals that settle on the pond floor"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Evaporation (sweat droplets "fully evaporate, leaving behind residue from the solutes")

F19 Light is not matter: the photon, the particle of light, has no mass. Grade 5 science teaches that light, heat and sound are not matter because they have no mass and take up no space. https://grokipedia.com/page/Photon ("a massless, chargeless elementary particle"); https://pengi.ai/skills/what-is-matter-macro-view-504d4dac-08b4-4b47-96e9-acad2ae16822 (Amplify Grade 5: "Light, heat, and sound are not matter because they don't have mass or take up space.")

F20 Steam and smoke are matter. True steam is water as a gas, an invisible gas of water molecules; the white cloud usually called steam is microscopic drops of liquid water. Smoke is solid particles, liquid droplets and gases. https://grokipedia.com/page/Steam ("Steam is the gaseous phase of water"; "aerosols of microscopic liquid water droplets"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Smoke ("a visible aerosol consisting of solid particles, liquid droplets, and gases")

F21 A cell is the basic living unit of every known living thing, the "building block of life" (so rocks, water and air are not made of cells). https://grokipedia.com/page/Cell_(biology) ("the fundamental structural, functional, and biological unit of all known living organisms")

F22 A rock is a natural solid made of one or more minerals, and minerals have their atoms in an ordered arrangement, often as crystals (so crystals are themselves made of atoms). https://grokipedia.com/page/Rock_(geology) ("a naturally occurring solid aggregate of one or more minerals"; minerals have an "ordered atomic arrangement, often manifesting as single crystals")

F23 A germ is a living thing too small to see with the eye alone that can cause disease (a pathogen, "simply a germ"). https://grokipedia.com/page/Microorganism ("a microscopic organism too small to be seen with the naked eye"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen ("A pathogen may also be referred to as an infectious agent, or simply a germ.")

## Misconceptions students of this grade hold

M1 Air has no weight, or air makes things lighter: TERC's grade 5 concept cartoon on a deflated leather ball gives three children's claims (air makes things lighter; air adds weight; air weighs nothing). Research summarised by the Royal Society of Chemistry finds students who think gases have no mass, so a tyre's mass would not change when air is pumped in; in an Australian test 74% of Year 3 students chose "air has no mass and/or volume" for a balloon balance. https://inquiryproject.terc.edu/curriculum/curriculum5/concept_cartoon/air_has_weight ; https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article ; https://www.acer.org/au/news/article/three-science-concepts-primary-students-struggle-to-grasp

M2 Only solids and liquids count as matter, not air, steam or smoke (things that cannot be seen, felt and touched); other children stretch matter to include heat and light. https://inquiryproject.terc.edu/research/inquiry_project/matter-and-materials.cfm.html

M3 Dissolving is the same as melting ("Melting and dissolving are used frequently as synonyms"). https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article

M4 Matter that disappears from sight stops existing (sugar dissolving in water); 44% of 14-year-olds think a dissolved solute "disappears". Children also think a property such as sweetness or a smell can go on existing without the matter. https://edu.rsc.org/resources/states-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017762.article

M5 Water that evaporates from a dish "went into the plate", or soaks into the pan. https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article

M6 Matter is continuous, one solid stuff with no particles; or the gaps between particles are filled with air, dust or other gases (Novick and Nussbaum); particles take on the properties of the substance. https://edu.rsc.org/resources/states-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017762.article ; https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article

M7 A smell spreads because it "reacts with the air", making the air itself smell (a diagnostic wrong statement in the University of York BEST deodorant probe). https://maken.wikiwijs.nl/bestanden/1307075/X%20BEST_BCL_1_4_Diagnostic_Deodorant.docx

M8 Students confuse atoms and cells. https://edu.rsc.org/resources/chemical-misconceptions-i-chemical-structure/1139.article ("confuse atoms and cells")

## Added in the revision (2026-11-25)

F24 A syringe that looks empty is full of air. With a finger held firmly over its tip, the plunger goes in only part of the way, which is evidence that air is something and takes up space; with the tip open, the plunger is pushed in and the air coming out can be felt. Grade 5 units use this to show that air takes up space. https://www.horizon-research.com/ASSET/?p=1067 (Student Experience 2, "Air is Something": "put a finger firmly on the tip of the syringe and then try to push in the plunger"; "Air coming out of the syringe feels like something, not nothing"; evidence that air takes up space); https://inquiryproject.terc.edu/curriculum/curriculum5/4_air/inv_13/index.html (Inquiry Project, grade 5, "Air, a Gas": syringes "let them experience a situation in which air clearly takes up space"); https://pengi.ai/skills/what-is-matter-macro-view-504d4dac-08b4-4b47-96e9-acad2ae16822 (Amplify Grade 5: air "takes up space (you can compress it in a syringe)")

M9 A child who thinks a syringe is empty blames the syringe itself (a sticking plunger, gripping walls) or "suction" for the stop. Author's judgment, from M2 (air is not counted as matter); no survey found.
