## Earthquake waves

F1. P-waves are compressional: the particles move back and forth along the direction the wave travels, and P-waves travel through solids, liquids and gases. S-waves move the particles perpendicular to the wave's path, shearing the material; liquids and gases "cannot sustain the shear stresses required for propagation", so S-waves cannot cross them. In the crust S-waves travel at roughly 60 percent of the speed of P-waves.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/S_wave ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Shadow_zone (a liquid's shear modulus is zero, so the S-wave speed, the square root of shear modulus over density, is zero)

F2. Shadow zones: no direct S-waves are recorded beyond about 103-105 degrees from an earthquake's epicentre, because they cannot pass through the liquid outer core; this "provided key evidence for the core's fluid nature". P-waves slow sharply where they enter the outer core (about 13.7 km/s in the lowest mantle to about 8 km/s in the outer core) and are refracted, leaving a P-wave shadow zone between about 104 and 140 degrees (the article also gives 103-142); weak diffracted P-waves are still detected in it. The solid mantle above the core carries S-waves.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Shadow_zone ; https://grokipedia.com/page/S_wave ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_outer_core ("seismic shear-waves are not transmitted through the outer core")

F3. Who found the core (sources differ on who first called it liquid, so no question names him): Richard Dixon Oldham (1906) gave "the first direct evidence of its existence and size", a dense core about 0.4 of Earth's diameter, from a break in S-wave travel times near 120 degrees (Wikipedia); Grokipedia credits him with the S-wave shadow and a liquid core. Beno Gutenberg located the core-mantle boundary at about 2,900 km depth in 1913 (Grokipedia, EBSCO; the brief's 1914 is not in these sources). Harold Jeffreys (1926, "The Rigidity of the Earth's Central Core") showed from Earth tides and seismic data that the core is far less rigid than the mantle and could be liquid (search summary; Wikipedia cites the paper).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dixon_Oldham ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth's_outer_core ; https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/earth-and-atmospheric-sciences/gutenberg-discovers-earths-mantle-outer-core

F4. Andrija Mohorovičić studied the earthquake of 8 October 1909 in the Kupa Valley (epicentre near Pokupsko, about 40 km south-east of Zagreb, Croatia), collecting records from 41 stations. Up to about 240 km from the epicentre only one longitudinal (P) and one transverse (S) wave arrived; farther out (240-720 km) two of each arrived, and beyond 240 km the new phase (Pa) was "the first to reach the stations" although its path was longer. He explained that wave speed jumps suddenly at a certain depth, so rays bent at the boundary travel faster through the layer below: P and Pa are "of the same type" and differ "only because they reach the surface of the Earth by different paths". He put the boundary at about 54 km depth, with P-waves at about 6 km/s above it and about 8 km/s below (Grokipedia; Wikipedia gives 6.7-7.2 above and 7.6-8.6 km/s below). He reasoned from wave speed depending on the material's density (Wikipedia).
    https://www.geotech.hr/en/discovery-of-mohorovicics-moho-discontinuity/ ; https://geoera.eu/blog/croatian-earthquakes-moho/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Andrija_Mohorovičić ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohorovi%C4%8Di%C4%87_discontinuity

F5. The Mohorovičić discontinuity (the Moho) is the base of the crust: about 5-10 km below the ocean floor and 20-90 km (average about 35 km) below the continents.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohorovi%C4%8Di%C4%87_discontinuity

F6. Inge Lehmann (Danish, 13 May 1888 - 21 February 1993) studied the records of the 1929 earthquake in New Zealand (Wikipedia: the Murchison earthquake, South Island; Grokipedia: the Buller earthquake, the same event). Stations at Sverdlovsk and Irkutsk in Russia, inside the shadow zone, "recorded unusually large P-wave amplitudes"; "if Earth's core were entirely liquid, these stations should not have detected these waves" (Earth Magazine). In her 1936 paper "P'" (Publications du Bureau Central Seismologique International) she proposed "a solid inner core bounded by a liquid outer core", in which P-waves travel faster than in the outer core (Grokipedia); the change of speed at its edge sends waves into the shadow zone (Earth Magazine; search summaries describe reflection).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Inge_Lehmann ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inge_Lehmann ; https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/march-29-1936-notes-earths-inner-core/

F7. Gutenberg, Richter and Jeffreys adopted Lehmann's interpretation "within two or three years" (Wikipedia). Its solidity was confirmed in 1970-1971 (Wikipedia: "It took until 1971"; Earth Magazine: a 1970 study; Grokipedia: Adam Dziewonski and Freeman Gilbert, 1971, from free oscillations, the whole Earth ringing after great earthquakes). She kept her data on cards in oatmeal boxes.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inge_Lehmann ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Inge_Lehmann ; https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/march-29-1936-notes-earths-inner-core/

## The age of the sea floor

F8. Harry Hess proposed sea-floor spreading in 1960 and published it in 1962 as "History of Ocean Basins": new ocean crust forms at the mid-ocean ridges and grows older toward the continental margins, like a conveyor belt. Ocean crust is youngest at the ridges; the oldest preserved sea floor is about 180 million years old, and no sediment older than about 200 million years has been found on it. Sediment thickness grows with distance from the ridge.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Seafloor_spreading

F9. The Deep Sea Drilling Project ran from 1968 to 1983 aboard the drilling ship Glomar Challenger. Its Leg 3 (December 1968 to January 1969) drilled sites across the Mid-Atlantic Ridge in the South Atlantic at different distances from the ridge axis. The oldest undisturbed sediments directly above the basalt date to the time the crust formed (Grokipedia, Glomar Challenger); sediment ages were found by biostratigraphy (fossils). Site 16, 191 km from the ridge, had oldest sediments of 11 +/- 1 million years; Site 21, 1,617 km away, more than 76 million years; ages increased with distance from the ridge, a spreading of about 2 cm a year on each side (4 cm a year in all). The cores, with the magnetic record, confirmed the Vine-Matthews hypothesis.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Deep_Sea_Drilling_Project ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Glomar_Challenger ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glomar_Challenger ("conclusive evidence for the seafloor spreading hypothesis")

F10. A DSDP summary (search result, not fetched) says the seabed was dated "by the age of the sediments just on top of the basalt rock that forms the basement", that Leg 3's sites lay 250-1,300 km from the ridge with ages of 11 to 67 million years, and that the sediment thickness there was roughly constant (once argued against spreading). The figures differ from Grokipedia's (F9), so only F9's figures are used, and the thickness at Leg 3 is not used.
    https://library.ucsd.edu/scilib/hist/dsdp-objectives-1-18.pdf (blocked to direct fetch)

## Radioactive clocks

F11. Radiometric dating rests on unstable parent isotopes turning into stable daughter isotopes at a constant rate. The half-life is "the time required for half of the parent isotopes to decay"; after each further half-life what is left halves again, whatever the starting amount (N = N0 x (1/2)^(t/half-life)). So after one half-life half is left, after two a quarter, after three an eighth.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiometric_dating ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Half-life

F12. Uranium-235 decays to lead-207 with a half-life of 703.8 million years (Grokipedia; Wikipedia: about 700 million); uranium-238 decays to lead-206 with a half-life of 4.468 billion years (about 4.5 billion).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiometric_dating ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometric_dating

F13. Zircon takes in uranium as it crystallises but strongly rejects lead, so its lead was made by decay; dating assumes a closed system in which parent and daughter are neither added nor removed.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiometric_dating ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometric_dating

F14. Decay rates are not affected by temperature, pressure, chemical environment, or electric and magnetic fields; tests at high temperatures and pressures showed no significant variation (Grokipedia). The nucleus "is buffered by surrounding electrons and external conditions" (LibreTexts). The exceptions are decays that use the atom's own inner electrons (electron capture, internal conversion): chemical bonds generally change electron-capture rates by less than 1 percent (beryllium-7, 0.9 percent); fully stripped rhenium-187 atoms, impossible in ordinary matter, decay in 32.9 years against 41.6 billion.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiometric_dating ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometric_dating ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay ; https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Physical_and_Theoretical_Chemistry_Textbook_Maps/Supplemental_Modules_(Physical_and_Theoretical_Chemistry)/Nuclear_Chemistry/Nuclear_Kinetics/Radioactive_Decay_Rates

## Kelvin's cooling Earth, and radioactivity

F15. Lord Kelvin (William Thomson), in work of 1862 ("On the Secular Cooling of the Earth"), treated Earth as having formed "as a completely molten object" (Wikipedia) and cooled by conduction as "a rigid, homogeneous sphere without internal heat generation" (Grokipedia), working from the rise of temperature with depth (about 1 degree F per 50 feet). He put Earth's age at 20 to 400 million years, and by 1897 narrowed it to about 20 to 40 million.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Lord_Kelvin ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Age_of_Earth ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Earth

F16. John Perry, in Nature in 1895, challenged Kelvin's assumption about conduction inside Earth: a fluid or partly fluid interior would carry heat far better ("much internal fluidity would practically mean infinite conductivity for our purpose"), and with convection he allowed 2 to 3 billion years. Grokipedia's modern assessment: Kelvin's main shortfall was the assumption of pure conduction, neglecting mantle convection; radioactivity "extended permissible ages but insufficiently alone".
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Perry_(engineer) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lord_Kelvin ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Age_of_Earth

F17. Pierre Curie and Albert Laborde announced in 1903 that radium produces enough heat to melt its own weight in ice in less than an hour; George Darwin and John Joly first pointed out, in 1903, that radioactive heat upset the age calculations (Wikipedia). Rutherford applied radioactivity to Earth's heat in 1904-1906 (Grokipedia gives both); Kelvin stayed sceptical until his death in 1907. Today uranium-238, thorium-232 and potassium-40 make over 99 percent of Earth's radiogenic heat, about 20 terawatts, roughly half of Earth's internal heat flow of 44-47 terawatts.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Earth ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lord_Kelvin ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth's_internal_heat_budget

F18. Bertram Boltwood linked lead to the end of the uranium decay chain and in 1907 applied uranium-lead ratios to date minerals, "the first quantitative link between radioactivity and Earth's antiquity" (Grokipedia); refined, his 26 samples gave 410 million to 2.2 billion years (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Radiometric_dating ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Earth

## Clair Patterson and the age of Earth

F19. Clair Patterson (2 June 1922 - 5 December 1995) did his doctorate at the University of Chicago under Harrison Brown, who promised the lead work would be "duck soup". He found lead contamination "pervasive throughout his laboratory, even in his own hair" (Wikipedia); his samples were "continuously and unaccountably contaminated with large doses of atmospheric lead whenever they were exposed to air" (UChicago, quoting Bill Bryson). At Caltech from 1953 he worked in "a clean room he had designed and built himself" (Caltech), perhaps the first research clean room (UChicago): he sealed the points of entry against air and other contaminants, acid-cleaned all apparatus and distilled all chemicals shipped to him (Wikipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Cameron_Patterson ; https://mag.uchicago.edu/science-medicine/immeasurable ; https://www.caltech.edu/news/getting-lead-out-47935 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/clair_patterson

F20. In 1953 Patterson used the mass spectrometer at Argonne National Laboratory, with Mark Inghram, on lead from the Canyon Diablo iron meteorite (Wikipedia, UChicago). Troilite from Canyon Diablo has almost no uranium, so its lead is the solar system's original lead, the least radiogenic known (Grokipedia Age of Earth; Wikipedia lead-lead dating).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Cameron_Patterson ; https://mag.uchicago.edu/science-medicine/immeasurable ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Age_of_Earth ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead%E2%80%93lead_dating

F21. Patterson's paper "Age of Meteorites and the Earth" (Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 10, 1956) plotted three stony and two iron meteorites; they fell on one isochron, whose slope gave 4.55 +/- 0.07 billion years. He also analysed sediment from the ocean floor, believed to represent the bulk Earth's composition, and its lead fell on the same isochron: "evidence that meteorites and the Earth are closely related and of the same age" (HyperPhysics); Wikipedia says the result suggested that Earth shared the meteorites' age and origin. With revised decay constants the isochron gives 4.48 (HyperPhysics).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead%E2%80%93lead_dating ; https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Nuclear/PbPb.html ; https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1956GeCoA..10..230P/abstract

F22. Earth's own rocks cannot date its birth: differentiation, plate tectonics, weathering and hydrothermal circulation have mixed and remade them, so few stayed closed systems. Earth's oldest rock (the Acasta Gneiss, Canada) is about 4.03 billion years old and its oldest mineral grains (Jack Hills zircons, Western Australia) 4.404 billion. Earth's age today is given as 4.54 +/- 0.05 billion years; the oldest solids in meteorites are about 4.567 billion.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Age_of_Earth ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Earth

## Clair Patterson and lead in the world

F23. Ocean water off southern California was "lead-laden at the surface" and "the contamination disappeared rapidly with depth"; Patterson and his colleagues named "the likely culprit" as "tetraethyl lead, a widespread gasoline additive" (Caltech). Surface waters held up to 20 times more lead than deep water (Wikipedia).
    https://www.caltech.edu/news/getting-lead-out-47935 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Cameron_Patterson

F24. Patterson analysed ice cores from Camp Century in Greenland (1964) and from Antarctica (1965); they showed atmospheric lead rising "steadily and dangerously" soon after tetraethyl lead was introduced (Wikipedia). Ice cores show yearly layers of snowfall (Wikipedia, Ice core). "Lead levels in Greenland ice had increased by a factor of over 200 since pre-industrial times" (Wikipedia, Ice core, for 1969); the paper is Murozumi, Chow and Patterson, Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 33 (1969), and a later review agrees on "around 200-fold" since prehistoric times (search summaries).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Cameron_Patterson ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_core

F25. Robert Kehoe was chief medical adviser of the Ethyl Corporation, the maker of tetraethyl lead, from 1925, and directed the Kettering Laboratory at the University of Cincinnati, funded by General Motors, DuPont and Ethyl. He held that the "presence of lead in humans and other organisms was normal" and that "exposure to low lead levels was not harmful"; the "Kehoe Rule" put the burden of proof on opponents of tetraethyl lead. He was not seriously challenged until Patterson's paper of 1965; his work "is now considered discredited".
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Kehoe ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Cameron_Patterson

F26. Tetraethyl lead was developed in December 1921 by Thomas Midgley Jr. at General Motors to stop engine knock, and first sold in petrol on 1 February 1923 (Grokipedia). Patterson was excluded from a 1971 National Research Council panel on atmospheric lead; on a 1978 panel he wrote a 78-page minority report (Wikipedia). The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) issued rules in 1973 to cut lead in petrol in stages; from 1975 cars with catalytic converters, which lead damages, needed unleaded fuel; the lead additive was cut by 91 percent by the end of 1986; sale of leaded fuel for road vehicles was banned in the United States from 1 January 1996; Algeria, the last country, stopped in July 2021. The mean US blood lead level fell from 16 micrograms per decilitre in 1976 to 3 in 1991, a 78 percent drop (Wikipedia, Tetraethyllead).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Tetraethyllead ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetraethyllead ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Cameron_Patterson

## Misconceptions a 9th-grader brings (for honest wrong choices)

- Two sets of earthquake waves mean two shocks (F4: one wave by two paths).
- Deeper rock is melted, or waves go faster through hotter or softer rock (F4, F2: the mantle is solid, and the faster rock lies below the Moho).
- A stronger wave travels faster (it does not; speed is set by the rock).
- S-waves are stopped because the core is too dense or too hot (F1, F2: the solid mantle above it is hot and dense and carries them; a liquid has no shear rigidity).
- The core is solid all the way through, or entirely liquid (F6, F7).
- After three half-lives a quarter is left, or a 3:1 daughter-to-parent ratio means three half-lives (F11: it means two).
- Heat and pressure deep underground speed up or slow down radioactive decay (F14).
- Meteorites are pieces of Earth, or the ocean mud is as old as the meteorites (F21: the mud is young; its lead shares the meteorites' history).
- Lead found everywhere, even in hair, proves it is natural (F19, F24, F25: only the dated ice shows when it rose).
- Radioactivity alone proved Kelvin wrong (F16, F17: Perry's flowing interior is now counted the larger flaw; radioactivity also gave the clocks that settled it, F18).
