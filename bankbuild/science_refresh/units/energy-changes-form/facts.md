# Energy Changes Form: fact sheet (T1, 5th grade)

Researched 2026-11-25. Grokipedia first; Energy Kids (EIA), CK-12 on LibreTexts K12, PBS NOVA, state standards and teaching pages to corroborate.

## Facts

F1 Energy is the ability (capacity) to do work or cause change: to move something, warm it, light it or make it sound. https://grokipedia.com/page/Energy ("the capacity of a system to perform work or cause change"); https://www.teachengineering.org/lessons/view/cub_energy2_lesson01 (Grades 3-5: "the ability to do work, to make things happen and to cause changes"); https://sites.google.com/lcps.org/5thgradesolreview/science/energy-sol-5-2 (Virginia grade 5, SOL 5.2: "recognize examples of energy causing change or doing work")

F2 The basic forms of energy a 5th grader names are light, heat (thermal), sound, electrical, chemical and mechanical. Florida grade 5 benchmark SC.5.P.10.1, "Investigate and describe some basic forms of energy, including light, heat, sound, electrical, chemical, and mechanical" (quoted at https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/sc5p101-18754691/packs/22848152); https://grokipedia.com/page/Energy ("mechanical, thermal, electrical, chemical, nuclear, and radiant energy"); https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a (TEKS 5.8A lesson)

F3 Energy changes from one form to another (an energy transformation); when people use energy it does not disappear but changes form. https://grokipedia.com/page/Energy_transformation ("the process by which energy changes from one form to another"); https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/laws-of-energy.php ("it changes from one form of energy into another form")

F4 Chemical energy is energy stored in substances: food, fuels (wood, coal, gasoline, oil, natural gas) and batteries. Burning or the body's breakdown of food releases it, as heat, light or motion. https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_energy (food, coal and petroleum "store vast amounts of chemical energy"; "the burning of wood or gasoline, chemical energy stored in molecular bonds is released"; "often manifesting as heat, light, or mechanical work"); https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a ("Chemical energy is stored in batteries, food, fuel, and wood."); https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("energy stored in the bonds of atoms and molecules"; batteries, biomass, petroleum, natural gas, coal); the body: https://grokipedia.com/page/Energy_transformation ("converting chemical energy from food into kinetic energy for movement") and https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/laws-of-energy.php ("The human body isn't very efficient at converting food into useful work"; "The remaining energy is converted to heat")

F5 A battery stores chemical energy and changes it into electrical energy when it is used. https://grokipedia.com/page/Electric_battery ("designed to convert stored chemical energy into electrical energy through oxidation-reduction reactions"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Energy_transformation ("chemical energy stored in batteries directly to electrical energy")

F6 The flashlight chain: the battery's stored chemical energy becomes electrical energy when the switch is turned on; the electrical energy flows through the wires to the bulb, which changes it into light and a little thermal energy (heat). A flashlight is a small, battery-operated portable electric light. Texas grade 5 TEKS 5.8A (curriculum map line G5.P5, bankbuild/science_refresh/curriculum_map.json); https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a ("Chemical energy stored in the battery turns into electrical energy when the switch closes the circuit"; at the bulb "it turns into light energy (and a little bit of thermal energy too)"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Flashlight ("a small, battery-operated portable electric light")

F7 Every bulb gives off some heat along with its light. An old-style (incandescent) bulb lights by heating a thin wire (filament) until it glows, and turns over 90% of its energy into heat; a light-emitting diode (LED) wastes far less as heat but still warms and needs metal heat sinks. https://grokipedia.com/page/Incandescent_light_bulb ("a filament is heated to high temperatures by passing an electric current through it"; "over 90% of input electrical energy dissipates as heat"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Flashlight (LEDs "generating minimal heat"; "aluminum heat sinks or copper MCPCBs are employed to dissipate heat effectively"); https://www.teachengineering.org/lessons/view/cub_energy2_lesson01 (a light bulb: "electrical energy changing into heat energy")

F8 Thermal energy is another name for heat. https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("Thermal energy, or heat, is the energy that comes from atoms and molecules moving in a substance"); https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a ("Thermal energy is the heat coming off a stovetop burner, a hot cup of cocoa, or a campfire.")

F9 Mechanical energy is the energy of motion (and of position): anything that moves has it, and so does moving water. https://grokipedia.com/page/Mechanical_energy ("the sum of kinetic energy, which arises from an object's motion," and potential energy, "from an object's position"); Florida SC.5.P.10.1 study cards ("_____ energy is the energy of motion or movement", mechanical: https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/sc5p101-18754691/packs/22848152); https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a ("Mechanical energy can be stored (a stretched rubber band, a compressed spring, a book on a shelf) or moving")

F10 An electric motor, such as the one in a fan, changes electrical energy into mechanical energy (motion); a motor also loses some energy as heat. https://grokipedia.com/page/Electric_motor ("converts electrical energy into mechanical energy"; "powering pumps, fans, and compressors"; "losses that dissipate energy as heat"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Mechanical_energy (electric motors give "continuous rotation for applications like fans and vehicles")

F11 An electric generator does the reverse of a motor: it changes mechanical energy (motion) into electrical energy. Steam, water or wind spins it. https://grokipedia.com/page/Electric_generator ("a device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy"; "an electric motor operates in the reverse manner"; "use steam, water, wind, or combustion gases to spin the rotor"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Wind_turbine ("converts the kinetic energy of wind into electrical power"; the rotor "drives an electrical generator")

F12 At a hydroelectric dam, falling, rushing water spins turbines (big bladed wheels) that drive generators; the water's energy becomes mechanical energy and then electrical energy. The water itself carries no electricity. https://grokipedia.com/page/Hydroelectricity ("converting the gravitational potential energy of water into mechanical energy" "using turbines, which then drive electrical generators"); https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("gravity forces water down through a hydroelectric turbine to produce electricity")

F13 A loudspeaker (speaker) changes an electrical signal into sound by making a cone or diaphragm vibrate (shake back and forth); a radio's loudspeaker makes its sound. https://grokipedia.com/page/Loudspeaker ("converts an electrical audio signal into corresponding sound waves"; "vibrating an attached diaphragm or cone that displaces surrounding air molecules"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Radio_receiver ("a power amplifier driving a loudspeaker to reproduce sound waves")

F14 A microphone does the opposite job: it changes sound into an electrical signal. https://grokipedia.com/page/Microphone ("a transducer that converts acoustic sound waves into corresponding electrical signals")

F15 A phone holds microphones, speakers, a rechargeable lithium-ion battery, a display (screen) and vibration motors. https://grokipedia.com/page/Mobile_phone ("Audio hardware in mobile phones includes microphones and speakers optimized for voice calls"; "Mobile phones predominantly utilize rechargeable lithium-ion (Li-ion) batteries"; displays; "vibration motors")

F16 A phone's screen gives off light made from electrical energy (an organic light-emitting diode (OLED) screen glows when an electric current passes through it). https://grokipedia.com/page/OLED ("to produce light when an electric current passes through them"; "powering high-resolution screens in smartphones")

F17 A solar panel is made of solar (photovoltaic) cells that change light straight into electrical energy, with no heating step. Heat does not help: a panel's output falls as it gets hotter, and cold, bright weather raises its efficiency. https://grokipedia.com/page/Solar_cell ("converts sunlight directly into electricity through the photovoltaic effect"; "Temperature coefficients quantify output decline, typically -0.3 to -0.5%/°C for silicon"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Photovoltaic_effect ("generate an electric voltage or current when exposed to light"; "without moving parts or heat intermediaries"); https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/laws-of-energy.php ("Solar photovoltaic cells change radiant energy from the sun into electrical energy"); https://skepticalscience.com/print.php?r=498 ("cold temperatures actually increasing solar panel efficiency by increasing voltage")

F18 Almost all the energy on Earth comes from the Sun. https://k12.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Science_and_Technology/Earth_Science/20%3A_Earth's_Energy/20.01%3A_Energy_Resources (CK-12 Earth Science: "Almost all energy comes from the Sun."); https://grokipedia.com/page/Energy_transformation (on Earth, "energy transformations primarily originate from solar radiation"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth's_energy_budget ("Solar radiation provides the dominant external energy input"; Earth's internal sources are "less than 0.04% of the absorbed solar radiation")

F19 Wind comes from the Sun heating Earth's surface unevenly, which sets the air moving; Earth's spin (the Coriolis effect) bends the path of the wind but does not drive it. https://grokipedia.com/page/Wind (wind "arises primarily from the uneven heating of Earth's surface by solar radiation"; the Coriolis effect is "modifying the direct path of winds"); https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/sources-of-energy.php ("Wind energy from uneven heating from the sun"); https://k12.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Science_and_Technology/Earth_Science/20%3A_Earth's_Energy/20.01%3A_Energy_Resources ("The Sun heats some areas more than others, which causes wind.")

F20 Coal, oil and natural gas formed from the remains of ancient plants and animals that stored the Sun's energy millions of years ago. https://k12.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Science_and_Technology/Earth_Science/20%3A_Earth's_Energy/20.01%3A_Energy_Resources ("Fossil fuels are made of the remains of plants and animals that stored the Sun's energy millions of years ago."); https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/sources-of-energy.php ("from the buried remains of ancient sea plants and animals")

F21 The few energy sources that do not come from the Sun: Earth's internal heat (geothermal energy, left from the planet's formation and from radioactive decay) and the tides, raised by the Moon's gravitational pull. Both are tiny beside the Sun's energy. https://k12.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Science_and_Technology/Earth_Science/20%3A_Earth's_Energy/20.01%3A_Energy_Resources ("Earth's internal heat does not depend on the Sun for energy."); https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/labs/lab/energy/2/1 (geothermal: "the naturally occurring heat coming from Earth's core"; tidal: "ocean tides caused by the moon's gravitational pull"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth's_energy_budget (geothermal 0.087 W/m², tidal "only about 0.001 W/m²", together under 0.04% of absorbed sunlight)

F22 Sound energy is made by vibrating things, such as a ringing bell, a drum or a plucked guitar string. https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a ("Sound energy is vibrations spreading through the air from a drum, a tuning fork, or a person talking."); Florida SC.5.P.10.1 study cards ("When you pluck a guitar string, it makes _____ energy", sound: https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/sc5p101-18754691/packs/22848152)

F23 A wound-up or squeezed spring stores mechanical energy, not chemical energy. https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a ("Mechanical energy can be stored (a stretched rubber band, a compressed spring, a book on a shelf)"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Mechanical_energy (potential energy "from an object's position or configuration")

F24 A car engine burns gasoline and changes its chemical energy into mechanical energy (motion). https://www.eia.gov/kids/what-is-energy/laws-of-energy.php ("A car engine burns gasoline, converting the chemical energy in gasoline into mechanical energy.")

F25 A heat engine, such as a steam engine, changes thermal energy (heat) into motion; an electric fan does not run on heat. https://grokipedia.com/page/Heat_engine ("a device that extracts heat from a high-temperature source", converting part of it to mechanical work; steam turbines as examples)

F26 A force is a push or a pull on an object; it is not the same thing as energy. https://grokipedia.com/page/Force ("a force is a push or pull upon an object"); curriculum map G5.P4 (forces and motion)

## Misconceptions a 5th grader may hold (for honest wrong choices)

M1 Batteries have electricity stored inside them (rather than chemical energy). ASCD, citing Wieler (1998), Children's misconceptions about science (AIP Operation Physics): "Batteries have electricity inside them." https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/what-do-kids-know-and-misunderstand-about-science; Harvard-Smithsonian CfA MOSART list (9-12): "Students think of a battery as a 'giver' of electricity" https://waps.cfa.harvard.edu/mosart/misconceptions.php

M2 Light is the only kind of energy a flashlight produces. Listed in the TEKS 5.8A lesson's misconceptions: https://keslerscience.com/describe-energy-transformations-lesson-plan-teks-5.8a

M3 Energy confused with a force, with fuel, or with a stuff inside things. MOSART: "Energy is thought of as a force" (9-12); "Students think of energy as fuel" (9-12); "Energy is an entity contained within substances and can be created and destroyed" (K-4). https://waps.cfa.harvard.edu/mosart/misconceptions.php. The "kind of matter that takes up space" wording of the last is the author's reading of the K-4 entry.

M4 Solar panels run on the Sun's heat. Solar-industry pages answer this belief (SunPower: panels "extract energy from light, not heat", seen in search; that page now returns 404); the physics is F17. That students hold it is the author's judgment.

M5 Electricity flows into a lamp and is used up there. MOSART (K-4 and 5-8): "Electricity flows through hollow wires and is used up by lights or appliances." https://waps.cfa.harvard.edu/mosart/misconceptions.php. Used for the lure "Light and electricity".

M6 Wind is caused by Earth's spinning. Author's judgment (no survey found); F19 says the spin only bends the wind's path.

M7 The microphone and the speaker mixed up (which way each changes energy). Author's judgment; F13, F14.

M8 Coal's energy came from Earth's heat underground, where coal forms. Author's judgment; F20 says it is stored sunlight from ancient plants.

M9 Moving water carries electricity (because dams make electricity). Author's judgment; F12.

## Notes and cautions

- Kinetic and potential energy by name are 6th-grade content (unit kinetic-and-potential-energy, T2), and so is "energy is never created or destroyed"; this unit says "mechanical energy (motion)" and asks no conservation question.
- Friction turning motion into heat is the T2 unit's; it is not used here.
- How a circuit works (closed loops, switches, conductors) is the electric-circuits unit's; how light travels is how-light-travels'. No question here asks either.
- "The energy in food first came from the Sun" is how-plants-make-food's; the Sun question here uses wind and coal, not food.
- Do not write that a fan cools a room: a fan moves air (its motor even adds a little heat, F10). The stem says only that it blows air.
- Do not say an LED makes no heat (F7).
- A phone call reaches the phone as radio waves; inside the phone the voice is an electrical signal, so the stem says "inside a phone".
