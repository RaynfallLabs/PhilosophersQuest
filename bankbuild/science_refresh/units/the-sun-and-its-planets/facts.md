# The Sun and Its Planets: fact sheet (T1, 5th grade)

Researched 2026-11-25. Grokipedia first; NASA and others to corroborate.

## Facts

F1 The Sun is a star: a hot, glowing ball of gas (plasma), and the only star in our solar system. It sits at the centre of the solar system. https://grokipedia.com/page/Sun ("The Sun is the star at the center of the Solar System"); https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/ ("the only star in our solar system", "a hot glowing ball of hydrogen and helium"); https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-sun/en/ ("A star is a hot, glowing ball of gas")

F2 The Sun is the closest star to Earth, far closer than any other star; the next nearest, Proxima Centauri, is 4.24 light-years away. https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-sun/en/ ("our closest star: the Sun"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Sun (Proxima Centauri at 4.24 light-years)

F3 The Sun looks so much brighter than the other stars mainly because it is so much closer to Earth (a 5th-grade standard). https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/5-ess1-1-earths-place-universe (5-ESS1-1: "the apparent brightness of the sun and stars is due to their relative distances from the Earth")

F4 The Sun is not the biggest star: some stars are up to 1,000 times wider than the Sun, and some are only a tenth its size. https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/sun-compare/en/

F5 The Sun is not the hottest star: its surface is about 5,770 K (kelvin), while blue O-type stars are above 30,000 K. https://grokipedia.com/page/Stellar_classification

F6 The Sun's light and heat keep Earth warm enough to live on; life on Earth depends on them. https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-sun/en/ ("Heat from the Sun makes Earth warm enough to live on"); https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/ ("life on Earth is only possible because of the Sun's light and energy")

F7 The Sun's gravity holds the solar system together and keeps the planets travelling round it in their orbits; an orbit is the balance between gravity's pull and the planet's own forward motion. https://grokipedia.com/page/Solar_System ("exerts gravitational dominance to maintain all orbits"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Orbit ("the balance between gravitational attraction and the object's inertial motion"); https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/ ("Its gravity holds the solar system together"); https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-sun/en/

F8 Eight planets orbit the Sun. In order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. https://grokipedia.com/page/Solar_System; https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/ ("orbited by eight planets"); position lines on https://grokipedia.com/page/Venus (second), https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth (third), https://grokipedia.com/page/Mars (fourth), https://grokipedia.com/page/Jupiter (fifth), https://grokipedia.com/page/Saturn (sixth), https://grokipedia.com/page/Uranus (seventh), https://grokipedia.com/page/Neptune (eighth)

F9 The four inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are small and rocky. The four outer planets are giants: Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, Uranus and Neptune are ice giants. https://grokipedia.com/page/Solar_System; https://floridascience.squarespace.com/sc5e52-space-planets-the-solar-system (Florida grade 5 benchmark SC.5.E.5.2)

F10 Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun (the innermost) and the smallest planet; it goes round the Sun in 88 Earth days. https://grokipedia.com/page/Mercury_(planet)

F11 Mercury has almost no atmosphere, so it cannot hold its heat: about 430 C by day and about -180 C at night. https://grokipedia.com/page/Mercury_(planet)

F12 Venus is the hottest planet, hotter even than Mercury, which is closer to the Sun. Its surface is about 462-475 C (NASA: about 467 C), hot enough to melt lead. https://grokipedia.com/page/Venus ("the hottest planet in the Solar System"); https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/ ("Venus is hotter even than the innermost planet, charbroiled Mercury"; "hot enough to melt lead")

F13 Venus is so hot because its thick air, mostly carbon dioxide and over 90 times as dense at the ground as Earth's, traps heat (a runaway greenhouse effect). Thick clouds cover the whole planet. https://grokipedia.com/page/Venus; https://grokipedia.com/page/Atmosphere_of_Venus (96.5% carbon dioxide; about 93 bars; sulfuric acid clouds); https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/ ("Its thick atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect")

F14 Venus is the brightest object in the night sky after the Moon, so it is the brightest planet; its clouds reflect about three-quarters of the sunlight that hits them. https://grokipedia.com/page/Venus; https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/

F15 Earth is the third planet from the Sun, the only place known to have living things, and the only planet in the solar system known to have liquid water on its surface (oceans cover about 71% of it). https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/ ("the only place we know of inhabited by living things"; "the only planet in our solar system with liquid water on the surface"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth ("the only confirmed planet known to support life"; oceans "covering 70.8 percent")

F16 Earth takes about 365 days (365.25) to go once round the Sun; that trip is one year. https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/ ("It takes 365.25 days to complete one trip around the Sun"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Earth (365.256 days); Florida grade 4 benchmark SC.4.E.5.3, https://floridascience.squarespace.com/sc4e53-revolution-and-rotation-of-the-earth ("Earth revolves around the Sun in a year and rotates on its axis in a 24-hour day")

F17 Mars, the fourth planet, looks red because the iron in its rocks and dust has rusted (iron oxide). https://grokipedia.com/page/Mars ("iron oxide (rust) responsible for much of its surface's distinctive reddish hue"); https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/ ("oxidization, or rusting, of iron in the rocks"); https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2025/02/Why_is_Mars_red

F18 Mars is not hot: it is farther from the Sun than Earth, and even on a warm day its ground reaches only about 20 C. It has a thin atmosphere, mostly carbon dioxide. https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/ ("as high as 70 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius)"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Mars

F19 Other planets have volcanoes and canyons too: Mars has Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system, and Valles Marineris, the largest canyon. Venus, Jupiter and Saturn have thick clouds. https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/; https://grokipedia.com/page/Olympus_Mons; https://grokipedia.com/page/Atmosphere_of_Venus; https://grokipedia.com/page/Jupiter (Great Red Spot storm)

F20 Jupiter is the largest planet, with more than twice the mass of all the other planets put together, and it has no solid surface. https://grokipedia.com/page/Jupiter ("Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System"; "more than twice the combined mass of all other planets")

F21 Saturn is the sixth planet and the second largest. Its rings are made of countless pieces of ice and rock, from dust grains to chunks metres across, mostly water ice. https://grokipedia.com/page/Saturn; https://grokipedia.com/page/Rings_of_Saturn; https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/ ("billions of small chunks of ice and rock")

F22 All four giant planets have rings, but Saturn's are the broad, bright ones: Jupiter's, Uranus's and Neptune's are faint and dark. https://grokipedia.com/page/Ring_system ("all four gas and ice giants"; Saturn's "the most extensive and visually striking"; Jupiter's "far fainter"); https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/ ("none are as spectacular or as complex as Saturn's")

F23 A small telescope shows Saturn's rings; the other giants' rings are not easily seen. https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/planets/saturn ("A small telescope is needed to see Saturn's rings"); https://floridascience.squarespace.com/sc5e52-space-planets-the-solar-system ("Saturn's rings are easily visible through a telescope")

F24 Neptune is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun. https://grokipedia.com/page/Neptune

F25 The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and travels round Earth about once a month (27.3 days). https://grokipedia.com/page/Moon; https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/moon-phases/en/ ("about 27.3 days for the Moon to orbit Earth")

F26 The Moon makes no light of its own: moonlight is sunlight reflected off the Moon's rocky surface. https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/moon-phases/en/ ("The Moon does not produce its own light"); https://grokipedia.com/page/Moon (reflects about 12% of the sunlight that hits it); https://www.nsta.org/lesson-plan/moonlight (Grade 5 probe: "The light from the Sun bounces off the Moon")

F27 The asteroid belt, a ring of rocky and metal chunks round the Sun, lies between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter, so it sits between the rocky planets and the giants. Its largest object is the dwarf planet Ceres. https://grokipedia.com/page/Asteroid_belt; https://grokipedia.com/page/Solar_System; https://floridascience.squarespace.com/sc5e52-space-planets-the-solar-system

F28 Pluto, found by Clyde Tombaugh in 1930, was counted as the ninth planet until 2006, when the International Astronomical Union (IAU) renamed it a dwarf planet. It lies in the Kuiper belt, beyond Neptune. https://grokipedia.com/page/Pluto

F29 Space probes have studied every planet up close: Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have made close-up observations of all four giant planets (Uranus in 1986, Neptune in 1989), and Mariner 10 flew past Mercury. https://grokipedia.com/page/Voyager_2; https://grokipedia.com/page/Mercury_(planet)

## Misconceptions a 5th grader may hold (for honest wrong choices)

M1 The Sun is not a star (or is a "ball of fire" of some other kind). Harvard-Smithsonian CfA lists "the sun is not a star" among grades 5-8 misconceptions: https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/news/what-do-students-know

M2 The Sun is the biggest or hottest star (it only looks that way because it is close). Corrected by F4, F5; NGSS 5-ESS1-1 targets exactly this (F3). The "bigger / hotter" belief is the author's reading of the standard, not a published survey.

M3 Mercury is the hottest planet because it is closest to the Sun. https://www.mentalfloss.com/posts/solar-system-misconceptions ("it's still not the hottest planet"); corrected by NASA (F12).

M4 The Moon makes its own light. Allen (2010), Misconceptions in Primary Science, lists "the moon is a source of light" (via SCIMAP, https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=545); NSTA's Grade 5 Moonlight probe is written to surface it: https://www.nsta.org/lesson-plan/moonlight. The "stores sunlight and glows" version is the glow-in-the-dark-toy model of the same belief (author's judgment).

M5 There is no gravity in space, and gravity is made by magnetism. CfA (grades 5-8): "there is no gravity in space"; Stover and Saunders (2000), Journal of Elementary Science Education: "Gravity is generated by magnetic fields" (https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=778); Science Learning Hub: https://www.sciencelearn.org.nz/resources/294-alternative-conceptions-about-gravity

M6 Mixing up Earth's year (one trip round the Sun) with its day. Stover and Saunders (2000) list "The Earth's revolution around the sun causes night and day" (https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=778).

M7 Pluto is a planet. Allen (2010) lists "Pluto is a planet" (https://wordpressua.uark.edu/scimap/?p=547).

M8 Red means hot, so Mars must be a hot, burning or lava-covered world. Author's judgment (no survey found); Mars is cold (F18).

M9 Saturn is the only planet with rings. Not used as a key claim; every question here avoids saying "only Saturn has rings" (F22).

## Notes and cautions

- Do not say Venus is "Earth's closest neighbour" (true only at closest approach; on average Mercury is nearer to Earth).
- Do not say "the Sun is an average star" (half-truth). Say only that many stars are bigger and many are hotter (F4, F5).
- Pluto's path sometimes crosses inside Neptune's, so say "beyond Neptune" (the Kuiper belt), not "always farther than Neptune".
- Venus's surface temperature differs by source (462, 467, 475 C): say "over 450 degrees Celsius".
- Jupiter's centre is far hotter than Venus's surface, so ask about the hottest SURFACE and keep the giants out of that question's choices.
