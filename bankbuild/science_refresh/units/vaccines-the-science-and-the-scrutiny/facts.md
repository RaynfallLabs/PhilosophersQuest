## The immunology (settled)

F1. A vaccine is a biological preparation of killed (inactivated) or weakened (attenuated) germs, or fractions of them; it "typically incorporates an antigen derived from a pathogen". Inactivated vaccines "contain whole pathogens that have been killed or inactivated"; live attenuated vaccines expose a person "to a weakened strain of a pathogen"; subunit vaccines "only expose the patient to the key antigens of a pathogen", not whole cells or viruses.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vaccine ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-5-vaccines

F2. Antigens are "molecules that activate adaptive immunity"; adaptive defences are "triggered by pathogen-specific molecular structures called antigens."
    https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-summary ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-1-overview-of-specific-adaptive-immunity

F3. "B lymphocytes or B cells produce antibodies"; activated B cells become plasma cells that make antibodies. Vaccines work by "directing B cells to undergo proliferation" (Grokipedia).
    https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-summary ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Vaccine

F4. Vaccination is ARTIFICIAL ACTIVE immunity: the body's own adaptive defences are set off by a weakened or killed germ or its key antigens, and memory cells are made. ARTIFICIAL PASSIVE immunity is different: "the transfer of antibodies produced by a donor" (human or animal) to another person, used for toxin diseases such as tetanus, botulism and diphtheria. MISCONCEPTIONS: that a vaccine is ready-made antibodies, or a medicine that kills the germ.
    https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-5-vaccines

F5. The first exposure to an antigen "stimulates a primary response"; later exposures "stimulate a faster and strong secondary response." The secondary antibody response is "initiated by memory B cells" and "develops more quickly and produces higher and more sustained levels of antibody" (OpenStax). Grokipedia: primary response peaks in 5-10 days, the secondary in 2-5 days, with antibody at "10- to 100-fold greater titers"; memory B cells "swiftly differentiate into antibody-secreting plasma cells" on re-exposure. (CONFLICT inside Grokipedia: elsewhere it says 100- to 1000-fold; no fold figure is keyed.) Vaccine memory "can diminish over time, often requiring booster doses"; inactivated vaccines "usually require higher doses and multiple boosters" (OpenStax). MISCONCEPTION: that the booster works because antibody from the first dose is still there.
    https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-summary ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Immunological_memory ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-5-vaccines

## Variolation before Jenner (1717-1721)

F6. Variolation (engrafting, inoculation) put matter from a smallpox patient ("powdered smallpox scabs or fluid from pustules") into small cuts or scratches, giving a real but usually milder smallpox that left immunity. "Only 1-2% of those variolated died from the intentional infection compared to 30% who contracted smallpox naturally." Variolated people could pass smallpox to others; Jenner's cowpox vaccination was "far safer to those vaccinated and was far more rarely transmitted to their contacts."
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variolation ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-5-vaccines

F7. Lady Mary Wortley Montagu survived smallpox in 1715 (her face scarred) and lost her brother to it in 1713. In the Ottoman Empire she saw engrafting in March 1717 and described it in her letter of 1 April 1717; in March 1718 the surgeon Charles Maitland engrafted her son Edward at the embassy, and in April 1721, in a London epidemic, Maitland engrafted her daughter, the first such operation in England. She then persuaded Caroline, Princess of Wales, to have it tested. (Grokipedia places the son's engrafting at Adrianople or Constantinople; it names the 1717 letter's recipient as Lady Rich, which Wikipedia does not.)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Lady_Mary_Wortley_Montagu ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Mary_Wortley_Montagu

F8. Onesimus, an enslaved West African man given to the Boston minister Cotton Mather by his congregation in December 1706, told Mather that he had had smallpox "both, Yes and No", described the cut and the matter, and showed his scar. (CONFLICT on when he told Mather: Grokipedia gives about 1706 and also a 1716 account; Wikipedia gives 1715. No date is keyed.) In the 1721 Boston epidemic Mather wrote to the town's physicians urging them to inoculate.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Onesimus_(Bostonian) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1721_Boston_smallpox_outbreak

F9. Zabdiel Boylston, the one Boston doctor who took up Mather's call, began on 26 June 1721 with his son Thomas, 6, and two enslaved people, Jack, 36, and Jackey, 2. He kept counts. Six of his inoculated patients died. CONFLICT on how many he inoculated: 247 (Wikipedia, 1721 outbreak; the James Lind Library record: "of 247 persons inoculated by Boylston in 1721 and 1722, only 6 died (a mortality of 2.4%)"), 248 (Grokipedia, Onesimus page; Wikipedia, Boylston page "about 248"), 282 (James Lind Library table: 6 died, 276 survived) and "300" (Wikipedia, Variolation, rounded). Natural smallpox in Boston: 844 deaths among 5,759 cases (Wikipedia; Lind Library table: 844 died, 4,915 survived; 14.7%), or 5,889 cases (Grokipedia, 14.3%). Whatever the count, the inoculated died at about 2 in 100 (2.1-2.4%) and natural cases at about 1 in 7; the brief's "about 1 in 40" holds only for the 247-248 count, so the questions say "about 2 in 100". Boylston presented his account to the Royal Society, chaired by Isaac Newton, in July 1726 and published it in London in 1726.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1721_Boston_smallpox_outbreak ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabdiel_Boylston ; https://www.jameslindlibrary.org/articles/zabdiel-boylstons-evaluation-of-inoculation-against-smallpox/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Onesimus_(Bostonian) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variolation

F10. The opposition: the physician William Douglass, "a vehement inoculation opponent", published pamphlets against it; he held that only accredited physicians should do such a dangerous procedure, and warned that the inoculated could spread the disease.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1721_Boston_smallpox_outbreak ; https://www.jameslindlibrary.org/articles/zabdiel-boylstons-evaluation-of-inoculation-against-smallpox/

## Jenner and Jesty (1774-1798)

F11. The folk belief came first: "farmers and dairymaids observed that cowpox infection seemed to protect against smallpox" (Grokipedia). By 1768 John Fewster had realised that cowpox gave immunity to smallpox, and between 1770 and 1791 at least five investigators in England and Germany tested cowpox in people (Wikipedia). The story that Jenner noticed milkmaids' clear complexions "appears to be a myth" (Wikipedia). MISCONCEPTION: that Jenner discovered that cowpox protects.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Edward_Jenner ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jenner

F12. Benjamin Jesty (about 1736-1816), a Dorset farmer, took his wife Elizabeth and two eldest sons to a cow with cowpox at Chetnole during the 1774 smallpox epidemic in Yetminster and scratched matter from the cow into their arms with a darning needle. The sons were later exposed to smallpox and did not catch it; in 1805 the eldest, Robert, agreed to be inoculated with smallpox to show he was still immune. Jesty "did not publicise his findings"; Grokipedia: the case "was not publicly documented."
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Jesty ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Edward_Jenner

F13. Edward Jenner (1749-1823), a country doctor, on 14 May 1796 took matter from cowpox sores on the hand of the milkmaid Sarah Nelmes and put it, through two small cuts, into the arm of James Phipps, the eight-year-old son of his gardener. On 1 July 1796 he inoculated the boy with "variolous material" (matter from smallpox), and no disease followed; Phipps was challenged again later with the same result. Jenner repeated the procedure on more than twenty others between 1796 and 1798.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jenner ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Edward_Jenner ; https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/18-5-vaccines

F14. The Royal Society did not publish Jenner's first paper ("the society had rejected an earlier submission on vaccination in 1797", Grokipedia). Jenner paid to print An Inquiry into the Causes and Effects of the Variolae Vaccinae (1798), covering 23 cases, with four engraved plates.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Edward_Jenner ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jenner

F15. Donald Hopkins, an American physician and historian of smallpox: "Jenner's unique contribution was not that he inoculated a few persons with cowpox, but that he then proved [by subsequent challenges] that they were immune to smallpox." He adds that Jenner showed the cowpox matter could be passed "from person to person, not just directly from cattle." Grokipedia credits Jenner with systematic observation and controlled testing, then the 1798 publication.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jenner ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Edward_Jenner

F16. Jenner "rashly claimed that the protective effect was lifelong. This last proved to be incorrect" (revaccination was later needed). Compulsory infant vaccination came to England only with the Vaccination Act 1853, thirty years after Jenner's death; Parliament had granted Jenner money (10,000 pounds in 1802, 20,000 in 1807).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox_vaccine ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Jesty

## Smallpox eradicated (declared May 1980)

F17. A commission certified smallpox's eradication in December 1979, and "On May 8, 1980, the 33rd World Health Assembly formally declared smallpox eradicated" (Grokipedia). The last natural case was in Somalia in 1977 (Ali Maow Maalin, a hospital cook in Merca, rash on 26 October 1977). Smallpox is the only human disease eradicated so far.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Smallpox ; https://webharvest.gov/peth04/20041014235807/http://www.who.int/emc/diseases/smallpox/factsheet.html

F18. Why it could be done: "There is no animal reservoir. Insects play no role in transmission" (WHO); spread is "from person to person"; variola "has no other known host or reservoir species" (National Academies); "a human disease without animal reservoirs, which became an important factor in its successful eradication" (StatPearls). Grokipedia names "the virus's lack of animal reservoirs" and "the visible, non-latent nature of cases", with a "distinctive rash enabling reliable identification". WHO history (via Fenner et al.): no long-term carriers, and one attack gave essentially life-long immunity.
    https://webharvest.gov/peth04/20041014235807/http://www.who.int/emc/diseases/smallpox/factsheet.html ; https://www.nap.edu/read/12616/chapter/4 ; https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470418 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Smallpox ; https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/blaw/bt/smallpox/who/red-book/Chp%2031.pdf

F19. Stocks of the virus were not all destroyed: since 1984 the only known stocks have been kept at the CDC in the United States and at VECTOR in Koltsovo, Russia.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox_vaccine

F20. How it was done: the campaign adopted "a surveillance-containment strategy that prioritized targeted interventions over blanket mass vaccination", and "Central to containment was ring vaccination" (Grokipedia); early mass campaigns had aimed for 80 percent coverage. Ring vaccination vaccinates "those who are most likely to be infected", contacts and their contacts, and "requires thorough and rapid surveillance" (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Smallpox ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_vaccination

F21. William Foege, a CDC doctor in eastern Nigeria (he joined the CDC smallpox effort in December 1966), faced an outbreak with too little vaccine; a shipment for mass vaccination was months away. "Health authorities thought they needed to inoculate 80 to 100 percent of the population to stop smallpox." He asked a ham-radio network of missionaries to seek out cases, vaccinated the villages with cases and then the places the disease would go next along family and market routes; "Four weeks later, there was no more smallpox" (University of Washington Magazine). Grokipedia: "vaccinating only confirmed cases, their contacts, and surrounding populations to encircle outbreaks." CONFLICT on the share of people vaccinated: Lasker Foundation "7-8%", UW Magazine "less than 50 percent", Grokipedia "as low as 6%" and "under 50%"; no share is keyed.
    https://magazine.washington.edu/feature/william-foege-61-honored-for-his-role-in-eradicating-smallpox ; https://laskerfoundation.org/stopping-smallpox-in-its-tracks/ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/William_Foege

## The Cutter Incident (1955)

F22. Cutter Laboratories of Berkeley, California, was licensed in 1955 to make Jonas Salk's polio vaccine, which was meant to hold only killed (inactivated) virus. "Approximately 120,000 doses" from the bad lots were given; over 200,000 children in five western and midwestern states got Cutter vaccine (Grokipedia). Some lots held live poliovirus because inactivation was incomplete.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Cutter_Laboratories ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_Laboratories

F23. The harm, by source: Paul Offit's account (his 2005 book The Cutter Incident and his NEJM article of 7 April 2005, as summarised by Michael Fitzpatrick, J R Soc Med 99:3, 2006): the vaccine "had caused 40 000 cases of polio, leaving 200 children with varying degrees of paralysis and killing 10." Nathanson and Langmuir (1963, via Wikipedia): among the vaccinated, about 40,000 abortive cases, 56 paralysed and 5 dead; among family and community contacts, 113 paralysed and 5 dead. Grokipedia: "approximately 200 children developed paralytic poliomyelitis", "over 160" permanently, 10 deaths (it also gives 204 cases, "over 250" with contacts, and "11 deaths" from official surveillance). FDA: "more than 260 people contracted polio". An early CDC-held count: 71 paralytic cases and 11 deaths. CONFLICT: the totals differ by what is counted (vaccinees, contacts, permanent paralysis, date of the count). The stem uses Offit's figures, attributed to him.
    https://brewminate.com/the-cutter-incident-how-our-first-polio-vaccine-led-to-a-growing-vaccine-crisis/ (Fitzpatrick, JRSM 2006) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_Laboratories ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cutter_Laboratories ; https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/histories-product-regulation/science-and-regulation-biological-products

F24. The lots had passed the tests: "some lots of the Cutter vaccine, despite passing required safety tests, contained live polio virus" (Wikipedia); the monkey-kidney tests "were not sensitive enough to catch low viral loads" (Grokipedia). Surgeon General Scheele's inspectors, William Tripp and Karl Habel, "found nothing wrong with Cutter's production methods" (Wikipedia). In 1954 Bernice Eddy of the NIH had reported paralysed test monkeys with photographs, and the NIH director William Sebrell "rejected the report" (Wikipedia). A June 1955 congressional hearing concluded "the problem was primarily the lack of scrutiny from the NIH Laboratory of Biologics Control" (Wikipedia). MISCONCEPTIONS: that Cutter faked its tests; that the failure proved a killed-virus vaccine cannot work.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_Laboratories ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cutter_Laboratories

F25. What changed: "In 1955, as a result of the Cutter Incident, the Laboratory of Biologics Control was raised to division status within NIH, to strengthen and expand its biologics control function. It became the Division of Biologics Standards" (FDA CBER history, read in a search excerpt; the PDF itself would not open). Grokipedia lists the reforms: lot-by-lot federal testing before release, longer formaldehyde inactivation, several confirmatory tests for live virus, and unannounced inspections. Fitzpatrick (on Offit): the incident "led to the effective federal regulation of vaccines." The Division moved to the FDA in 1972 as the Bureau of Biologics.
    https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916235132/http:/www.fda.gov/cber/inside/cberbkp3.pdf ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Cutter_Laboratories ; https://brewminate.com/the-cutter-incident-how-our-first-polio-vaccine-led-to-a-growing-vaccine-crisis/

F26. In Gottsdanker v. Cutter Laboratories (1958) the jury "found Cutter not negligent, but liable for breach of implied warranty"; Offit calls the result liability without fault, which he argues later drove makers out of vaccines.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_Laboratories ; https://brewminate.com/the-cutter-incident-how-our-first-polio-vaccine-led-to-a-growing-vaccine-crisis/

## The National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act (1986)

F27. Before the Act: lawsuits over the diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis (DTP) shot "rose from nine cases between 1978 and 1981 to over 200 by the mid-1980s", and the average claim rose from 10 million dollars in 1978 to 46.5 million by 1984 (Grokipedia). "Only one company was still manufacturing pertussis vaccine in the US by the end of 1985" (Wikipedia; Grokipedia "just one by the end of 1985"). Congress acted on "the concern that tort lawsuits against vaccine manufacturers could result in vaccine shortages" (Congressional Research Service).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/National_Childhood_Vaccine_Injury_Act ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Childhood_Vaccine_Injury_Act ; https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-04_IF12213_2ffe0e7cf0133aedd6f704758ebadb29fcdc72bb.html

F28. President Ronald Reagan signed the Act on 14 November 1986. It set up the National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program (VICP), "a federal no-fault system for compensating vaccine-related injuries or death" (Wikipedia); claims are decided by special masters of the US Court of Federal Claims, with no jury; a claim against a maker is generally barred "until after a petition has been filed and judgment entered through the VICP" (CRS). Makers remain liable only for manufacturing defects, inadequate warnings or wilful misconduct (Grokipedia); in Bruesewitz v. Wyeth (22 February 2011, 6-2) the Supreme Court read the Act to bar design-defect suits. The trade: makers spared most lawsuits; families paid without proving anyone at fault.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Childhood_Vaccine_Injury_Act ; https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-04_IF12213_2ffe0e7cf0133aedd6f704758ebadb29fcdc72bb.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/National_Childhood_Vaccine_Injury_Act

F29. The money: awards come from the Vaccine Injury Compensation Trust Fund, filled by an excise tax of 75 cents on each dose for each disease it prevents (HRSA's example: a flu shot 75 cents, measles-mumps-rubella 2.25 dollars); the flat 75-cent rate dates from a 1997 law (earlier the tax varied by vaccine). The Treasury collects it. A death award is 250,000 dollars (CRS). About 5.4 billion dollars had been paid on 12,339 compensated petitions by 1 September 2025 (Grokipedia). (The tax was set by later funding laws, not the 1986 text itself, so the questions say the programme is "paid for by" the tax, not that the 1986 law levied it.)
    https://www.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/hrsa/advisory-committees/vaccines/dec-29-2025-overview-of-vicp-dicp-combined.pdf (via search excerpt) ; https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-04_IF12213_2ffe0e7cf0133aedd6f704758ebadb29fcdc72bb.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/National_Childhood_Vaccine_Injury_Act

## VAERS and under-reporting

F30. The Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) began on 1 November 1990, "co-managed by the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)" (Grokipedia); it "is an outgrowth of the 1986 National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act" (Wikipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vaccine_Adverse_Event_Reporting_System ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine_Adverse_Event_Reporting_System

F31. "Anyone, patients, family members, healthcare providers and vaccine manufacturers, can submit a report to VAERS." It is "a passive surveillance system, meaning it relies on people sending in reports." "A VAERS report alone does not indicate whether a vaccine caused or contributed to an adverse event"; "Some reports can lack details or contain errors" (CDC). Grokipedia: "temporal association does not imply causality, as reports often include coincidental conditions unrelated to vaccination."
    https://www.cdc.gov/vaccine-safety-systems/vaers/index.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Vaccine_Adverse_Event_Reporting_System

F32. What VAERS is good for: from 1 September 1998 to 7 July 1999 it received fifteen reports of intussusception (a bowel blockage) in babies after RotaShield, the first rotavirus vaccine; the CDC urged a suspension on 16 July 1999, the maker withdrew it on 15 October 1999, and the CDC's own case-control study confirmed the raised risk.
    https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/vaccines/vpd-vac/rotavirus/vac-rotashield-historical.htm ; https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081020162156/http://www.fda.gov/cber/recalls/rota101599.htm

F33. The Harvard Pilgrim report: Ross Lazarus of Harvard Pilgrim Health Care led the AHRQ-funded project ESP:VAERS (grant R18 HS017045, 7 December 2007 to 29 September 2010) to detect possible vaccine side effects automatically in clinic records and send clinician-approved reports to VAERS; the final report is cited as Lazarus R., Rockville, MD: AHRQ, 2010 (the PDF's file name says 2011; Michael Klompas was on the team). Its BACKGROUND states: "Adverse events from vaccines are common but underreported," with "less than one percent reported to the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)" (the widely quoted form: "fewer than 1% of vaccine adverse events are reported"). Its own preliminary data (June 2006 to October 2009) covered about 715,000 patients and 1.4 million doses of 45 vaccines given to 376,452 people, in which "35,570 possible reactions (2.6 percent of vaccinations) were identified." CONFLICT with the brief: the brief says the report "estimated" the under-1-percent figure; on the AHRQ summary it is stated as background, a reason for the work, not a count the project made. The question attributes it as what the report stated.
    https://digital.ahrq.gov/ahrq-funded-projects/electronic-support-public-health-vaccine-adverse-event-reporting-system ; https://digital.ahrq.gov/ahrq-funded-projects/electronic-support-public-health-vaccine-adverse-event-reporting-system/final-report ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Vaccine_Adverse_Event_Reporting_System

## The schedule and the Institute of Medicine (2013)

F34. Early 1980s: vaccines were routinely given in childhood against seven diseases, "diphtheria, measles, mumps, pertussis, poliomyelitis, rubella, and tetanus" (CDC MMWR, Childhood Immunization Initiative 5-year follow-up, about 1982). Hib vaccine was added in 1985 and hepatitis B at birth in 1991 (secondary). By 2013, the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices recommended vaccines that protect children "age 6 years and under" against 14 pathogens, and "children may receive up to 24 immunizations by age 2 years and up to 5 injections in a single visit" (IOM 2013). Dose counts for today differ by counting rule (injections or doses, to age 2, 6 or 18, flu shots in or out), so no dose count is keyed.
    https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/00001091.htm ; https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13563/chapter/2 ; https://vaxopedia.org/2016/10/26/historical-immunization-schedules/

F35. The Institute of Medicine's committee (chair Ada Sue Hinshaw), The Childhood Immunization Schedule and Safety: Stakeholder Concerns, Scientific Evidence, and Future Studies, released 16 January 2013. First half: "The committee found no significant evidence to imply that the recommended immunization schedule is not safe" (summary; HealthDay quoted the committee as finding no evidence that the schedule "is not safe"; a secondary summary renders it "no evidence that the schedule is unsafe"). Second half: "Thus, key elements of the entire schedule, the number, frequency, timing, order, and age at administration of vaccines, have not been systematically examined in research studies"; "few studies have comprehensively assessed the association between the entire immunization schedule or variations in the overall schedule and categories of health outcomes, and no study has directly examined health outcomes and stakeholder concerns in precisely the way that the committee was charged to address"; the summary also names stakeholder concerns about "the long-term effects of the cumulative number of vaccines."
    https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13563/chapter/2 ; https://www.healthday.com/health-news/public-health/childhood-vaccine-schedule-is-safe-report-says-672575.html ; https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2013/01/iom-report-details-strategy-for-monitoring-safety-of-childhood-immunization-schedule

F36. The committee on a trial of the schedule: "it would be unethical and infeasible to conduct an RCT" (randomised controlled trial), since children assigned fewer or no vaccines would be left at greater risk of the diseases; "new RCTs of the childhood immunization schedule are not justified at this time." It called the Vaccine Safety Datalink (VSD), linked records of health plans, "the best available system for studying the safety of the immunization schedule in the United States" and urged HHS to keep funding it (Recommendation 6-3). The press release: such a trial "cannot be safely and efficiently performed."
    https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13563/chapter/2 ; https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2013/01/iom-report-details-strategy-for-monitoring-safety-of-childhood-immunization-schedule

## The dissent and the answer (2025-2026)

F37. On 1 May 2025 the US Department of Health and Human Services, under Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., announced through its spokesman Andrew Nixon that "all new vaccines will undergo safety testing in placebo-controlled trials" before approval, "a radical departure from past practices". Kennedy said "every innovation in vaccine development must be grounded in gold standard science and transparency." Before a Senate subcommittee in May 2025 Kennedy said: "I'm not suggesting we go back and placebo test all childhood vaccines." (PBS NewsHour, Ali Rogin, 9 September 2025, read in full; NPR and the Boston Globe, 1 May 2025, read in search excerpts, as both pages timed out.) Nixon's further claim that no vaccine on the childhood schedule had been tested against an inert placebo was disputed: PBS reports most were tested against a placebo at some point, and Jesse Goodman, a former FDA vaccine regulator, called the blanket claim incorrect. That dispute is not keyed.
    https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/rfk-jr-wants-all-new-vaccines-tested-against-a-placebo-doctors-say-that-isnt-good-science ; https://www.npr.org/sections/shots-health-news/2025/05/01/nx-s1-5383172/rfk-jr-placebo-vaccine-testing-studies ; https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/05/01/nation/rfk-jr-issues-demands-vaccine-approvals-that-could-affect-fall-covid-boosters/

F38. Others asking for trials of vaccines already in use: Christine Stabell Benn and Martin Kulldorff, "Proposals for Post-Market Randomized Childhood Vaccine Trials with Ethical Designs" (Research Square preprint, 2025), propose nine placebo-controlled randomised trials of FDA-approved childhood vaccines; a January 2026 HHS assessment of the schedule by Kulldorff and Tracy Beth Høeg called for "more and better science, including gold standard placebo-controlled randomized trials." ## Misconceptions a 9th-grader brings (for honest wrong choices)

- A vaccine is a medicine that kills the germ, or a dose of ready-made antibodies (F4: that is passive immunity).
- A booster works because antibody from the first dose is still there, or because the antigen stayed in the arm (F5: memory B cells).
- Jenner discovered that cowpox protects against smallpox (F11: dairy folk said so; Jesty used it in 1774).
- Jenner did away with the cut in the skin or with taking matter from a person (F13: he made cuts; his matter came from a milkmaid's hand), or gave protection for life (F16).
- Smallpox was ended by vaccinating everyone (F20, F21: surveillance and containment), or every lab destroyed its stocks (F19).
- Cutter faked its tests; the Cutter failure proved the killed-virus vaccine could not work (F24, F25).
- A VAERS report shows the vaccine caused the problem; a VAERS report has been checked by a doctor (F31).
- The 1986 Act ended all lawsuits against makers, or gave families jury trials (F28).
- The IOM said the schedule had never been studied at all, or that its vaccines were untested in children (F35: the gap is the schedule as a whole).
- A control group always gets a dummy shot (F39: an active control, a vaccine against another disease).
