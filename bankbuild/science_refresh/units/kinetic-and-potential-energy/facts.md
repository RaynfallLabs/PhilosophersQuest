# kinetic-and-potential-energy: fact sheet (T2, 6th grade, G6.P8)

Scope: kinetic energy is the energy of motion (faster and heavier means more); potential energy
is stored energy: gravitational (height), elastic (a stretched rubber band, a drawn bow, a
squeezed spring) and chemical (food, fuel, batteries). Energy is never created or destroyed, only
changed from one form to another or passed on: a roller coaster trades height for speed and back;
a pendulum swings; friction turns some motion into heat. No arithmetic (computing energy is the
math bank's): meaning and consequence only.

## Kinetic energy

F3. Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it is moving.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_energy ("Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object or particle due to its motion")
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy ("the form of energy that it possesses due to its motion")

F4. Kinetic energy depends on an object's mass and its speed (not on its direction). At the same speed, more mass means more kinetic energy, in proportion: twice the mass, twice the kinetic energy.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_energy ("depends on the object's mass and speed but not on its direction of motion")
    https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps3-1-energy ("proportional to the mass of the moving object")

F5. Kinetic energy grows with the square of speed: an object that doubles its speed has four times as much kinetic energy.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy ("an object doubling its speed has four times as much kinetic energy")
    https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps3-1-energy ("grows with the square of its speed")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_energy ("one-half the product of its mass and the square of its speed")

F6. A Wiffle ball is a hollow, lightweight plastic ball with eight oblong holes in one half, about the size of a baseball; it weighs about two-thirds of an ounce (about 19 grams), an eighth as much as a baseball.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiffle_ball ("hollow, lightweight, of resilient plastic"; "about the same size as a regulation baseball")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Wiffle_ball ("a lightweight plastic ball perforated with eight oblong holes on one hemisphere"; circumference of 9 inches)
    https://baseballhall.org/discover/wiffle-ball ("The size of a baseball, it was – at two-thirds of an ounce – only an eighth as heavy.")
    Note: Grokipedia gives two different weights (1.6 oz and under 0.5 oz); both are well under a tennis ball's. The questions give no weight.

F7. A tennis ball has a mass of 56.0 to 59.4 grams and a diameter of 6.54 to 6.86 cm (ITF rules), so it has about three times the mass of a Wiffle ball (about 19 grams, F6) of a similar size (a Wiffle ball is about 2.8 inches, about 7 cm, across).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Tennis_ball ("The mass ranges from 56.0 grams to 59.4 grams"; "between 6.54 cm and 6.86 cm")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Wiffle_ball ("approximately ... 2.8 inches in diameter")

F8. A cyclist's legs pass the chemical energy from food into kinetic energy of the bike and rider; braking turns the kinetic energy into heat through friction.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy ("transfers chemical energy provided by food to the bicycle and cyclist's store of kinetic energy"; "the kinetic energy would be dissipated through friction as heat")

## Potential energy: stored energy

F9. Potential energy is stored energy, the energy of position (or of shape or make-up): gravitational, elastic and chemical are kinds of it.
    https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("Potential energy is stored energy and the energy of position.")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Potential_energy ("the energy possessed by an object or system due to its position within a force field"; elastic and chemical bond energies are both named as potential energy)

F10. An object that is not moving can still have energy: a pencil lying on a table has energy because of its height above the floor, though it is still.
    https://www.cpp.edu/respect/resources/documents_4th/gr4.et_common_student_ideas.pdf (CPP and BSCS, RESPeCT Grade 4 Energy Transfer Module: "Even if it isn't moving, the pencil has energy.")

F11. Gravitational potential energy is greater the higher an object is lifted (and the heavier it is). Lifting an object takes work against gravity, and that work is stored as gravitational potential energy.
    https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("The higher and heavier the object, the more gravitational energy is stored.")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Potential_energy ("Gravitational potential energy depends on the object's mass and is linear in height"; "the work done to lift the object against gravity")

F12. Elastic potential energy is stored in an object that is stretched, compressed, bent or twisted out of shape: a spring, a rubber band, a bow, a trampoline, a slingshot. When the object springs back, the energy can become kinetic energy.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Elastic_energy ("the potential energy stored in an elastic object, such as a spring or rubber band"; "deforming it through stretching, compressing, or twisting"; "a bent bow stores energy to propel an arrow"; trampolines; "convertible to kinetic energy when the deformation is released")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Potential_energy ("This energy powers devices such as catapults, shock absorbers, bows, and rubber bands")
    https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("Compressed springs and stretched rubber bands are examples of stored mechanical energy.")

F13. A drawn bow stores elastic potential energy in its bent limbs; when the archer lets go, the string converts that energy into the arrow's kinetic energy.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Bow_and_arrow (the bow "stores elastic potential energy when its string is drawn back"; the limbs store "elastic potential energy in the deformed structure"; the string is "converting the potential energy into kinetic energy that accelerates the arrow")

F14. Chemical energy is potential energy stored in the chemical bonds that join atoms in a substance. It is released (or taken in) during chemical reactions.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_energy ("Chemical energy is the potential energy stored in the chemical bonds between atoms and molecules in substances"; it "can be released or absorbed during chemical reactions")
    Caution: breaking a bond takes energy; the energy comes out because the new bonds formed are stronger (Grokipedia Chemical_energy: "Breaking such bonds requires energy input"). So no question says the energy "is in the bonds and comes out when they break." The questions say the energy is released when substances react and form new ones.

F15. Fuels, food and batteries all release their chemical energy by reactions: burning wood or gasoline (combustion) gives heat, and in a car engine motion; the body releases food's energy by breaking down glucose in cellular respiration; a battery's electrochemical cells change chemical energy into electrical energy. Batteries, biomass, petroleum, natural gas and coal are examples of chemical energy.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_energy ("the burning of wood or gasoline"; "the combustion of gasoline in a car engine transforms chemical energy into kinetic energy"; "the breakdown of glucose during cellular respiration provides the chemical energy needed for bodily functions"; "Batteries convert chemical energy into electrical energy through electrochemical cells")
    https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/forms-of-energy.php ("Batteries, biomass, petroleum, natural gas, and coal are examples of chemical energy.")

F16. In a chemical reaction one or more substances (the reactants) are changed into different substances (the products). Combustion reactions release a great deal of heat.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Chemical_reaction ("A chemical reaction is a process during which one or more substances, known as reactants, are transformed into" different substances; "Combustion reactions are highly exothermic, releasing substantial heat")

## Energy is never created or destroyed

F17. Conservation of energy: energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only change from one form to another (or pass from one object to another). Apparent losses, such as friction turning motion into heat, are changes of form, not losses.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Conservation_of_energy ("Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another"; friction converting mechanical energy to heat is "merely conversions rather than losses"; it "prohibits perpetual motion machines of the first kind")
    https://www.cpp.edu/respect/resources/documents_4th/gr4.et_common_student_ideas.pdf ("The law of energy conservation states that energy is neither created nor destroyed.")

F18. A roller coaster: on most coasters an electric motor drives a chain that pulls the train up the lift hill. At the top the train lets go of the chain and runs downhill on gravity alone, its gravitational potential energy changing into kinetic energy. Friction and air drag change some of the energy into heat, slowing the train over the ride, and brakes stop it at the end.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Roller_coaster ("an electric motor drives a continuous chain loop embedded in the lift hill track"; "Once the peak is reached, the train disengages and descends under gravity, converting potential energy into kinetic motion"; "Friction and aerodynamic drag counteract motion, dissipating energy as heat and slowing the train over the ride"; "final stop runs")

F19. A swinging pendulum trades energy back and forth: its gravitational potential energy is greatest at the high ends of its swing, where its kinetic energy is least, and its kinetic energy is greatest at the lowest point.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Pendulum ("converting gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy and vice versa during each cycle"; "Equilibrium occurs at the lowest point of the swing")
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_energy ("least kinetic energy and greatest potential energy at the extreme positions of its swing"; "greatest kinetic energy and least potential energy when in the vertical position")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Conservation_of_energy ("gravitational potential energy at the highest point converts to kinetic energy at the lowest point")

F20. The bowling-ball pendulum ("nose basher") is a classic physics demonstration of energy conservation: a heavy ball hung from the ceiling is held at the demonstrator's nose and let go with no push; it swings away and comes back without hitting him, because it cannot rise higher than its starting point. A push would give it extra kinetic energy, and then it could hit him. University demonstration guides tell him to release it with no initial velocity and to keep still (often with his head against a backstop), since leaning forward puts his face in the ball's path.
    https://wonders.physics.wisc.edu/bowling-ball-pendulum/ ("Conservation of Energy tells us that the bowling ball will not go higher than its starting height"; "if you push the ball instead of just letting go, you give the ball some extra kinetic energy")
    https://learning.physics.iastate.edu/bowling-ball-pendulum ("When it returns to you, it will not hit your face"; a push's extra energy "will cause the ball to hit you in the face")
    https://www2.oberlin.edu/physics/catalog/demonstrations/mech/bowlingball.html ("standing with your head against the backstop"; "releasing the bowling ball from just in front of your nose (with no initial velocity)")

F21. A real pendulum or swing slows down: air resistance and friction at the pivot damp it, and each swing is a little lower than the last. Friction changes mechanical energy into thermal energy (heat). The warming is too small to feel: a pendulum that slows to a stop does not feel warmer.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Pendulum ("real pendulums experience damping from air resistance and friction, which gradually reduces the amplitude over time"; pivot friction named)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Friction ("a dissipative force that converts mechanical energy into thermal energy"; brakes: friction "dissipates kinetic energy as heat")
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0379 (Daane, Vokos and Scherr 2014: these scenarios "involve a decrease in total kinetic and potential energy and a compensating yet imperceptible increase in thermal energy (e.g., as a pendulum slows to a stop, it does not feel warmer)", p. 3 of the PDF)

F22. Energy passed on: in pool (billiards), when the cue ball hits another ball it slows sharply and the ball it hit speeds up as the kinetic energy is passed on to it. In a head-on hit between equal masses the moving ball stops and the struck ball moves off with its speed (the ideal case); real hits lose a little energy to heat, sound and denting.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy ("If the cue ball collides with another ball, it slows down dramatically, and the ball it hit accelerates as the kinetic energy is passed on to it.")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_energy ("the incoming ball transfers nearly all its velocity to the target ball in a head-on impact")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Elastic_collision ("the incident object stops, acquiring the initial velocity of the target"; real interactions have "some energy loss due to factors like friction and material deformation"; perfect elasticity means "no conversion of kinetic energy into heat, sound, or other irreversible losses")
    Note: a cue ball stops dead only when it slides, without spin, into the other ball (a stop shot); a rolling cue ball keeps going after the hit. So the stem says the player strikes it hard and straight and does not say it rolls.

## Misconceptions (honest wrong choices)

M1. "An object at rest has no energy" (Cyberphysics, misconceptions for teachers in training); "If an object isn't part of some action, it has no energy" (CPP and BSCS, Common Student Ideas). F10 answers it.
    https://cyberphysics.co.uk/PGCE/Misconceptions/energy.htm
    https://www.cpp.edu/respect/resources/documents_4th/gr4.et_common_student_ideas.pdf

M2. "The only type of potential energy is gravitational." (Cyberphysics.) F12 and F14 answer it.
    https://cyberphysics.co.uk/PGCE/Misconceptions/energy.htm

M3. "Gravitational potential energy depends only on the height of an object and kinetic energy only on the speed." (Cyberphysics.) F4 and F11 answer it.
    https://cyberphysics.co.uk/PGCE/Misconceptions/energy.htm

M4. "Doubling the speed of a moving object doubles the kinetic energy." (Cyberphysics.) F5 answers it.
    https://cyberphysics.co.uk/PGCE/Misconceptions/energy.htm

M5. "Energy can be used up or destroyed" (CPP and BSCS). In research on dissipative cases (a pendulum swinging to a stop, a ball bouncing to a stop), students describe the energy as "used up or lost"; in one study only 4 of 34 German students aged 15-16 described a stopping pendulum's kinetic energy as turning into thermal energy.
    https://www.cpp.edu/respect/resources/documents_4th/gr4.et_common_student_ideas.pdf
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0379 (p. 4 of the PDF: "to describe the energy in dissipative thermal processes as being used up or lost"; "only four out of 34 students described kinetic energy as transforming into thermal energy")

M6. The energy of a thing that has slowed to a stop is "stored up" in it as potential energy: students said a golf ball that bounced to a stop had "stored up" its energy, and university students said a damped pendulum's kinetic energy turned into potential energy as it slowed.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0379 (p. 4 of the PDF: "students claimed that the stopped ball had “stored up” the energy"; "they described the kinetic energy of the pendulum as transforming into potential energy as the pendulum slowed to a stop")

M7. Mixing up whose energy changes in a transfer: the person who lifts or pedals spends energy, so the thing lifted or the rider's motion is thought to lose energy too. Author's judgment (no survey found); F8 and F11 say the lifter's chemical energy passes into the object.

M8. A roller coaster's motor keeps pushing it down the hills. Author's judgment (no survey found); F18 says the train leaves the chain at the top and runs on gravity.

M9. Gravity makes new energy as a thing falls (energy created). Author's judgment, the "created" half of M5 (arXiv 1410.0379 quotes students who "used the terms used up, created, made or lost"); F17 answers it.

M10. A swing's lost energy goes back into the swinger's body as chemical energy. Author's judgment (no survey found); F21 says it becomes heat.

M11. A bouncier ball carries more kinetic energy. Author's judgment (no survey found); F4 says kinetic energy depends on mass and speed only, and bounciness is about how well a ball springs back (elastic energy, F12).

## Notes and cautions

- energy-changes-form (T1) already asks what energy is, a battery's chemical energy, a bulb's light and heat, a fan's motor, a speaker, a solar panel, the Sun as the main source, dam water's mechanical energy and food's chemical energy. None is re-asked; the chemical question here asks how stored chemical energy is released (a reaction), not where it is.
- Temperature as the particles' average kinetic energy is temperature-and-moving-particles' (T3); conduction, convection and radiation are heat-on-the-move's (T3); friction as a force is forces-and-net-force's; inertia is T3. The friction question here asks only where a swing's energy goes (into heat).
- No question computes an energy. "Four times" (F5) is the descriptive relationship NGSS MS-PS3-1 names ("grows with the square of its speed"), asked as meaning, with the misconception "it doubles" (M4) as the lure.
- Do not say chemical energy is released when bonds break (F14 caution).
- Do not say a pendulum's swings die away with no cause, or that the energy is lost: it becomes heat (F21).
- The nose basher's ball comes back to just short of its start, because a little energy becomes heat (F20, F21); the question asks why it cannot come back higher, which is conservation, not the losses.
