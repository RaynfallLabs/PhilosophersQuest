# particles-in-motion: fact sheet (T2, 6th grade, G6.P1)

Scope: why the states behave as they do, in particles (solid packed and vibrating; liquid close
and sliding; gas far apart and fast), heating speeds particles up so solids melt and liquids
boil, and a gas can be squeezed because of the space between its particles.

## Facts

F1. In a solid the particles are packed tightly and held in fixed positions; they cannot move about freely but they do vibrate on their spots (they are not still).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/State_of_matter ("particles are tightly packed and vibrate in fixed positions")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Solid ("solids exhibit only vibrational motion, with atoms or molecules oscillating around equilibrium sites")
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_matter ("the particles cannot move freely but can only vibrate")

F2. Because a solid's particles are held in fixed positions, a solid has a definite shape and a definite volume.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_matter ("held in fixed positions, giving the material a definite shape and volume")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Solid ("This arrangement gives solids a definite shape and volume")

F3. In a liquid the particles stay close together but are not held in place: they slide past one another. So a liquid keeps a fixed volume but flows and takes the shape of its container.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/State_of_matter ("particles that are close together but can slide past one another"; "a fixed volume but the ability to conform to the shape of its container")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Liquid ("closely packed but possess sufficient kinetic energy to move relative to one another ... maintain a fixed volume while adopting the shape of its container")
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_matter ("the particles remain close together but can move past one another")

F4. A liquid can hardly be squeezed (it is nearly incompressible) because its particles are already in close contact.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/State_of_matter ("near-incompressibility because the particles are already in close contact")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Liquid ("unlike gases, they are nearly incompressible under normal conditions")

F5. In a gas the particles are widely spaced and move freely, at high speed, in all directions.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gas ("widely separated from one another and move freely at high speeds")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/State_of_matter ("particles are widely spaced and move freely in all directions")

F6. A gas particle moves in a straight line until it hits another particle or a wall of its container; the motion is random and equal in all directions.
    https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_(CK-12)/13%3A_States_of_Matter/13.01%3A_Kinetic_Molecular_Theory ("move in straight-line motion until they collide with another particle, or with one of the walls")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_theory_of_gases ("constant, random motion ... collisions with each other and the container walls"; "chaotic and isotropic (equal in all directions)")

F7. A gas has no fixed shape or volume: it spreads out by itself to fill whatever space it is given, including an emptied (evacuated) container.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gas ("no fixed shape or volume"; "they spontaneously expand to fill available space when unconstrained")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/State_of_matter ("filling the entire volume and shape of their container")

F8. Gas particles are far apart compared with their own size, so most of the volume of a gas is empty space between the particles.
    https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_(CK-12)/13%3A_States_of_Matter/13.01%3A_Kinetic_Molecular_Theory ("far apart from one another compared to their size"; "Most of the volume of a gas, therefore, is composed of the empty space between the particles")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_theory_of_gases ("the actual volume occupied by the molecules themselves is negligible compared to the volume of the container")

F9. Because of that space, a gas is easily compressed: pressure pushes its particles closer together.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Gas ("high compressibility due to the large average distance between particles")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/State_of_matter ("gases are highly compressible"; "reduce the volume by bringing particles closer together")
    https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_(CK-12)/13%3A_States_of_Matter/13.01%3A_Kinetic_Molecular_Theory ("Because so much volume of a gas is empty space, gases are easily compressed into smaller volumes")

F10. The common aluminium scuba tank (the AL80) has an internal volume of about 11 litres, yet holds about 80 cubic feet (about 2,300 litres) of air measured at surface pressure, when filled to 3,000 pounds per square inch (about 210 bar). Air in the filled tank is still a gas.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Diving_cylinder ("the AL80 has an actual internal volume of about 11 liters"; "about 80 cubic feet of gas at surface pressure")
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_cylinder ("a nominal 'free gas' capacity of 80 cubic feet (2,300 L) when pressurized to 3,000 pounds per square inch (210 bar)")

F11. Heating a substance gives its particles more energy and makes them move faster; cooling takes energy away and slows them down.
    https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_(CK-12)/13%3A_States_of_Matter/13.05%3A_Average_Kinetic_Energy_and_Temperature ("some of the energy increases the motion of the particles"; "As a sample of matter is continually cooled, the average kinetic energy of its particles decreases")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_theory_of_gases ("the average kinetic energy per particle is directly proportional to the absolute temperature")

F12. Melting in particles: as a solid is heated its particles vibrate more and more rapidly; at the melting point the vibrations overcome the attractions holding them in place, the orderly structure breaks down, and the particles begin to slide past one another as a liquid.
    https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_(CK-12)/13%3A_States_of_Matter/13.11%3A_Melting ("As a solid is heated, its particles vibrate more rapidly"; "the disruptive vibrations of the particles of the solid overcome the attractive forces")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Melting ("requires heat input to overcome the intermolecular forces holding the solid together"; "molecules slide past one another more freely")

F13. Boiling in particles: as a liquid is heated its particles gain energy until particles all through the liquid have enough energy to break away from one another and become a gas; bubbles of vapour form inside the liquid.
    https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Introductory_Chemistry_(CK-12)/13%3A_States_of_Matter/13.09%3A_Boiling ("As a liquid is heated, the average kinetic energy of its particles increases"; "the molecules all throughout the liquid have enough kinetic energy to vaporize")
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Boiling ("require the absorption of latent heat of vaporization to overcome intermolecular forces")

F14. When liquid water becomes steam (water vapour), it takes up about 1,700 times as much room. (Not used as a key; background only.)
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam ("When liquid water becomes steam, it increases in volume by 1,700 times at standard temperature and pressure")

F15. Condensation in particles: when water vapour is cooled, its particles lose energy, slow down and come together into the closer-packed liquid; dew and drops form on a cool surface this way.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Condensation ("allowing molecules to lose kinetic energy and come together to form a denser liquid state"; "enabling them to cluster and coalesce into a liquid state")

F16. In a change of state the particles themselves stay the same: the water particles in ice, liquid water and water vapour are the same particles. What changes is how they move and how strongly they hold together (their arrangement and the pulls between them).
    https://www.sciencelearn.org.nz/resources/608-melting-and-freezing ("Even though the water's physical form changes, its molecules stay the same")
    https://www.chem.uwec.edu/Chem150_Resources/content/elaborations/unitxxxx/unit4-a-states.html ("What changes when heat is added are the strength and numbers of interactions between the water molecules"; "the kinetic energy (motion) of the water molecules increases")

F17. Particles do not get bigger when heated (or smaller when cooled): when a heated thing expands, the space between its particles grows, not the particles.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Thermal_expansion ("the increased vibrational energy of atoms or molecules with rising temperature, leading to greater average separation")
    https://www.pa.uky.edu/sciworks/THE/heat/atomz.htm ("The atoms stay the same, but the object gets bigger")
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article (that particles expand when heated is listed as a student misconception)

F18. A drop of dye or ink let into still water spreads out by itself; a drop in hot water spreads much faster than a drop in cold water, because the hot water's particles move much faster.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Diffusion ("dropping ink into still water, where the dye particles spread uniformly over time")
    https://instructional-resources.physics.uiowa.edu/node/3106 ("the drop in the hot water diffuses much faster than the drop in the cold water")
    https://serc.carleton.edu/sp/mnstep/activities/25763.html ("When the water is warmer, the heat energy in it causes the water molecules to move much faster. This makes the food coloring spread out more rapidly.")

F19. Gas particles do not spread because they push one another away: an ideal gas fills its container from the particles' own motion, with collisions only (Grokipedia: interacting "primarily via elastic collisions"). The idea that repulsion between gas particles spreads a gas is a documented student mistake.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Kinetic_theory_of_gases ("interacting primarily via elastic collisions with each other and the container walls")
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article (students "explain this by suggesting that repulsive forces exist in between them")

## Misconceptions a 6th grader holds (honest wrong choices)

M1. Particles expand when heated and shrink or contract when cooled (Griffiths and Preston, 1992, as summarised by the RSC).
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article ("that particles expand when they are heated")
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article ("particles can expand, contract, break up and/or are static")

M2. The particles in a solid are not moving at all (static particles).
    https://science.nasa.gov/eclips/videos/movement-of-molecules ("confront the misconception that molecules in a solid are not moving")
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article ("... and/or are static")

M3. Particles take on the properties of the substance they make up: they melt, go soft, change shape.
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article ("Students ascribe macroscopic properties to particles ... expand and / or change shape")
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article ("the particles melted or died")

M4. Molecules break up when a substance boils and join up again when it condenses.
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-changes-of-state-beyond-appearances/4017768.article ("molecules breaking up on boiling and reforming on condensing")

M5. A gas spreads because its particles repel one another (see F19).
    https://edu.rsc.org/resources/students-ideas-about-the-particulate-nature-of-matter-beyond-appearances/4017763.article
