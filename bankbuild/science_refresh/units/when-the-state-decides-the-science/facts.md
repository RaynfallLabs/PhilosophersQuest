## Trofim Lysenko and vernalisation

F1. Vernalisation is the bringing-on of flowering by a long spell of cold (or an artificial equal of winter's cold). Winter wheat needs the cold before it will head; Lysenko treated winter wheat seed with moisture and cold so that it would bear a crop when sown in spring (1928). Chilling seed to shift its season was known before him (old-bank lead; Grokipedia Lysenko places his early work in 1925-1928).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vernalization ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernalization ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko

F2. Lysenko's further claim: the change would be inherited. Wikipedia: he believed "the offspring of a vernalized plant would themselves possess the capabilities of the generation that preceded it"; Grokipedia: repeated treatment could turn spring wheat into a winter form "whose progeny kept the changes", a neo-Lamarckian view that "acquired traits from environmental stresses could be passed to offspring." Lysenkoism also claimed that one wheat species could transform into another and that grafting gave "vegetative hybridisation" (Wikipedia, Lysenkoism).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism

F3. What plant biology has since found: the vernalised state "is not transmitted to subsequent generations", so the offspring "revert to requiring cold" (Grokipedia); "The removal of silent chromatin marks at FLC during embryogenesis prevents the inheritance of the vernalized state", so offspring must be chilled themselves (Wikipedia). Grokipedia calls Lysenko's claim "empirically unsupported"; Wikipedia says he "inaccurately asserted that the vernalized state could be inherited." (Wikipedia also notes that Xiuju Li and Yongsheng Liu have argued it is "not unreasonable to postulate epigenetic mechanisms"; that view is attributed, not used as a key.)
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Vernalization ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernalization

F4. Lysenko rejected Mendelian genetics and chromosomes; he "did not believe in genes and only spoke about them to say that they did not exist" (Wikipedia). Grokipedia: he called formal genetics "metaphysically speculative", dismissed statistical processing as "bourgeois formalism", and in 1939 called Mendelian ratios such as 3:1 non-biological artifacts. After Nikolai Maximov criticised his statistical errors, Lysenko "boldly claimed that mathematics had no place in biology" (Wikipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Trofim_Lysenko

F5. Why officials liked his promises (steel-man): he was born into a peasant family in 1898 (Wikipedia); he gained support "especially following the famine" and crop losses of forced collectivisation in the early 1930s, and promised year-round work in farming (Wikipedia); vernalisation was promoted as a quick fix for grain shortages, his approach promised "rapid crop yield increases", while Mendelian breeding was portrayed as slow (Grokipedia, Lysenkoism).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism

F6. Nikolai Vavilov at first backed him: "It was Vavilov who initially supported Lysenko and encouraged him in his work"; Vavilov hoped to use vernalisation to bring different plants into flower together and stopped supporting it when it did not give the expected results (Wikipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko

## Kolmogorov tests the data (1939-1940)

F7. N. I. Ermolaeva repeated parts of Mendel's classical experiments and published in 1939 that Mendel's principle, that self-pollinated hybrids split 3:1, was false; Lysenko supported her (Stark and Seneta 2011, abstract, read through search-engine extracts). Grokipedia (Lysenkoism) says the data came from "Lysenko's own students."
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3115669 (Stark and Seneta, Genetics and Molecular Biology 34(2):177-186, 2011) ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism

F8. The mathematician Andrey Kolmogorov re-examined her counts in 1940 with the statistical test he had proposed in 1933 (now the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test) and found, "contrary to Ermolaeva, that her results clearly confirmed Mendel's principle" (Stark and Seneta 2011). Grokipedia: his paper "On a New Confirmation of Mendel's Laws" found "no significant deviation from Mendelian expectations", "a new brilliant confirmation." Stark and Seneta found flaws in his method but a "substantially adjusted approach" still "confirms his conclusions"; they note too that some of her families were small. Lysenko held that Kolmogorov was wrong (Stark and Seneta); Grokipedia gives his reply as "I, as a biologist, don't care whether Mendel was a good or a bad mathematician" (one source only; not quoted in the questions).
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3115669 ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Andrey_Kolmogorov ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism

## Stalin's backing and the August 1948 session

F9. The session of the Lenin All-Union Academy of Agricultural Sciences (VASKhNIL) ran from 31 July to 7 August 1948; it was "organized by Lysenko and approved by Stalin" (Wikipedia). Grokipedia: Stalin edited Lysenko's report beforehand; about 700 attended; of 56 speakers only eight defended classical genetics.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism

F10. On the last day Lysenko told the hall: "the Central Committee of the Communist Party has examined my report and approved it" (Wikipedia, Lysenkoism; Grokipedia gives the same line). Lysenkoism was declared "the only correct theory."
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Trofim_Lysenko

F11. Afterwards genetics was called a "bourgeois pseudoscience" and barred from teaching; more than 3,000 mainstream biologists were dismissed or imprisoned (Wikipedia, Lysenkoism; Grokipedia: "dismissal of over 3,000 scientists", genetics departments closed); an order went out to destroy Drosophila (fruit fly) stocks (Wikipedia). Earlier, in the purges, several geneticists who would not renounce genetics were executed, among them Izrail Agol, Solomon Levit and Georgii Karpechenko (Wikipedia, Lysenko).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko

## The methods in the field, and the end

F12. "The mass introduction of vernalization into USSR agriculture ended in failure" (Wikipedia); independent tests found "no consistent yield gains and frequent crop losses" (Grokipedia, Lysenkoism). In 1947 Lysenko promised wheat yields of 15,000 kg per hectare, against about 2,000 for the best variety in exceptional conditions (Wikipedia, Lysenkoism). His data came from questionnaires that were easy to falsify (Wikipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism

F13. Lysenko "denied intraspecific competition", holding that only competition between species occurs (Wikipedia, Lysenkoism). He planted trees in "nests", "where members of the same species helped each other", at high densities, and claimed that "some plants would sacrifice themselves for the main plant" (Wikipedia, Great Plan for the Transformation of Nature, 1948). A Soviet report of the time (CIA reading-room translation, read through a search extract) describes acorns sown by "the Lysenko nest method" in shelter belts under nurse crops of wheat and oats.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plan_for_the_Transformation_of_Nature ; https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/print/1928440

F14. The ecologist Vladimir Sukachev, an opponent of Lysenko, recorded that "by September 1951, 100% of the trees planted using the 'nest method' in the Ural territories had died" (Wikipedia). Grokipedia: a September 1951 Ural report documented 100% failure; overall survival was about 10%. MISCONCEPTION: plants of one kind growing together always share and help; seedlings of one kind crowded together compete hardest, because they need the same water, light and nutrients.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plan_for_the_Transformation_of_Nature ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Trofim_Lysenko

F35. Intraspecific competition is "the interaction among individuals of the same species contending for limited resources", such as light, water and nutrients; it "typically exerts a stronger per capita effect due to greater niche similarity"; "self-thinning represents a classic example", as "denser plantings lead to increased mortality among smaller individuals due to resource limitation" (Grokipedia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Intraspecific_competition

F15. After Nikita Khrushchev was ousted on 14 October 1964, Lysenko's immunity from criticism ended; in 1965 he was removed as director of the Institute of Genetics (Wikipedia; Grokipedia). The ban on genetics was "finally lifted in the mid-1960s" (Wikipedia, Lysenkoism); universities had Mendelian genetics back in the curriculum by 1965 (Grokipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Lysenkoism

## Nikolai Vavilov and the seed collection

F16. Vavilov made about 115 expeditions to 64 countries on five continents (Grokipedia; Science History Institute) and built in Leningrad the largest crop-seed collection of its day: "the world's largest collection of plant seeds" (Wikipedia). Sizes conflict: over 148,000 specimens by 1933 (Wikipedia), about 250,000 samples by the siege (Wikipedia), "over 200,000", "250,000" and "380,000" (Grokipedia), about 380,000 (Science History Institute). No figure is used.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Nikolai_Vavilov ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Vavilov ; https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-tragedy-of-the-worlds-first-seed-bank

F17. His purpose: he was "obsessed from an early age with ending famine" (Wikipedia). He believed domesticated crops were vulnerable because they were too genetically uniform, and that crossing them with wild, more diverse kinds could make them hardier (Science History Institute). Grokipedia (Seed bank): his effort was "driven by the recognition that crop genetic diversity was essential for developing famine-resistant varieties"; seed banks keep landraces (old local varieties) and wild relatives whose traits, such as drought tolerance and pest resistance, breeders can use. He looked for each crop's centre of origin where its variety was greatest (Grokipedia; Wikipedia, Center of origin, which notes later scholars dispute that diversity always marks origin).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Vavilov ; https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-tragedy-of-the-worlds-first-seed-bank ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Seed_bank ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_origin

F18. Vavilov was arrested by the NKVD (the secret police) on 6 August 1940 while collecting seeds in western Ukraine (Grokipedia), "imprisoned for opposing Lysenko" and charged with spying for the British and ruining Soviet agriculture (Wikipedia). Lysenko and Isai Prezent had sent a denunciation of him to Molotov in 1939 (Grokipedia). Sentenced to death in July 1941, commuted to 20 years in 1942; he died in Saratov prison on 26 January 1943. Cause: Grokipedia "starvation and associated complications such as dystrophy"; the Science History Institute "died of starvation"; Wikipedia gives the hospital record (pneumonia, dystrophy, oedema) and says some authors assert the cause was starvation. His sentence was voided in 1955.
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Nikolai_Vavilov ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Vavilov ; https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-tragedy-of-the-worlds-first-seed-bank

F19. The siege of Leningrad (872 days): the institute's staff moved a cross-section of the seeds to the basement and took shifts guarding them; "the staff refused to eat its contents", and by the spring of 1944 "a number of them had died of starvation" (Wikipedia, Vavilov). Science History Institute: they did not eat it even while starving, seeing themselves as stewards of humanity's crop heritage for the postwar world; it tells of a scientist who died at his desk holding a packet of peanuts. VERIFIED in outline. The count CONFLICTS: nine (Science History Institute; Grokipedia, Seed bank: "at least nine"), "at least 19" (Wikipedia, Institute of Plant Industry, citing The Guardian 2024), 28 (AFP via phys.org 2017, the institute's own portrait gallery of employees who died of hunger). No number is used. The name Dmitri Ivanov (rice) is in the old bank but was not found in the sources read; not used.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Vavilov ; https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-tragedy-of-the-worlds-first-seed-bank ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Plant_Industry ; https://phys.org/news/2017-01-russia-vavilov-guardian-world-lost.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Seed_bank

## Eugenics taught as science

F20. Francis Galton first used the word eugenics in 1883 (Wikipedia, Eugenics in the United States).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_in_the_United_States

F21. Charles Davenport founded the Eugenics Record Office (ERO) at Cold Spring Harbor, New York, in 1910 with a grant from Mary Harriman; the Carnegie Institution of Washington took over its funding in 1918 (Wikipedia, Davenport; Grokipedia, ERO). Harry Laughlin was its superintendent from 1910 (Wikipedia, Laughlin; Grokipedia gives conflicting later titles). Carnegie withdrew support and the ERO closed in December 1939 (Wikipedia, ERO; Grokipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Davenport ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Eugenics_Record_Office ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_Record_Office ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_H._Laughlin

F22. The ERO's data were family histories: field workers, usually educated women, went door to door (Wikipedia, ERO); 258 were trained, 85 percent of them women, and their interviews built pedigree charts (Grokipedia, ERO). Davenport treated human traits as heritable "unit characters" following Mendelian patterns, among them feeblemindedness, criminality, pauperism and nomadism (Grokipedia, Davenport); he studied the genetics of alcoholism, pellagra "(later shown to be due to a vitamin deficiency)", criminality, feeblemindedness and a "tendency to seafaring" (Wikipedia, Davenport). The ERO portrayed "traits such as criminality or pauperism as single-gene recessives" (Grokipedia, ERO). Henry Goddard held that feeble-mindedness was hereditary, "most likely caused by a single recessive gene" (Wikipedia, Feeble-minded). His book Heredity in Relation to Eugenics (1911) argued that negative eugenic measures were warranted (Grokipedia, Davenport).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_Record_Office ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Eugenics_Record_Office ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Charles_Davenport ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Davenport ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeble-minded

F23. Critics of the day: Thomas Hunt Morgan argued that Davenport treated feeblemindedness as a single Mendelian recessive, ignored imprecise diagnoses and overlooked environmental influences; Herbert Spencer Jennings argued that most human variation involves many interacting genes and the environment (Grokipedia, Davenport). A. M. Carr-Saunders said eugenicists could not separate biological heredity from environment; Joseph Gillman (1924) found selection bias, "Facts were therefore selected in such a manner" (Wikipedia, ERO). David Heron (1914) faulted the analyses for failing to correct for age, sex and place (Grokipedia, ERO).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Charles_Davenport ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_Record_Office ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Eugenics_Record_Office

F24. What is known now: "Intelligence in the normal range is a polygenic trait", with "a large number of genes, each with only a small effect" (Wikipedia, Heritability of IQ). Intellectual disability has many causes, genetic (Down syndrome, fragile X) and environmental (heavy drinking in pregnancy, infections such as rubella and meningitis, severe malnutrition, lack of oxygen at birth), and polygenic burdens "interact with prenatal or postnatal exposures" (Grokipedia, Intellectual disability). "Feebleminded" was a broad label that "broadly connoted mental deficiency of various forms" (Wikipedia, Feeble-minded). MISCONCEPTION: a trait that runs in a family must be carried in its genes; a family shares its home, food, schooling and poverty too (F23; pellagra in F22).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritability_of_IQ ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Intellectual_disability ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeble-minded

F25. Indiana passed the world's first eugenic compulsory sterilisation law in 1907 (Wikipedia, Eugenics in the United States; Grokipedia, Buck v. Bell: 12 April 1907). California sterilised the most people: "20,000 of the 60,000 that occurred between 1909 and 1960" and "by the year 1964, a total of 20,108" (Wikipedia); "over 20,000" (Grokipedia, Buck v. Bell). The US total was over 64,000 from 1907 to 1963 (Wikipedia); 60,000 to 70,000 (Grokipedia).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_in_the_United_States ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Buck_v._Bell

## Carrie Buck and Buck v. Bell

F26. Virginia passed its Eugenical Sterilization Act in March 1924 (20 March, Grokipedia); 18 states, Virginia among them, passed laws based on Laughlin's Model Eugenical Sterilization Law of 1922 (Wikipedia, Laughlin). Carrie Buck was committed on 23 January 1924, aged 17, to the Virginia State Colony for Epileptics and Feeble-Minded, after a pregnancy she attributed to rape (Grokipedia). Her teacher had called her "very good" in "deportment and lessons", and she was an average student, taken out of school in sixth grade to do housework (Wikipedia, Buck v. Bell; Carrie Buck). The superintendent, Albert Priddy, held back every other sterilisation order to test the law's constitutionality on her case (Encyclopedia Virginia).
    https://grokipedia.com/page/Buck_v._Bell ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_v._Bell ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Buck ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_H._Laughlin ; https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/buck-v-bell-1927/

F27. Harry Laughlin, "who had never met Carrie Buck", gave a deposition supporting her sterilisation, describing the Bucks as "the shiftless, ignorant, and worthless class of anti-social whites of the South" (Wikipedia, Laughlin). His written testimony was presented at the trial in his absence (Wikipedia, Buck v. Bell). The historian Paul Lombardo: Laughlin "provided a written deposition based on language given to him by doctors at the Virginia Colony" and "parroted language provided to him by the doctors in the case" (DNA Learning Center); UVA's eugenics exhibit (read through a search extract): he had never met any Buck family members and repeated Priddy's description. Grokipedia says only that he drew on ERO data and a pedigree chart. Encyclopedia.com (read through a search extract): the deposition rested on a letter from Dr Priddy. The hearing in the Circuit Court of Amherst County was on 18 November 1924 (Encyclopedia Virginia and the National Archives record of Estabrook's testimony; Encyclopedia.com gives 19 November); Laughlin's deposition was read into the record, while Estabrook testified in person.
    https://www.encyclopedia.com/law/law-magazines/buck-v-bell-1927 ; https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/buck-v-bell-1927/ ; https://docsteach.org/documents/document/testimony-ah-estabrook-buck-v-bell ;
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_H._Laughlin ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_v._Bell ; https://dnalc.cshl.edu/view/15219-Harry-Laughlin-at-Buck-vs-Bell-Paul-Lombardo.html ; https://exhibits.hsl.virginia.edu/eugenics/3-buckvbell

F28. Vivian, Carrie's daughter, was judged as a baby: a social worker testified that she "had a look about it that is not quite normal" (Encyclopedia Virginia); Arthur Estabrook of the ERO testified that he gave her "the usual test for a baby of about six months old" and that she had failed: he held a coin before her eyes and "She didn't focus" (DNA Learning Center, Lombardo); she "was found to be feebleminded at the age of about seven months" (DNALC). Estabrook spent a single day interviewing and photographing Carrie, her mother and her child (Encyclopedia Virginia).
    https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/buck-v-bell-1927/ ; https://dnalc.cshl.edu/view/15837-Vivian-Dobbs.html

F29. Vivian attended Venable Public Elementary School in Charlottesville for four terms, September 1930 to May 1932, and "was placed on the honor roll in April 1931" (Wikipedia, Buck v. Bell and Carrie Buck, after Stephen Jay Gould); Lombardo: "Vivian was on the honor roll in the first grade at Venable School" (DNALC); Gould (1984) found her "a perfectly normal, quite average student" (Wikipedia). She died at eight in 1932 of enteric colitis after measles (Wikipedia). Her death date CONFLICTS: June 1932 (Wikipedia), 3 July 1932 (Encyclopedia Virginia, via a search extract), 23 September 1932 (Grokipedia); no date is used. Her marks were mixed (Wikipedia: in her second term in 1B she earned an A in deportment, C in mathematics, B in the rest), so no wrong choice claims she was held back or failed a subject.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_v._Bell ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Buck ; https://dnalc.cshl.edu/view/15837-Vivian-Dobbs.html ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Buck_v._Bell

F30. The Supreme Court upheld the sterilisation 8-1 on 2 May 1927; Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. wrote: "Three generations of imbeciles are enough"; Pierce Butler dissented without an opinion (Wikipedia; Grokipedia). Carrie Buck was sterilised on 19 October 1927 (Wikipedia). The decision "has never expressly overturned" (Wikipedia); "never expressly overruled" (Grokipedia). Setting and context only. Later visitors found Carrie of normal intelligence (Wikipedia, Carrie Buck). Steel-man of the day: eugenics had cross-party support in the early 1900s (Grokipedia, Carrie Buck) and was published and funded by leading institutions (F21).

## The Nuremberg Code (1947)

F31. The Doctors' Trial (United States v. Karl Brandt et al.) tried 20 physicians and 3 SS officials; the main charge was performing "medical experiments, without the subjects' consent, on prisoners of war and civilians of occupied countries." The trial ran from 9 December 1946 to its decision on 20 August 1947; seven were sentenced to death, five to life, four to 10-20 years, and seven were acquitted (Wikipedia, Doctors' trial; Grokipedia agrees on 16 convicted, 7 hanged).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctors%27_trial ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Nuremberg_Code

F32. The Code was set out in the judgment itself (Wikipedia: "articulated in the judgment"; Grokipedia: the judges drew the principles from the trial evidence as standards for permissible experiments). The prosecution's medical experts Leo Alexander and Andrew Ivy contributed (Alexander's memorandum of April 1947 had six points, later ten); each later claimed authorship, and the accepted view is shared authorship (Wikipedia; Grokipedia). Several of the accused argued that their experiments differed little from pre-war experiments and that no law distinguished legal from illegal experiments (Wikipedia). The Code was not legally binding (Grokipedia; Wikipedia: no nation has adopted it as law).
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Code ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Nuremberg_Code

F33. Point 1 opens: "The voluntary consent of the human subject is absolutely essential." It goes on that the person must have legal capacity, free power of choice without "force, fraud, deceit, duress", and enough knowledge to make "an understanding and enlightened decision"; "The duty and responsibility for ascertaining the quality of the consent rests upon each individual who initiates, directs or engages in the experiment", a duty that "may not be delegated to another with impunity" (Yale Avalon Project text; Wikipedia).
    https://avalon.law.yale.edu/imt/nurecode.asp (Trials of War Criminals before the Nuernberg Military Tribunals, Vol. 2, 1949) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Code ; https://grokipedia.com/page/Nuremberg_Code

F34. The other nine points (Avalon; UNC; Wikipedia): (2) results for the good of society unprocurable by other means; (3) based on animal experiments and knowledge of the disease; (4) avoid all unnecessary physical and mental suffering; (5) "No experiment should be conducted where there is an a priori reason to believe that death or disabling injury will occur; except, perhaps, in those experiments where the experimental physicians also serve as subjects"; (6) the risk never above the humanitarian importance of the problem; (7) preparations against even remote injury; (8) only scientifically qualified persons; (9) the subject "should be at liberty to bring the experiment to an end"; (10) the scientist in charge must be prepared to end it if it is likely to cause injury, disability or death. The Code makes no exception for condemned prisoners, for volunteers who are paid, or for diseases that kill most of those who catch them.
    https://avalon.law.yale.edu/imt/nurecode.asp ; https://research.unc.edu/human-research-ethics/resources/ccm3_019064/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Code

## Misconceptions a 9th-grader brings (for honest wrong choices)

- A plant changed in its own life passes the change to its seed (F2, F3).
- Data that do not hit a ratio exactly disprove it; counts scatter by chance (F7, F8).
- A trait that runs in families must be genetic (F22-F24).
- Plants of one kind help each other when crowded (F13, F14).
- An expert's sworn word is evidence in itself (F27).
- Consent is a box to tick; the Code puts the duty on every researcher (F33).
