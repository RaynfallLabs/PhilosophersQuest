# Subject: Economics

The economics bank is **substantively Austrian** in its intellectual frame and treats **Bitcoin (not crypto) as a great human achievement** of monetary engineering. The bank carries forward the anti-authoritarian thread from the other banks: Mises and Hayek showed that central planning fails for the same fundamental reason the Holodomor and Great Leap Forward happened — central planners cannot replicate the dispersed price-signal information that voluntary exchange generates. Bitcoin solves the same political-economic problem in a different way: it removes the central-issuance trust requirement entirely.

In-game, the player answers economics questions when lockpicking (threshold mode) — the rogue's analytic skill.

## 3. Stance — what makes THIS bank different

The bank takes **substantive positions** on contested economic questions. Same standard the science bank applied to climate/COVID, the history bank applied to communism, the geography bank applied to colonialism: present what's true and what's false, don't perform false-equivalence balance on questions where the evidence is clear.

### What the bank does NOT do

- **No "balanced" presentation of Keynesianism vs Austrian as equally valid schools.** They are not. The bank presents Keynesianism + MMT honestly (the actual arguments, the actual figures, the actual evolution) AND honestly says where the historical record falsifies them.
- **No treating the Federal Reserve as a neutral technocratic body.** Cover its history honestly: Jekyll Island, the dual mandate's contradictions, the revolving door, the political-economic incentives, the actual track record.
- **No "crypto = Bitcoin" sloppy framing.** Bitcoin is a specific engineering + monetary achievement. Most "crypto" is pre-mined, centrally controlled, governance-fragile, or outright fraud. The bank distinguishes.

## 3.5 Practical financial literacy + entrepreneurship — IN SCOPE

The Pillar 5 "Practical Economics" pillar covers the **terms kids will actually encounter** as workers, taxpayers, savers, investors, and entrepreneurs — but **always through the Bastiat Pattern lens** (every question reveals an unseen cost, an incentive, a knowledge problem, or a cycle). No dry definitional content.

| Topic | Covered with Bastiat lens |
|---|---|
| Personal banking + paycheck | FICA's "employer half" as unseen tax (tax wedge concept); the gross-to-net gap; why your first $400 paycheck shows $337 deposited |
| Taxes | Tax brackets as marginal-not-average (the seen-vs-unseen pay raise); Roth vs Traditional IRA as time-preference recognition; capital gains long-vs-short as incentive to hold; W-2 vs 1099 as employee-vs-contractor structural difference |
| Investing | John Bogle 1975 Vanguard launch + the active-manager mockery + 40-year vindication = Hayek knowledge problem applied to investing; Buffett's "snowball" + compound interest + age-10 paper-route start; index fund vs active management; SPIVA data on after-fees performance; the rule of 72 |
| Retirement | 401(k) match as free money + the unseen vesting cliff; Roth vs Traditional as bet on future vs current marginal rate; the time-value-of-money case for starting at 18 instead of 28 |
| Business structure | LLC vs sole prop vs S-corp vs C-corp as INCENTIVE recognition — each is a different tradeoff between simplicity / liability / tax; why most US small businesses are LLCs; the S-corp salary trick as government creating arbitrage opportunities |
| Entrepreneurship metrics | CAC / LTV / churn / unit economics / burn rate / runway / gross margin — all framed as recognition skills (how do you know if a business is real or burning capital?) |
| Funding rounds | Series A/B/C as dilution arc + cap-table education; SAFE notes; preferred vs common; vesting + cliffs; convertible notes; why VCs need home runs (the power-law return distribution) |
| Accounting | Cash flow vs profit (the Bastiat distinction at the company level); income statement vs balance sheet; depreciation as government-permitted timing trick; accounts receivable as unseen cash-flow risk |
| Insurance | Premium / deductible / out-of-pocket maximum as risk-pool tradeoff; term-life vs whole-life as honest-product-vs-financial-product distinction; how moral hazard makes insurance markets fragile |

| Wealth-building celebrations | Buffett's "20-punch-card" investing method; Bogle's revolution; Munger's "Poor Charlie's Almanack"; Jim Simons's Renaissance + the rare exception that proves the rule |

**Voice rule for this content:** every question still leads with a NAMED FIGURE or NAMED MOMENT or DRAMATIC SCENARIO. No "What is the definition of X?" stems. Every answer is the recognition skill.

## 4. Voice rules

### Same wonder voice as geography + history + theology

PERSON / MOMENT / ARGUMENT → STORY → WONDER. Lead with a named figure, specific moment, or concrete example. Show the stakes. Pay off the question.

### Story-led, never definition-led

- Bad: *"What is comparative advantage?"*  (also banned by anti-rote regex)
- Good: *"In 1817, the English political economist David Ricardo published a thought experiment about Portugal and England producing wine and cloth. Even if Portugal could produce both more efficiently in absolute terms, Ricardo showed that both countries would gain by specializing. What principle did he establish?"* → **Comparative advantage**

### Bitcoin technical questions stay grounded

For Bitcoin, technical questions should ground concepts in real specifics — actual block heights, actual transaction examples, actual events. Don't write abstract crypto theory; write the SHA-256 hash function, the difficulty adjustment every 2016 blocks (~2 weeks), the 21M cap reached around 2140, the halvings every 210,000 blocks. Make it tangible.

### Austrian figures as people, not just names

- Mises lecturing at NYU on an unpaid visiting position from 1945 to 1969 (Rothbard attended)
- Hayek and Keynes debating publicly at the LSE in 1931
- Rothbard's Monday-night living room seminars in NYC
- Sowell's intellectual journey from Marxist to Chicago to free-market institutional economist

### Active voice, story rhythm

- "In 1920, Mises published the calculation problem paper..."
- "On Halloween 2008, Satoshi Nakamoto posted a nine-page paper..."
- "On August 15, 1971, Nixon went on television and closed the gold window..."

### Real names, real dates, real specifics — verified

Every name, date, event, and claim must be true. Bitcoin specifics especially — the user knows this material; bad facts will be caught.

## 6. Distractor design

- **Austrian doctrine**: distractors are real adjacent positions from rival schools (Keynesian, Chicago/monetarist, Marxist, mainstream neoclassical, mainstream MMT). E.g., for a question on the calculation problem, distractors include the labor theory of value, Marshallian partial equilibrium, Walrasian general equilibrium attempt, monetarist quantity theory.
- **Bitcoin technical**: distractors are real adjacent crypto concepts that are wrong for THIS question. For "what is proof-of-work," distractors include proof-of-stake (Ethereum's post-Merge), proof-of-authority (Layer 2 chains), proof-of-history (Solana).
- **Central banking history**: distractors are real adjacent events or real figures (e.g., Aldrich plan vs Glass-Owen, second-bank-of-US president distractors).
- **Hyperinflation cases**: distractors are other real hyperinflation cases (so the player learns there have been many).

## 7. Anti-patterns
