# Golden Exemplars — "This is what I want the game to feel like"

User-flagged questions that nail the target feel. When writing or auditing ANY
wonder-subject bank (history, philosophy, geography, theology, animal, cooking),
these are the north star. If a new question doesn't make you *feel* something
like these do, it isn't done.

Each is also tagged `"golden": true` in its source JSON so it can be found
programmatically (`grep '"golden": true' data/questions/*.json`).

> See also: `feedback_wonder_pattern.md` (the controlling voice rule) and
> `docs/quiz/moral_vision.md` (supreme). These exemplars are that pattern,
> realized.

---

## Why these work (the shared DNA)

1. **The stem tells a real, dramatic story** — a specific moment, named people,
   a vivid situation. You're *in the scene* before the question is even asked.
2. **The answer is the single most-memorable thing** — a vivid human ACTION or
   a NAMED THING, never a date/venue/generic label. It's the detail you'd
   repeat at dinner.
3. **It rewards imagination, not memorization** — you can reason toward the
   answer from the scene, and the right answer is the one that *lands*.
4. **The context (post-answer) deepens it** — the names, the aftermath, the
   poignant kicker — but the live moment lives entirely in stem + answer.

## 1. Beethoven & the contralto  (history, Tier 1)

> **Q.** On May 7 1824 Ludwig van Beethoven's 9th Symphony premiered in Vienna.
> Beethoven was on stage with the score but a second conductor beat time — he
> could hear nothing. When the music ended he stood still facing his music,
> missing the wild applause. The contralto did something so he would know the
> audience was cheering. What did she do?
>
> **A.** Turned him around to face the audience
>
> *(distractors: clapped his hands together for him / lifted his hand above her
> head / began clapping right beside him / held a candle close to his eye)*
>
> *Context:* The contralto soloist Caroline Unger gently turned Beethoven by the
> arm so he could see the crowd cheering. He had been deaf ~a decade. The Vienna
> audience cheered five times; protocol allowed only three even for the emperor.

**Why it's gold:** the deaf composer facing the wrong way while a hall roars
behind him is unforgettable, and the answer is a small, human, *visual* act of
kindness. Pure VIVID ACTION.

## 2. Socrates & the hemlock  (history, Tier 1)

> **Q.** Athens put the 70-year-old philosopher Socrates on trial in 399 BC,
> charging him with corrupting the youth and not believing in the city's gods.
> His friends bribed the guards to arrange an escape. Socrates refused, choosing
> to drink a cup of poison. What poison did Athens use for executions?
>
> **A.** Hemlock — from a flowering plant
>
> *(distractors: Belladonna / Wolfsbane / Aconite)*
>
> *Context:* Plato's Phaedo describes the slow paralysis as the hemlock worked
> upward from his feet. He drank the cup calmly and kept discussing the soul
> with his students until he could no longer speak.

**Why it's gold:** a man who could walk free choosing the cup instead — the
moral weight is in the stem, and "hemlock" is the NAMED THING every kid should
carry out of the scene.

## How to use this file

- **Writing new questions:** read these first. Ask "does mine have the scene,
  the named/vivid answer, the dinner-table memorability?"
- **Auditing:** a tier-1 question that's a dry date/label recall is the opposite
  of these — flag it.
- **Adding to this list:** when the user flags another "this is the feel,"
  append it here AND tag the source question `"golden": true`.
