# Monster family Field Notes: sources and lore notes

The 110 Field Notes in `data/monster_families.json` (one per family tier) were written and fact-checked on 2026-10-19 (Grokipedia first, Wikipedia second, primary texts where possible). This file records the source behind each note and the writer's suggestions for technique renames where the lore is thin.

**Checks run on the JSON:** 22 keys and 110 notes. Every note is 35 words or fewer and one or two sentences (quotation marks, "St." and "H. P." fooled the automatic sentence counter, so I checked those three by hand). There are no em-dashes or en-dashes. Every "they", "them" and "their" refers to a plural. Each T3 to T5 note was matched to the technique name and trigger line as shipped in `data/monster_families.json`. When Grokipedia had no page or didn't cover a point, I give the source I actually used.

**restless_dead**
- T1: Wikipedia "Bull of Heaven" (Ishtar threatens to raise the dead to devour the living; Tablet VI, standard Akkadian version).
- T2: Wikipedia "Eyrbyggja saga" (birds that landed on Thórólfr's grave fell dead; sheep in the valley died). Grokipedia "Draugr" has the haunting but not the birds. I dropped "cattle went mad" because only secondary retellings support it.
- T3: Agent-led search of John Brand's *Popular Antiquities*, as quoted on a MOLA Headland archaeology blog: in Scotland, salt and earth on a wooden platter on the breast; salt as the emblem of the immortal spirit; it deterred the Devil. A 1920 letter to James Frazer says the custom survived in Cumberland. I could not confirm the Pennant 1769 attribution, so I left it out.
- T4: Grettis saga ch. 35, sacred-texts translation via a mirror (head cut off and laid by his thigh; body burned "to cold coals"; ashes buried in a skin far from pastures and paths). Grokipedia "Draugr" confirms the beheading and burning.
- T5: Wikipedia "Eyrbyggja saga" (Snorri and Kjartan put the ghosts on trial); Grokipedia "Draugr" (the "door-doom"); Fróðá retellings (guidetoiceland.is) for the drowned men dripping by the fire. I avoided "one by one", which I could not confirm.

**ghosts**

- T5: Wikipedia "Jan Tregeagle" (harsh 17th-century steward; set impossible tasks until Judgment Day, including emptying Dozmary Pool with a limpet shell). The holed shell is not on the page, so I left it out.

**Techniques whose lore was weak, with suggested renames**
1. **Iron and Salt (ghosts T3):** no old tradition pairs iron and salt against ghosts; that pairing is mostly modern TV. The note joins two real halves. The better-grounded ghost ward is the Athenian Anthesteria (Wikipedia "Anthesteria"): people chewed hawthorn or buckthorn leaves, tarred their doors, and ended with "Out of doors, Keres!" Suggested rename: **Tar on the Door**.
2. **Circle of Salt (demons T3):** I found no historical salt circle against demons. Grimoires use a circle of holy names. Suggested rename: **The Conjurer's Circle**.
3. **Grave-Salt (restless_dead T3):** the name is fine, but the Pennant 1769 attribution could not be confirmed, so the note cites John Brand. The design doc's "grandmothers of Bohemia" line could not be verified and is not used.
4. **Fire and Dawn (giants T4):** dawn is real troll folklore. The fire weakness comes from Poul Anderson's *Three Hearts and Three Lions*, which D&D borrowed. Suggested rename: **Troll-Dawn** (or **Sunrise**).
5. **Averted Eyes (gorgons T3):** there is no folk technique for averting the gaze apart from Perseus, who is already T4. The note uses your weasel-and-rooster example framed by the gaze. Suggested rename: **Weasel and Rooster**.
6. **Trap-Wise (goblinkin T4):** there is no folk tradition of goblin trap-making (that's from D&D). The note uses *Goblin Market*, where Lizzie resists the goblins' trap. Suggested rename: **Goblin Fruit**.
7. **Steady Hand (constructs T3):** there is no patience lore for constructs. The note uses Rabbi Loew's Friday routine. Suggested rename: **The Sabbath Shem**.
8. **Angel's Due (celestials T4):** the shipped trigger ("the holy fire passes over") fits Passover best, so the note uses it. Suggested rename: **The Marked Door**.
9. **Wolfsbane (shapeshifters T3):** wolfsbane repelling werewolves is a 1941 *Wolf Man* idea. The real lore is wolf-poisoning, the Cerberus myth, and medieval cure attempts, and the note uses those. The name can stay.
10. **Silvered (shapeshifters T4):** the Gévaudan silver bullet is a novelists' addition from 1935. The note says so and points to the older German folklore.
11. **Oozes T3 to T5:** oozes have almost no pre-modern folklore. T3 uses witches' butter pins. T4 borrows Raktabija, a Hindu demon, because his multiplying blood is exactly the split mechanic. T5 uses a 1958 film. A rename for T4 would make the borrowing explicit: **Not a Drop Falls**.
12. **Hunter's Patience (vampires_liches T4):** the seed-counting belief fits a maths game neatly. Optional rename: **Scattered Seeds**.
13. **Erase the Letter (constructs T5):** emet to met is first attested in Polish tradition (Arnold 1674; Elijah of Chełm) and was attached to Prague later. Prague's classic shutdown is pulling the shem from the golem's mouth, which is now the constructs T3 note. The name is fine.
14. **The Missing Scale (dragons T5):** this is Tolkien's invention (1937). It is used as allowed, with the source named.

**Other flags**
- **Overlap with monster lore:** these notes repeat facts already in `monsters.json`, mostly because the technique names require them: vermin T4 (jorogumo), legend T4 (hydra, mythic_hydra), constructs T4 (talos, bronze_sentinel), gorgons T3 and T4 (basilisk, cockatrice, medusa_gorgon), and constructs T5 (clay_golem). The clay_golem entry credits emet to met to Prague alone.
- **Fair Folk T5 ending:** in the 1857 Grimm edition Rumpelstiltskin tears himself in two. If that's too gruesome, the 1812 ending (he runs off in a rage) is the softer option.
- **Celestials balance:** this family leans on Bible stories because most of its members are angels. Greek parallels sit in T3 (Semele) and T5 (Baucis and Philemon), all told as stories.
