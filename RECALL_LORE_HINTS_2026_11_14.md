# Recall Lore hints, redone (2026-11-14)

For Brandon. You said: "We need to entirely refresh Recall Lore, there's so much in here to understand now. Redo the hint list, follow all my rules about not directly giving something away." And: "Recall lore is a Trivia quiz that gives a random hint, doesn't do anything with monsters you can see and is not targeted."

**What changed in the game.** Recall Lore (N) is back to what it is: the trivia escalator chain that gives a random hint of the chain's tier from `data/hints.json`. It no longer reads the creature in view. The Wand of Probing now teaches the kind it reads, exactly as a corpse study does (the kind is known, and it counts once toward its family's tiers). The hint list below replaces the old one.

**The pool:** 380 hints (was 378). By tier: 1: 76 (was 76), 2: 87 (was 85), 3: 86 (was 86), 4: 83 (was 84), 5: 48 (was 47). 242 old hints kept word for word, 67 rewritten from an old one, 71 new. The longest is 186 characters (the old longest was 217); every hint fits the Recall Lore card whole at 1093x614, 1280x720, 1080x1920 and 3440x1440 (`tests/test_hint_pool.py`).

## How I read your rules

- **Point at the source, never the mechanic.** A hint names a myth, a story, nature or a craft (a knight's harness, a butcher's table, salamander's wool, Jacob and the angel) and lets the kid work out the rule. Your good trade-in line is in, word for word; your bad one is out, and so is "A golem has no mind to frighten" (now the golem of Prague and the letter on its brow).
- **No number, percentage, key, menu name, or the rule outright.** Every hint that named a key (Shift+C, Shift+S, O, D, the question mark), a screen ("Read the card") or a figure (half, twice, three rooms, a tenth of an ounce) is gone or rewritten. Myth numbers stay where they are the story (three old sisters, Odin's one eye). I also kept a few plain words that are the game's premise rather than a tuning ("One right answer lands a blow").
- **Never demystify a secret.** All the secret-build hints are gone (the door that knows a great name, and every hero's power: about thirty of them), with the hero powers' "small menu", the carrot, the cow, the unicorn coming along, the family's gold-trimmed sphere, the wolf come to heel and the hidden word's gifts. Where a light hint that points at a story already existed, I kept it as light or lighter: your own two duck lines (tiers 1 and 2); the cave game's nonsense word, with "It still works" cut; the Caesar-cipher line on the cave wall. The tier 3-5 duck lines tied to the mechanic (patience, a great many turns, wear it and endure it) and are gone. **Your call:** whether even the cave-game line and the cipher should go.
- **Fenrir's scraps:** exactly one hint, tier 4, the old "silent god's shoe" line (the scraps cobblers throw away; "It was never meant for walking in"). The two that said where the first scrap lies, to keep counting, and where the altar is are gone, and the ribbon line no longer mentions a shoe.
- **The Emberpup and Old Nickel:** story only. The abandoned pup in the rain, wary of anyone bigger; Old Nickel strutting in a cloak he tore from someone smaller (and why miners named an ore after his kind). No hint says to feed him, come back, or give him the cloak.
- **Voice:** short, concrete, a little wry; British spelling as the traders and cards use it ("armour"); "the Fair Folk" capitalised as the family's name is; no singular "they"; nothing sexual or gory.
- **Tiers:** 1 is the first floors (chains, the first quizzes, food, the first traders, the Emberpup), 5 the bottom (Abaddon, the Judgment, Death). Smiths moved to tier 2, since Brannoc opens at floor 10; Fenrir's hall moved to tier 4, since no hint below tier 4 may name Fenrir.

- **Ending Death**: I kept the riddles (the verse, the tablet, the place near the top) and took out "a place, a tool, a verse and a tablet ... in that order", which was a recipe.
